# Research brief: representation collapse in shared-trunk invariance + triplet setup (Track B)

## Summary
The observed failure — mode channel to a near-constant embedding, triplet loss pinned at 0.0 with zero gradients, one seed propagating into the shared trunk — matches the **complete collapse** class in self-supervised/metric literature, not a weak-embedding or mining artifact. The structural causes are identifiable: (a) a **symmetric invariance loss without stop-gradient/predictor asymmetry** has the constant function as an exact, stable optimum; (b) the variance defense is applied at the **wrong place** (per-band intermediates, plus a cross-channel covariance term that cannot prevent within-mode collapse); (c) routing loss magnitude (1–6) **dwarfs** task losses (~0.2) on a shared 140k-param trunk, so the destructive gradient wins. Each has a literature-backed fix, ranked below.

## Findings

### A. Collapse class: what the literature says

1. **Complete vs dimensional collapse.** Jing et al. (ICLR 2022) distinguish *complete collapse* (encoder constant, zero-rank) from *dimensional collapse* (embeddings span a low-dim subspace; singular spectrum → 0). Two causal mechanisms: augmentation variance exceeding data variance, and implicit rank-minimization of deep nets. Our crafted-perturbation invariance is exactly the "strong augmentation" regime, and the 128-dim GAT trunk is the depth regime — so both collapse drivers are present. Diagnostic to adopt: track the singular-value spectrum of mode embeddings; mass near zero = dimensional collapse in progress. [Jing et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09348)
2. **Stop-gradient + predictor is the causally-validated anti-collapse asymmetry.** SimSiam (Chen & He, CVPR 2021) ablates that collapsing solutions exist for the symmetric loss and that **only stop-gradient** prevents them; the predictor head supplies the needed asymmetry, formalized as EM-like alternating optimization (target fixed in E-step, online branch predicts it in M-step). BYOL uses the same principle via momentum target + predictor. Follow-ups (e.g., Zhang et al.; guided stop-gradient, NeurIPS 2023) confirm training stabilizes and works even without a predictor once gradient flow is asymmetric. Our routing loss `||Dr_b||²` is symmetric in the trunk (both clean and perturbed features flow grads into the same encoder) — it lacks this asymmetry, so the constant fixed point is attainable and stable. [Chen & He, CVPR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10566); [NeurIPS 2023 guided stop-gradient](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/71356)
3. **VICReg/Barlow Twins: variance defense must sit on the final embedding.** VICReg (Bardes et al., ICLR 2022) prevents collapse with three explicit terms — invariance, per-dimension variance hinge, covariance decorrelation — applied **to each branch's final embedding separately**, no negatives/momentum needed; ablations show the **variance term alone suffices** to prevent collapse. Barlow Twins (Zbontar et al., ICML 2021) decorrelates the cross-correlation matrix toward identity. Two mismatches in our setup: (i) our std-floor-1.0 hinge is on **per-band intermediates**, which the downstream MLP can still collapse (defense bypassed one layer later); (ii) our **cross-channel** covariance penalty decorrelates context-vs-mode, which says nothing about **within-mode** rank — it is not a substitute for VICReg-style within-embedding covariance. A miscalibrated floor (1.0 vs actual feature scale) further risks the hinge sitting permanently satisfied or permanently violated. [Bardes et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.04906); [Zbontar et al., ICML 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03230)
4. **Triplet zero-loss/zero-grad fixed point is a known mining interaction.** Standard triplet `max(d(a,p)−d(a,n)+m, 0)` (FaceNet, Schroff et al., CVPR 2015) relies on the margin plus **active mining**: a collapsed-to-constant embedding *should* give loss = margin with spreading gradients — unless the mining rule returns no violating triplets (all distances tie → semi-hard/batch-hard miners emit nothing → loss logged as exactly 0.0 with symmetric zero-net gradients). Practitioner reports and metric-learning analyses agree: without normalization + hard/semi-hard mining discipline, triplet training either collapses to zero loss (no triplets mined) or needs the margin to keep a spreading force. Our "margin exactly 0.0 + symmetric zero-net grads" signature points at the miner emitting zero valid triplets post-collapse — a **self-sustaining fixed point**: no triplets → no gradients → stays collapsed. Fix the miner, not just the loss. [Schroff et al., CVPR 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03832); [triplet vs contrastive mining analysis](https://metricgate.com/blogs/contrastive-loss-vs-triplet-loss)

### B. Multi-task evidence: invariance pressure on a shared trunk is destructive without balancing

5. **Gradient conflict is real; surgery methods help under genuine conflict.** PCGrad (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020) projects each task's gradient onto the normal plane of conflicting ones (negative cosine) and beats static weighting on MTL benchmarks; CAGrad (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2021) generalizes this by explicitly maximizing the worst-task improvement and improves over PCGrad/GradNorm combos on CityScapes/NYUv2-style suites. GradNorm (Chen et al., ICML 2018) adaptively reweights losses so **gradient norms at the shared trunk stay balanced** (single α hyperparameter), matching grid search at far lower cost. Our 5–30× magnitude imbalance (routing 1–6 vs tasks ~0.2) is precisely the regime GradNorm was built for. Caveat: later large-scale comparisons find plain scalar weighting competitive when conflict is mild — but ours is **severe and directional** (invariance actively erases the features the triplet/context losses need), i.e., genuine conflict, where surgery/balancing has the strongest evidence. [Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/3fe78a8acf5fda99de95303940a2420c-Paper.pdf); [Liu et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14048v2); [Chen et al., ICML 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/chen18a.html)

### C. What this implies for our architecture

6. **Readout-only training is correctly rejected, but the trunk needs gated shaping.** The goal ("mechanism objective must shape the shared representation") rules out freezing the trunk. The literature-compatible middle ground: let routing gradients reach the trunk **through an asymmetric, norm-capped path** (stop-grad target + GradNorm-style cap + warm-start), rather than the current symmetric, uncapped path. Decoupled-trunk-with-distillation remains a fallback, ranked below because it weakens the shaping claim.

## Ranked fixes (at most 3)

**Fix 1 — Asymmetric routing gradient: stop-gradient target + keep restricted head (strongest evidence).**
*Mechanism change:* for each band, compute `Dr_b = head_in(perturbed) − stopgrad(head_in(clean))` for the invariance term (perturbed branch predicts a **fixed** clean target; no grads into the trunk from the clean side of this term), and symmetrically `W_b Dr_b − stopgrad(c_b)` routing through the existing restricted linear head `W_b` (keep the head restricted/low-rank — it is the predictor analog). Leave triplet + context paths untouched. Optionally alternate: one optimizer step on heads, next on trunk (EM-style, per Tian et al.).
*Why it should work:* breaks the symmetry that makes the constant function a joint optimum — the trunk can no longer reduce invariance loss by collapsing both branches together, since the target is fixed per-step. This is the only intervention with direct causal ablations for exactly this failure (SimSiam: removing stop-grad → collapse; adding it back → no collapse).
*Key reference:* Chen & He, **SimSiam, CVPR 2021** (stop-gradient + predictor hypothesis + ablations).

**Fix 2 — Gradient-norm balancing with warm-start (addresses the 1–6 vs 0.2 dwarfing).**
*Mechanism change:* (a) initialize the routing weight so the routing gradient norm **at the shared trunk** ≤ context gradient norm (measure once at init, rescale); (b) adopt GradNorm (α ≈ 1.5) updating per-loss weights every ~100 steps toward equal trunk-gradient norms paced by inverse training rate; (c) two-timescale warm-start: train context + triplet heads to a non-collapsed point first (e.g., until pair accuracy > chance for 2 consecutive evals), then ramp routing weight from 0 → capped value. Add PCGrad projection on the trunk as a cheap add-on if cosine(context, routing) < 0 persists.
*Why it should work:* removes the scale mechanism by which invariance erases discriminative features before the variance/triplet terms can act, while still allowing routing to shape the trunk within a norm budget. Directly targets the reported magnitude gap and the one-seed trunk-propagation event.
*Key reference:* Chen et al., **GradNorm, ICML 2018** (adaptive balancing matches grid search); conflict-projection backup: Yu et al., **PCGrad, NeurIPS 2020**.

**Fix 3 — Move the variance floor to the final mode embedding + within-embedding covariance + mining repair (closes the bypass and the fixed point).**
*Mechanism change:* (a) apply the VICReg variance hinge (per-dim std floor calibrated to the **actual final-embedding scale**, e.g., normalize embeddings then floor ≈ 1.0 as in VICReg, not a blind 1.0 on unnormalized bands) on the **final 128-d mode embedding**, plus within-mode covariance decorrelation; demote the cross-channel penalty to a secondary term. (b) L2-normalize mode embeddings before triplet, switch to semi-hard (or batch-hard) mining with a logged **active-triplet fraction**, and assert it stays > 0 — if it hits 0, treat the run as collapsed and abort. (c) log embedding singular spectrum each eval (Jing diagnostic).
*Why it should work:* the current intermediate floor can be satisfied while the final embedding is constant (bypass); a final-embedding floor + decorrelation is the ablated-sufficient VICReg recipe. Normalization + mining discipline converts the constant point from a zero-gradient trap back into a loss = margin state with spreading gradients, i.e., removes the stable fixed point.
*Key reference:* Bardes et al., **VICReg, ICLR 2022** (variance-term-alone ablation); collapse framing: Jing et al., **ICLR 2022**.

*Considered but not ranked:* separate trunks + distillation of mechanism features into the shared trunk (works but concedes the shaping goal); raising triplet margin alone (does not fix symmetric zero-mining); larger batches alone (helps mining statistics but not the symmetric-invariance optimum).
