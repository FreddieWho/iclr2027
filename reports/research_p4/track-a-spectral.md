# Spectral band-filter approximation at n=20: research brief

Setup: 20 nodes, kNN k=4, symmetric normalized Laplacian L (spectrum [0,2]); B=6 equal-width cosine-tapered band-pass windows approximated by Chebyshev order R=5 via 64-node discrete orthogonality; partition-of-unity ≈ ±0.15 interior; applied to 128-dim GAT features with team-mean pooling; PyTorch; thousands of 20×20 snapshots.

## Summary

At n=20, exact eigendecomposition (O(n³) ≈ 8000 flops) is strictly cheaper in total work than any polynomial machinery and is the correct baseline: Chebyshev approximation exists only to avoid diagonalizing large Laplacians (Defferrard et al.), and its error analysis (Gibbs ringing at sharp band edges, damped by Jackson factors) is all downside at your scale. With only ~20 discrete eigenvalues, 6 fixed continuous bands oversample the spectrum — several bands are empty on many snapshots and realised responses are leakage-dominated — so bands should follow the empirical eigenvalue density, not equal-width windows. Ranked advice: (1) exact projectors batched on GPU, (2) if a polynomial path is needed, Chebyshev–Jackson at higher order, (3) spectrum-adapted / wavelet kernels only if exact proves insufficient.

## Findings

1. **Chebyshev exists to dodge eigendecomposition on large graphs; at n=20 exact is strictly better.** ChebNet (Defferrard et al., NeurIPS 2016) replaces U·diag(g(Λ))·Uᵀ filtering, which "required costly eigen-decompositions," with K-order polynomials of L so filtering costs O(K|E|) with K-hop localization and no diagonalization. [Defferrard et al. 2016](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/6081-convolutional-neural-networks-ongraphs-with-fast-localized-spectral-filtering.pdf) At n=20 the avoided cost is ~20³ symmetric eigensolve (~µs–ms, vectorized by `torch.linalg.eigh` over thousands of snapshots), while R=5 polynomial evaluation costs 5 sparse/dense 20×20 matmuls per band plus coefficient fitting — comparable or more expensive, with strictly nonzero approximation error. The revisit literature confirms Chebyshev is just one basis choice among monomial/Bernstein alternatives, and low-order truncations are a capacity bottleneck, not a fidelity feature. [He et al. 2022 (NeurIPS)](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/2f9b3ee2bcea04b327c09d7e3145bd1e-Paper-Conference.pdf) Practitioners diagonalize small graphs routinely; polynomial/wavelet fast paths are documented as large-graph fallbacks. [Hammond et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.3848)
2. **Order R=5 on sharp band-pass windows guarantees large edge error (Gibbs); Jackson damping is the textbook fix.** Ideal rectangular band responses are discontinuous, the worst case for polynomial approximation: truncated Chebyshev expansions ring near edges (Gibbs phenomenon). The standard remedy in graph signal processing is Chebyshev–Jackson damping — multiplying each degree-k term by gᴷₖ factors (closed form with αₖ=π/(K+2)) — which "reduces Gibbs oscillations resulting in a better convergence as the degree K increases." [Navarro thesis / Shuman et al. treatment](https://fnavarro.perso.math.cnrs.fr/files/phd_manuscript.pdf); [Chedemail et al. 2022](https://hal.science/hal-03769828/document) The SGWT literature's distributed-filtering paper likewise frames Chebyshev as a large-graph distributed substitute for exact multipliers. [Shuman et al. 2011](http://faculty.olin.edu/dshuman/Talks/Shuman_DCOSS_2011_Handout.pdf) Your ±0.15 partition-of-unity residual is consistent with undamped R=5 ringing; recent GNN work (ChebGibbsNet) adds exactly these Gibbs damping factors to ChebNet for this reason. [Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01789v1)
3. **With 20 eigenvalues, 6 equal-width continuous bands interact with a sparse discrete spectrum: empty bands + leakage.** Graph frequencies are the discrete Laplacian eigenvalues, so a "band energy" is a sum over the few eigenvalues falling in that window. [Sakiyama et al. 2016 (filter banks)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel7/6884276/7534952/07492192.pdf) A 20-node kNN Laplacian spectrum is highly non-uniform (bulk + isolated low/high eigenvalues), so several of 6 equal slices of [0,2] routinely contain 0–2 eigenvalues; per-snapshot band outputs then reflect polynomial sidelobes of neighbouring eigenvalues (leakage), not genuine band content. The established fix is spectrum-adapted (density-warped) filter banks: estimate the spectral cumulative density and place band boundaries so each band captures comparable eigenvalue mass (Shuman et al., "Localized Spectral Graph Filter Frames"). [Shuman et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.11220v2) Fewer bands (2–3: low/mid/high) or density-equalized bands are the right granularity at n=20; 6 uniform bands over-resolve the spectrum. Note cosine tapering helps but R=5 cannot realize steep skirts — transition width ~ O(1/R) of the interval, i.e. comparable to a whole band width at B=6.
4. **Alternative ranking for n=20 × thousands of snapshots: exact > Jackson-Chebyshev > wavelets/diffusion.** (a) *Exact spectral projectors*: one batched `eigh` per snapshot, then exact window masks g_b(Λ) applied as U(g_b(Λ)⊙(UᵀH)); zero approximation error, trivially handles arbitrary windows, cost negligible. (b) *Higher-order Chebyshev–Jackson*: only if eigendecomposition must be avoided (e.g. differentiating through filters, or scaling to large graphs later); needs R≫5 for 6 bands and still approximate. (c) *Spectral graph wavelets* (Hammond et al., ACHA 2011) use smooth dilated kernels plus a low-pass scaling function forming a frame — theoretically attractive for multiscale features — but their Chebyshev machinery again targets large graphs, and admissibility/frame-bound tuning is overhead with no payoff at n=20. [Hammond et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.3848) Diffusion wavelets (orthogonal, multiscale compression) answer a different question (compression/coarsening) and are overkill here. Bernstein-basis filters (BernNet, NeurIPS 2021) are worth knowing as the positive-control that arbitrary (incl. band-rejection/comb) responses are learnable with guarantees — but again via polynomials for large graphs. [He et al. 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/76f1cfd7754a6e4fc3281bcccb3d0902-Abstract.html)
5. **Approximation error propagates at most linearly into features, but band-leakage corrupts interpretation and learned weights.** Polynomial-filter stability theory bounds output perturbation by filter distance times input norm (‖g(L)x−g(Lₚ)x‖ ≤ C‖E‖ type bounds; interpretable degree-dependent bounds). [Kenlay et al., ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/kenlay21a/kenlay21a.pdf); [Dong et al., ICASSP 2020](https://web.media.mit.edu/~xdong/paper/icassp20.pdf) Transferability analysis further warns spectral filters are the unstable class under graph perturbation. [Levie et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1901.10524v1) Concretely: downstream MLPs can partially compensate fixed filter bias, so task metrics may look fine while "band b energy" means "leakage from band b±1" — dangerous if bands are interpreted tactically (e.g. "high-frequency = pressing intensity"). No published bound says ±0.15 response error is safe for representation quality; the honest test is exact-vs-approximate feature agreement (canonical correlation / linear probe gap), which is cheap to run since exact is available.

## Gaps
- No direct benchmark of "R=5, B=6, n=20" error vs downstream accuracy exists in literature; recommend a 1-hour internal ablation (exact vs R=5 vs R=5+Jackson vs R=16+Jackson: band-energy correlation + probe accuracy) rather than further literature search.
- Empirical eigenvalue histogram of your snapshots (how often each of the 6 bands is empty) is the single most informative unmeasured quantity.

## Recommendations (ranked, for OUR setting)

1. **Switch to exact batched eigendecomposition spectral projectors.** Method: single `torch.linalg.eigh` per 20×20 L (batched over snapshots on GPU); build 6 (or preferably 2–3 density-equalized) exact window masks on eigenvalues; apply as U(g(Λ)⊙UᵀH). Why: zero approximation error, less total compute than R=5×6 polynomial path at n=20, exact partition of unity, leakage only from window design (controllable), trivially reproducible. Key ref: Defferrard, Bresson & Vandergheynst, NeurIPS 2016 (defines the exact filter that Chebyshev approximates).
2. **If a polynomial path must be kept (differentiability/portability), use higher-order Chebyshev WITH Jackson damping and validate against exact.** Method: R≥16 with gᴷₖ Jackson factors, smooth (raised-cosine) windows, spectrum-adapted band edges; assert max band-response error vs exact <0.02 before use. Why: provably suppresses the Gibbs ringing that dominates your current ±0.15 residual. Key refs: Shuman, Vandergheynst & Frossard, DCOSS 2011 (Chebyshev–Jackson method); Hammond, Vandergheynst & Gribonval, ACHA 2011 (wavelets + fast Chebyshev).
3. **Reduce/redesign bands by measured eigenvalue density; keep wavelets as stretch goal only.** Method: histogram pooled eigenvalues across snapshots; use 3 density-equalized bands (or low/high + 1 mid) with smooth windows; optionally test one SGWT-style dilated-kernel bank against exact baseline. Why: at n=20, band count above spectral support buys leakage, not resolution. Key refs: Shuman et al. 2016 filter-frame warping; He et al., NeurIPS 2021 BernNet (arbitrary-response learnability guarantee).
