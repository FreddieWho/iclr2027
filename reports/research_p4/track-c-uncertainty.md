# Research brief: joint spectral–spatial localization for graph intervention probing

## Summary
Your tension is real and named in the literature: no nonzero graph signal is simultaneously perfectly band-limited and perfectly supported on a node subset, and node-masking acts as spectral convolution. For your regime (N=20, |S|=2–5, 1 of 6 bands) the classical fix is the **graph Slepian construction** (band-limited vectors maximally concentrated on S), which is exactly computable since a 20×20 eigendecomposition is trivial. Use exact spectral projectors (drop the Chebyshev approximation), sample Slepian-vector interventions, and always report the achieved concentration ratio as a covariate.

## Findings

**1. Graph uncertainty principles formalize your trade-off exactly.**
Agaskar & Lu (IEEE Trans. Signal Process., 2013) define spectral spread (xᵀLx) vs. graph spread around a center node (xᵀP²x) and derive the feasible uncertainty curve — the graph analogue of Heisenberg that you intuited [Agaskar & Lu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6356). Perraudin et al. (APSIPA Trans., 2018) extend this to global/local (per-atom) uncertainty bounds [Perraudin et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/apsipa-transactions-on-signal-and-information-processing/article/global-and-local-uncertainty-principles-for-signals-on-graphs/6D0F94F750522DE74F8E451E236E1273). Tsitsvero, Barbarossa & Di Lorenzo (IEEE Trans. Signal Process., 2016) go one step further and directly construct the signals "maximally concentrated on the graph domain and on its dual": for a vertex set S and a spectral band, the optimal vectors diagonalize the concentration operator B·S·B (band-project, mask, band-project), with eigenvalues μ ∈ [0,1] equal to the in-subset energy fraction [Tsitsvero et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.08822). This *is* the principled "band-limited content concentrated on S" object — the graph Slepian basis.

**2. Graph Slepians are the dominating construction; both naive orderings are strictly worse.**
Van De Ville et al. ("When Slepian Meets Fiedler," 2017) operationalize the above: Slepian vectors maximize energy in a predefined subgraph under a bandlimit, with eigenvalues reporting concentration quality, demonstrated on the C. elegans connectome [Van De Ville et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.08401). Theory comparison of your two primitives: **mask-then-filter** (your current δ) is exactly band-limited (up to Chebyshev error) but leaks spatially — and with Chebyshev order 5 on a dense 20-node kNN-4 graph (diameter ~3–4 hops), a degree-5 polynomial of L reaches essentially the whole graph, so off-support leakage is severe. **Filter-then-mask** has exact support but its spectrum is the band convolved with the mask's spectrum (leakage into all 6 bands). The Slepian vector dominates both: perfectly band-limited *and* the highest achievable on-S energy of any band-limited signal, with the shortfall quantified by μ. Practical kicker: at N=20 the Shannon number K ≈ |S|·(band fraction) ≈ (2–5)·(1/6) ≈ 0.3–0.8, i.e. **fewer than one well-concentrated degree of freedom exists** — mid/high-band, small-S interventions are fundamentally delocalized, and μ tells you exactly how much. No filtering trick evades this; report it, don't fight it.

**3. Vertex–frequency frames and polynomial filters explain your Chebyshev leakage quantitatively.**
Hammond, Vandergheynst & Gribonval (Appl. Comput. Harmon. Anal., 2011) define spectral graph wavelets T_g^t = g(L)δ_n and prove localization decays with graph distance at a rate controlled by the filter's polynomial degree — your order-5 Chebyshev filter is at most 5-hop localized, i.e. global on your graphs [Hammond et al.](https://inria.hal.science/inria-00541855/PDF/hammond-vandergheynst-gribonval-acha-2009r.pdf). Shuman, Ricaud & Vandergheynst (Appl. Comput. Harmon. Anal., 2016) build the windowed graph Fourier transform (joint vertex–frequency atoms) [Shuman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5708), and Shuman (2020) unifies these into localized spectral graph filter frames with design/localization trade-off survey [Shuman](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11220). Consequence for you: Chebyshev approximation was designed to avoid O(N³) diagonalization — pointless at N=20. Use exact projectors U_b U_bᵀ and eliminate approximation leakage entirely, leaving only the fundamental Slepian μ shortfall.

**4. Precedents for "spectral-band × spatial-region" conditional processing: one positive, none for probing.**
Positive (architecture, not probing): Geisler et al., *Spatio-Spectral Graph Neural Networks* (NeurIPS 2024) — parallel spatial (message-passing) and spectral (global filter) branches with per-node gating, proving reduced over-squashing and tighter approximation bounds; the closest published "band × region" conditional routing, but the routing is learned end-to-end, not an intervention probe [S²GNN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.19121). SpectralBrainGNN-style frequency-selective GNNs (2025) similarly learn band motifs, not interventions [example](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24901v1). Negative/adjacent: "spectral probing" in NLP (Müller-Eberstein et al., EMNLP 2022) means fitting frequency *filters over probe weights*, unrelated to graph bands [Spectral Probing](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.527.pdf); mechanistic-interpretability activation patching (Heimersheim & Nanda, 2024) gives best practice for intervention design (matched clean/corrupted baselines, on-distribution perturbations) but has no spectral-band conditioning [Activation patching guide](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15255). Bottom line: **no published method does band-conditioned, region-localized representation probing with purity guarantees** — the Slepian-probe design below would be novel.

## Recommendations (ranked)

**1. Slepian-sampled interventions (adopt).** Per snapshot, per (S, b): exactly diagonalize the 20×20 normalized Laplacian; form C = U_b U_bᵀ S U_b U_bᵀ (S = diag(1_S)); take top eigenvector ψ (or sample span{ψ₁,ψ₂} weighted by μᵢ with random signs); set δ = ε·ψ/‖ψ‖. Spectrally pure by construction; spatially optimal; μ₁ is your per-trial "achieved localization" covariate. *Why:* dominates both naive orderings with a measured guarantee. *Key ref:* Tsitsvero–Barbarossa–Di Lorenzo, IEEE TSP 2016; applied form Van De Ville et al. 2017.

**2. Exact projectors + two-number leakage reporting (adopt regardless).** Replace Chebyshev-5 P_b(L) with exact U_b U_bᵀ (O(20³) per snapshot, negligible in PyTorch batched `eigh`); for every δ report η_spec = ‖U_bU_bᵀδ‖²/‖δ‖² and η_spat = ‖1_S⊙δ‖²/‖δ‖². *Why:* kills approximation leakage so the only impurity is the fundamental one (μ), which becomes regressable. *Key ref:* Hammond et al., ACHA 2011 (localization-vs-degree).

**3. Slepian-matched nulls (adopt for routing claims).** Control each (S,b) trial with (a) same-S, adjacent-band Slepians and (b) same-band, complement-region Slepians — never node-permuted noise, which destroys graph structure and inflates contrast. *Why:* isolates "band-b content" from "region-S content" in the routing objective. *Key ref:* Heimersheim & Nanda 2024 (matched-baseline patching discipline) + Shuman et al., ACHA 2016 (vertex–frequency atoms justify the 2-D null grid).
