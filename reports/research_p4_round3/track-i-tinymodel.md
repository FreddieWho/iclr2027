# Research: Joint SSL + Supervised Training That Works at Tiny Scale (≤200k params)

## Summary
Nobody at tiny scale co-trains an *invariance-based* SSL loss (triplet/contrastive/VICReg-style) and a supervised loss on one shared trunk with both heads healthy — the winners either (a) use a **supervised** contrastive loss that pulls the same direction as CE, or (b) **separate the stages** (contrast-then-freeze-then-linear). The closest true-joint existence proof on graphs is JGCL (WWW 2022 companion). The molecular-multitask contrast resolves mechanistically: Tox21-style heads all pull toward correlated label features (no invariance pressure), while your triplet/CE pair pulls in opposite directions on the same trunk. Ranked prescriptions below.

## Findings

1. **S4L (Zhai et al., ICCV 2019) is the canonical small-scale joint-training recipe — and it uses prediction-style SSL, not invariance.** S4L jointly optimizes CE + rotation-prediction or exemplar loss on the same trunk. Protocol: keep the supervised baseline's settings fixed and tune *only* LR, weight decay, and the SSL weight; self-supervised weight w=1 worked best "though not by much." Variants add VAT consistency or entropy minimization the same way. Lesson for you: joint training is documented to work when the auxiliary head is a *classification* loss over pseudo-labels (rotation angle), i.e. gradient-aligned with CE, not an invariance loss. [S4L paper (ICCV 2019)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Zhai_S4L_Self-Supervised_Semi-Supervised_Learning_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)

2. **SupCon (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020) deliberately avoids joint training — and that avoidance is the recipe.** Supervised contrastive loss (same-class positives, temperature ~0.07–0.1, large batch, 128-d projector) is trained first; the projector is then *discarded* and a linear CE classifier is trained on frozen features. Result: ResNet-50/AutoAugment 78.7% top-1 (vs 77.6% CE), ResNet-200 81.4%. The field's best "contrastive + CE" result at CIFAR/ImageNet scale is explicitly two-stage, which corroborates your H1 (decoupled towers pass) and suggests your 15 joint failures are the expected outcome, not bad luck. [SupCon (NeurIPS 2020)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/d89a66c7c80a29b1bdbab0f2a1a94af8-Paper.pdf) ([code/reference](https://github.com/HobbitLong/SupContrast))

3. **JGCL (Akkas & Azad, WWW Companion 2022) is the direct graph-domain joint existence proof.** Joint self-supervised + supervised *graph* contrastive learning for node classification: one InfoNCE-style loss where positives come from both the same node across views (self) and same-class nodes (supervised), with 2N−2 negatives per node, cosine similarity, temperature τ. Small-GNN regime, joint from scratch — both channels healthy because the two terms share one pair-similarity geometry instead of fighting over invariance vs. discrimination. [JGCL (WWW 2022)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3487553.3524722) ([full text](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10543005))

4. **Li et al. (ECCV 2022) threshold behavior: collapse scales with capacity-vs-complexity ratio, and EMA is the small-model escape.** ResNet-18/34 show increasing partial collapse as the training subset grows; tricks (EMA teacher, stop-grad, predictor head, high predictor LR) that are "unnecessary on ImageNet with ResNet-50" become *load-bearing* on smaller models and more complex data. Your regime (150k params / 23k snapshots, batch 64, 80 epochs) sits squarely in their collapse zone for non-contrastive Siamese losses — and your 15 failures used stop-grad without (apparently) an EMA teacher. Documented escapes besides "train longer": EMA target network (BYOL/BGRL-style), wider projector than trunk, multi-crop/views, explicit feature-decorrelation losses, or simply keeping negatives (SimCLR-style InfoNCE does not collapse this way). [Li et al. (ECCV 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15007) ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.15007v2))

5. **VICReg/Barlow Twins are the batch-64-compatible SSL family; SimSiam/BYOL are not.** VICReg reports only ~1.2% accuracy drop at batch 128–256 vs. 2048 (Bardes et al., 2022); Barlow Twins works down to batch 256 (and was probed at 64). Both use explicit variance + covariance/decorrelation terms rather than pure invariance — i.e. they carry their own anti-collapse guard, which matters because your VICReg-final run applied decorrelation at the *end* of a trunk already collapsed by the joint dynamics. Note Barlow's projector is 3 layers × 8192-wide — far wider than its trunk output — consistent with the width escape in (4). [VICReg](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.04906v2) ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.04906)) [Barlow Twins (PMLR 139)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zbontar21a)

6. **Molecular multitask survives for three mechanistic reasons — all absent in your setup (highest-value answer).** (a) *No invariance pressure*: Tox21-style pipelines train all 12 endpoints simultaneously with multi-label CE and NaN-masking of missing labels — every head pulls the trunk toward label-discriminative features, so gradients are co-directional on average. Your triplet loss pulls augmented views *together* while CE/MSE pushes classes *apart*: a direct representational tug-of-war on one trunk. (b) *Correlated labels dilute conflict*: toxicity endpoints share underlying chemistry, so task gradients agree; structured-multitask work (Liu et al., PMLR 151) explicitly reports drug-discovery multitask often *underperforms* single-task when tasks are unrelated — negative transfer is the norm, correlation is the rescue. (c) *Head-count averaging*: with 12 heads, any one conflicting gradient is outvoted; with 2 heads (yours), one conflict is 50% of the signal — PCGrad (NeurIPS 2020) exists precisely to project away such pairwise conflicts. [Tox21 multitask masking](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26183v2) [Structured multitask (PMLR 151)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/liu22e/liu22e.pdf) [PCGrad (NeurIPS 2020)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/3fe78a8acf5fda99de95303940a2420c-Paper.pdf) [Multitask GNN auxiliary (J. Cheminformatics 2024)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13321-024-00880-7)

7. **Joint-vs-sequential meta-evidence favors joint only in low-label regimes.** A 2026 systematic study finds joint training (JT) improves data/training efficiency and is robust in low-label settings, while pretrain-then-finetune (PFT) is more reliable in specialized domains — i.e. joint is worth attempting, but the documented wins come from aligned (classification-style) auxiliaries, not invariance-vs-discrimination pairs. [Pretrain-vs-JT study (arXiv 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13192v1)

## Ranked prescriptions (max 3)

**P1 — Replace the invariance SSL term with a label-aligned supervised-contrastive term (JGCL-style single loss, or SupCon-style two-stage).** Concretely: one InfoNCE loss whose positives are same-class snapshots (+ same-snapshot views), temperature ~0.1, projector discarded before the CE/MSE head — or fully sequential contrast-then-freeze-then-linear. *Why it survives*: removes the invariance-vs-discrimination conflict that killed all 15 runs; every term now pulls same-class points together, exactly the molecular-multitask geometry. Key refs: Akkas & Azad, WWW Companion 2022; Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020.

**P2 — If an unsupervised branch must stay, make it BGRL-style: EMA teacher + projector wider than trunk + variance/covariance guard at the branch, batch ≥256 if possible.** Concretely: stop-grad alone is insufficient per Li et al. at your capacity ratio — add the EMA target, a 2–3-layer projector wider than the encoder output, and VICReg-style variance/covariance terms computed on the branch (not as a post-hoc final loss). *Why it survives*: each element is a documented Li-et-al. escape; VICReg/Barlow are the only Siamese family validated at batch ≤256. Key refs: Li et al., ECCV 2022; Bardes et al., 2022 (VICReg); Zbontar et al., PMLR 139.

**P3 — One-way sharing: stop-grad the SSL branch so it reads the trunk but cannot write to it (or PCGrad-project conflicting gradients).** Concretely: CE/MSE gradients update the trunk; the SSL head trains on detached trunk features (its loss updates only its own projector/predictor). *Why it survives*: reproduces the decoupled-towers H1 result inside one model — supervised channel guaranteed healthy while the SSL head becomes a diagnostic/regularizer rather than a trunk saboteur; PCGrad is the softer version of the same idea. Key refs: Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020 (PCGrad); Thakoor et al., 2022 (BGRL bootstrapping on graphs).

## Gaps
- No paper found that jointly trains triplet/contrastive + CE/MSE to full health on a ≤200k-param trunk — the brief's central negative result; P1–P3 are assembled from the nearest validated neighbors, not an exact precedent.
- JGCL full-text λ-weighting and epoch/batch details sit behind the NSF-hosted PDF; exact scalar recipe not verified beyond the loss form.
- Li et al. thresholds are reported in ResNet-18/ImageNet-subset units, not directly convertible to a 150k-param GAT / 23k-snapshot number; "collapse zone" placement is qualitative.
