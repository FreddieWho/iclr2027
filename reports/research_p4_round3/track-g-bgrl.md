# Research: BGRL-style bootstrapping rescue for shared-trunk spectral routing

## Summary
BGRL (Thakoor et al., 2021/2022) transplants cleanly onto the failing setup: replace the symmetric routing loss + triplet loss with a one-directional student→EMA-teacher cosine loss through a 2-layer predictor, keeping stop-grad on the teacher always. The ablations say stop-grad is the non-negotiable piece, predictor + slow EMA are jointly what blocks the constant-channel collapse, and bootstrapped methods are the family most robust to batch-64 regimes. For factorized per-band targets, the closest precedent is data2vec (ICML 2022: per-token teacher regression), and the main new risk is the predictor ignoring the rare high band — counter with per-band heads and μ-balanced sampling.

## Findings

1. **BGRL exact recipe (small-scale-relevant values).** Online encoder + target encoder (EMA), two augmented views, node-level cosine loss symmetrized; augmentations are node-feature masking (p_f ≈ 0.1–0.3) + edge masking (p_e ≈ 0.2–0.4) with *different* probabilities per view; predictor is an MLP with one hidden layer (≈512-dim at large scale); target decay annealed 0.99 → 1.0 on a cosine schedule; BN decay fixed at 0.99; AdamW. [PDF](https://misovalko.github.io/publications/thakoor2022bootstrapped.pdf) [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06514) [Code](https://github.com/nerdslab/bgrl/blob/main/train_transductive.py)
2. **Which piece prevents collapse.** SimSiam ablations (Chen & He, CVPR 2021) show stop-gradient is *the* necessary condition — architecture (predictor, BN, L2-norm) alone is insufficient; removing stop-grad or removing the predictor collapses. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.10566) [Summary](https://docsaid.org/en/papers/contrastive-learning/simsiam). Theory (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2021, "Understanding Self-Supervised Learning Dynamics without Contrastive Pairs") explains why: predictor + stop-grad decouple eigenmode dynamics so informative directions amplify instead of collapsing; practical corollary is the predictor needs relatively large effective LR. [Paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.06810). BYOL follow-ups show collapse is avoidable even BN-free with proper init/normalization (GroupNorm/LayerNorm suffice). [Overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2010.10241v1)
3. **BYOL exact numbers to copy.** Target decay τ_t = 1 − (1 − 0.996)·(cos(πt/T)+1)/2 (0.996 → 1.0); loss = 2 − 2·cos(q, z′) on L2-normalized vectors, symmetrized; projector and predictor are same-dim MLPs (orig. 4096-hidden/256-out; predictor mirrors projector). [Paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/f3ada80d5c4ee70142b17b8192b2958e-Paper.pdf) [Supplement](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/f3ada80d5c4ee70142b17b8192b2958e-Supplemental.pdf). Batch robustness is the reason this family fits batch-64: BYOL holds ~68% top-1 at batch 64 where SimCLR falls to ~52%; ablations show <1% drop 4096→256 while contrastive methods degrade several points. [Blog](https://leeyngdo.github.io/blog/representation-learning/2023-01-18-bootstrap-your-own-latent) [Explainer](https://www.abhik.ai/papers/byol)
4. **SimSiam predictor spec (closest to 150k-param regime).** Predictor h is a 2-layer bottleneck MLP (in/out 2048, hidden 512), BN on the hidden layer only, no BN/ReLU on output; projection MLP is 3-layer. [Docs](https://docs.lightly.ai/self-supervised-learning/examples/simsiam.html). Transfer: hidden ≈ in/4 bottleneck, BN-hidden-only, He init — no dependence on large batches since there are no negatives at all.
5. **Small-scale failure evidence (honest: no direct "BGRL dies at 150k params" report found).** The documented fragility is augmentation-sensitivity, not size per se: AFGRL (2022) motivates augmentation-free graph SSL from BGRL's dependence on handcrafted augmentation choice/strength. [Slides](https://namkyeong.github.io/assets/pdf/slides_AFGRL.pdf). Theory predicts the small-model risk: fewer eigenmodes in a tiny encoder means the predictor must stay expressive relative to the trunk or the dynamics re-collapse ("Understanding Collapse in Non-Contrastive Siamese" notes MoCo/BYOL resist small-model collapse *because of* the momentum + predictor pair). [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.15007v2). Implication: at 140k params, keep the predictor relatively wide and never shrink it to "save params."
6. **Precedent for factorized/localized teacher targets.** data2vec (Baevski et al., ICML 2022) is the direct precedent: student regresses *masked, localized* teacher features (per-patch/token, averaged top-K teacher layers, EMA teacher, stop-grad) rather than one global embedding — works across vision/speech/NLP. LaplaceGNN (Bini & Marchand-Maillet, 2025) is the graph-spectral analogue: spectral bootstrapping with Laplacian/centrality-guided views and a teacher–student pair, no negatives, linear complexity. [Abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20362) [PDF](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20362) [Review](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=J9IE10XsMC). Known failure mode of this pattern: the predictor underfits rare/hard targets (in our case the [1.3, 2.0] band, starved when Slepians have high μ / smooth trajectories dominate) while the teacher drifts toward easy smooth content — i.e. channel destruction reappearing one level up.
7. **Asymmetry warning specific to this setup.** BGRL/BYOL symmetrize by swapping views; here views are *not* interchangeable (perturbed→clean is recovery; clean→perturbed is ill-posed invention). Use one-directional loss only. The Slepian intervention plays the role of "augmentation," so μ (concentration ratio) is the augmentation-strength knob — AFGRL's lesson says sweep it, and consider a μ curriculum (high-μ/easy → low-μ/hard).

## Prescriptions (ranked, ≤3)

**P1 — Minimal BGRL transplant (run this first, alone).**
Mechanism: delete the symmetric gradient path that makes constant-channels a joint optimum; teacher targets are stop-grad-fixed within each step so the trunk cannot co-collapse both branches at once, and the slow EMA bounds teacher drift.
Settings: shared GAT trunk = online encoder; EMA twin τ = 0.99 cosine→1.0 over the 80 epochs (short run ⇒ faster than BYOL's 0.996; if <2500 total steps, use fixed τ = 0.99); stop-grad teacher on clean input always; student on perturbed input → shared trunk → per-band heads → one 2-layer predictor MLP per band-head output (hidden 256 ≈ bottleneck, BN+ReLU hidden, linear out, He init); loss = mean over the 3 bands of (2 − 2·cos) on L2-normalized band vectors, one-directional; Adam 1e-3 trunk/heads, predictor param group 1e-2 (≈10×, per Tian et al.); weight decay 1e-6 excluding biases/BN; DROP triplet + context CE/MSE entirely for this run.
Why it breaks the pattern: v4 already showed stop-grad helps on decoupled towers — P1 extends that to the shared trunk by making the clean side unfireable (EMA + stop-grad) so killing a channel no longer reduces loss on either side.
Key refs: Thakoor et al., arXiv 2102.06514 (2021; TMLR 2022); Grill et al., NeurIPS 2020 (BYOL); Tian et al., NeurIPS 2021 (predictor-LR dynamics).

**P2 — Factorized-target guard (add iff high-band channel erodes under P1).**
Mechanism: force gradient to each band independently so the easy low band cannot dominate the shared predictor.
Settings: 3 *separate* predictor MLPs (one per band, same 2-layer/BN-hidden spec, hidden 128–256); per-band L2-normalize before cosine; equal weight per band (already density-equalized — keep); μ-balanced batching (oversample low-μ Slepians / high-band-content graphs so the teacher sees rare bands each step); monitor per-band teacher std + student→teacher cosine separately.
Why: shared predictor + smooth-trajectory dominance is exactly the data2vec "rare-target neglect" failure; separate heads + balanced sampling is the data2vec/LaplaceGNN answer.
Key refs: Baevski et al., ICML 2022 (data2vec); Bini & Marchand-Maillet, arXiv 2506.20362 (2025, LaplaceGNN).

**P3 — Tripwires + staged reintroduction (protocol, not a model).**
Mechanism: detect teacher-side collapse early (it is slower than student collapse, so cheap to catch) and keep the destructive losses out until routing is stable.
Settings: log per-channel std (student, predictor-out, teacher) and per-band cosine gap every epoch; rules: if any teacher band-std decays >30% from epoch-5 value → raise τ (slow teacher further); if student-teacher gap saturates near 1.0 with flat std → predictor too weak, widen hidden; only after 80-epoch bootstrap run is stable, reintroduce triplet/context terms as *frozen-trunk probes* first, then joint with 0.1× weight. Never backprop the teacher (SimSiam Table 7 lesson).
Key refs: Chen & He, CVPR 2021 (SimSiam stop-grad ablations); "BYOL works even without batch statistics" (2021, normalization ablations).

## Gaps
- No published report of BGRL specifically failing at ~150k params / batch 64 / 20-node graphs was found; the small-scale risk is inferred from augmentation-sensitivity (AFGRL) and collapse theory, not a direct benchmark. Next step: empirical — P1 run with the tripwires in P3 *is* the test.
- Predictor width scaling laws at tiny-trunk scale are uncharted (all predictor ablations are at ResNet-50 / large-GNN scale). Suggested: if P1 collapses, widen predictor before touching anything else, and report it — that datapoint is itself novel.
- Exact μ ↔ augmentation-strength mapping (which μ range acts like BYOL's "color jitter on/off") is setup-specific and must be swept locally; no literature value transfers.
