# Research: ICLR reviewer taste for a "diagnose → repair → externally confirm" representation-learning study (multi-agent sports data)

## Summary

ICLR's stated policy and recent acceptance record are favorable to this paper shape: official reviewer guidance says lack of SOTA/scale is *not* grounds for rejection, and ICLR 2025/2026 accepted multiple small-model mechanistic/geometry-of-representations analyses — including spotlights — when the claim was scientific rather than benchmark-climbing. The realistic risks are not the 140k-param model or the sports domain (sports multi-agent trajectory papers were accepted as posters at both ICLR 2025 and 2026) but the classic fatal triad: correlational-not-causal mechanism claims, no demonstrated downstream gain from the repair, and perceived narrow generality. Your causal switch + prospective prediction (Spearman ~0.85) + single-read holdout + 8-match external-provider confirmation directly pre-empts the first and third of these. Recommendation: main track, primary area "unsupervised/self-supervised representation learning" (or interpretability/analysis of deep networks), with ICBINB or geometry-of-representation workshops as fallback.

## Findings

### (a) How ICLR 2025/2026 treated causal RL, non-LLM mechanistic interpretability, and geometry/spectral embedding analyses

1. **Small-model "science of representations" can earn a Spotlight.** *Formation of Representations in Neural Networks* (Ziyin, Chuang, Galanti, Poggio) — a theory-driven account of how representations/gradients co-evolve, validated on modest networks — was an **ICLR 2025 Spotlight**. Precedent that mechanistic/representation-analysis claims do not require large-scale baselines when framed as scientific understanding. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Njx1NjHIx4) · [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ab6a2c6ee757afe43882121281f6065c-Abstract-Conference.html)

2. **Conceptual/critical mechanistic interpretability accepted even with toy demos.** *Everything, Everywhere, All at Once: Is Mechanistic Interpretability Identifiable?* (Méloux et al.) — a position-style analysis of identifiability of MI explanations with XOR/MNIST-scale demos — accepted as **ICLR 2025 Poster**. Shows ICLR rewards rigorous *diagnostic* framing of interpretability, not just new methods. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=5IWJBStfU7) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20914)

3. **Geometry/spectral analyses of representations are an established main-track genre.** *The Geometry of Reasoning: Flowing Logics in Representation Space* (Zhou et al., Duke) — geometric framework modeling reasoning as flows in representation space — accepted as **ICLR 2026 Poster**; keywords include Representation Learning, Geometry, Interpretability. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ixr5Pcabq7) · [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007922). Similarly *I-Con: A Unifying Framework for Representation Learning* (Alshammari et al.) — exposing "a hidden information geometry underlying clustering, spectral methods, dimensionality reduction, contrastive learning" — accepted at ICLR 2025. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=WfaQrKCr4X)

4. **Purely descriptive spectral-geometry papers can struggle at ICLR.** *Tracing the Representation Geometry of Language Models through Pretraining and Post-training* (Li et al.) — spectral measures of embedding geometry, well-cited (~40) — has an ICLR 2025 submission forum but ultimately appeared in the **NeurIPS 2025 proceedings**, not ICLR. Suggests analysis-only papers without an actionable consequence (predictor/repair) are borderline at ICLR — your Phase-3 predictor + repair closes exactly this gap. [OpenReview (ICLR 2025 forum)](https://openreview.net/forum?id=FDruZlKWUb) · [NeurIPS 2025 proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/4eac651e6c06d6b374332cd14ab50f55-Paper-Conference.pdf)

5. **Intrinsic-dimensionality decision-analysis found its home at NeurIPS, not ICLR.** *Geometry of Decision Making in Language Models* (Joshi, Bhatt, Modi) — ID of hidden states across layers — is tagged **NeurIPS 2025 poster** on OpenReview despite ICLR-style framing. Reinforces finding 4: descriptive geometry papers are accepted somewhere top-tier, but ICLR reviewers push harder for consequences. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Jj4NdJtXwp)

6. **Causal representation learning papers with limited evaluation get borderline treatment.** *Reward-oriented Causal Representation Learning* (Yan, Acartürk, Tajer) received ICLR review text in the borderline band ("Technically solid paper where reasons to accept outweigh reasons to reject, **e.g., limited evaluation**") and was ultimately published as a **NeurIPS 2025 poster**. CRL is welcome at ICLR but "limited evaluation" is a recurring score-depressor — your external-provider confirmation is the antidote. [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=I1hgynwxGZ) · [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/118855)

7. **Non-LLM mechanistic interpretability methods papers have a harder path.** *Decompose the model: mechanistic interpretability in image models with generalized integrated gradients (GIG)* was submitted to ICLR 2025 and shows no acceptance tag; *Incidental Polysemanticity* (Lecomte, Schaeffer, Koyejo et al.) was likewise "Submitted to ICLR 2025" without an ICLR accept tag. Vision/non-LLM MI is underrepresented in the main track relative to LLM circuit work. [GIG](https://openreview.net/forum?id=avlfmW32qO) · [Incidental Polysemanticity](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OeHSkJ58TG)

8. **Interpretability submissions also get desk-rejected in the volume crunch.** *Circuit Compositions* and *Evaluating Synthetic Activations composed of SAE Latents in GPT-2* were both **ICLR 2025 desk rejects** — a reminder that formatting/scope compliance matters and that the interpretability track is crowded. [Circuit Compositions](https://openreview.net/forum?id=u4XyECA6Zd) · [SAE latents](https://openreview.net/forum?id=U0y32WKeOd)

9. **Sports multi-agent modeling is an accepted ICLR domain.** *Sports-Traj: A Unified Trajectory Generation Model for Multi-Agent Movement in Sports* (Xu & Fu, Northeastern) — **ICLR 2025 Poster**; *JointDiff: Bridging Continuous and Discrete in Multi-Agent Trajectory Generation* (sports-domain diffusion) — **ICLR 2026 Poster**. "Domain too niche" is empirically not fatal for sports trajectory work. [Sports-Traj](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9aTZf71uiD) · [JointDiff](https://openreview.net/forum?id=6jThckejtL) · [JointDiff ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011354)

10. **"X Done Right" corrective/diagnostic papers are accepted.** *Test-Time Training Done Right* (MIT/Adobe) — diagnosing failures of an existing method and fixing them principledly — accepted as **ICLR 2026 Poster**. Direct precedent for the "diagnose → repair" arc. [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009303) · [proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/ffbfff81fb78e4bb558273b91e12e318-Paper-Conference.pdf)

### (b) What reviewers reward/penalize

11. **Official policy: scale and SOTA are explicitly not required.** ICLR 2026 Reviewer Guide FAQ: "**No, a lack of state-of-the-art results does not by itself constitute grounds for rejection.** Submissions bring value to the ICLR community when they convincingly demonstrate new, relevant, impactful knowledge." Quote this norm in your rebuttal if scale objections arise. [ICLR 2026 Reviewer Guide](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuide)

12. **Practical reality: small-scale/toy-scope complaints are among the most frequent weakness categories.** A large analysis of ICLR 2024–2025 reviews (arXiv 2511.15462) taxonomizes the most-cited weaknesses as: "Small-scale experiments / lack of real-world validation", "Too few datasets / limited domain coverage", "Synthetic or toy datasets only", "Missing ablations", "Marginal gains / not significant". Also found score changes concentrate in borderline papers, so rebuttal engagement matters. Expect at least one scale/generality review; pre-write the response. [arXiv:2511.15462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15462) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15462)

13. **Small custom models are fine when the claim is mechanistic, penalized when the claim is empirical-general.** Compare finding 1 (140k-param-class analysis → Spotlight) with finding 12. Your framing must be "controlled scientific study of intervention response," not "new encoder achieving SOTA on sports analytics."

14. **Pre-registered-style protocols have no formal ICLR norm but align with rewarded rigor.** ICLR has no registered-reports track; however, reviewers reward evaluation hygiene, and the reviewer guide constrains rebuttal demands ("new experiments should not significantly change the scope"), which a frozen-protocol paper can invoke. Candidate locks / split locks / single-read holdout should be presented as an *evaluation-methodology contribution* (protection against adaptive overfitting), since most reviewers will not have seen the machinery before. Risk: a minority of reviewers may read the protocol as gimmick; mitigate with a short "protocol" box and a leakage-threat model. [ICLR 2026 Reviewer Guide](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuide)

15. **Negative/diagnostic results have both main-track precedent and a dedicated workshop.** Main track: findings 2 and 10. Dedicated venue: the *I Can't Believe It's Not Better* (ICBINB) workshop ran at ICLR 2025 ("Challenges in Applied Deep Learning") and ICLR 2026 ("Where LLMs need to improve"), explicitly soliciting "rigorous studies that demonstrate and analyze limitations… diagnostics, replications, counterfactual analyses", with an Entropic Award for "most surprising negative result". [ICBINB 2026](https://sites.google.com/view/icbinb-2026/home) · [ICLR 2026 workshop page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/10000772) · [ICLR 2025 workshop page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/workshop/23987)

### (c) Is "mechanism discovery + repair + independent external confirmation" a recognized shape?

16. **Yes — it is the "science-of-deep-learning → actionable fix" arc, and the interventional evidence is the key currency.** ICLR's interpretability tradition (e.g., *Interpretability in the Wild*, ICLR 2023) established causal intervention (patching/switching) as the gold standard over correlational probes; your evidence-selected causal switch speaks that language. What is less common — and therefore differentiating — is the *external confirmation on an independent data provider*: this reads as external validity, which finding 12 shows is a top reviewer pain point. [Interpretability in the Wild (ICLR 2023)](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11341)

17. **Typical fatal objections for this shape** (synthesized from findings 4–7, 11–12, and the weakness taxonomy): (i) "mechanism is correlational" — preempted by the causal switch and prospective Spearman ~0.85; (ii) "limited scale / toy models" — preempt only partially; add an argument for why 140k-param encoders are the *right* instrument (controlled intervention readout) plus, if feasible, one confirmatory probe on a larger off-the-shelf encoder; (iii) "limited generality — one sport, two providers" — mitigate by framing provider-shift confirmation as distribution-shift validation and scoping claims explicitly; (iv) "no downstream gain" — the repair must show a measurable task-metric improvement, not just restored geometry; make this the headline number; (v) "autoresearch selection is underspecified search" — document the frozen rules and 2-round bound as a protocol feature, not a bug.

18. **Environmental context: ICLR 2026 was noisy (27.4% acceptance, ~19.5k submissions, AI-generated-review controversy).** Expect variance; a clean, self-contained, protocol-rigorous paper is easier for an overloaded reviewer to score confidently than a sprawling one. [ICLR 2026 retrospective](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/03/31/a-retrospective-on-the-iclr-2026-review-process) · [Pangram analysis](https://www.pangram.com/blog/pangram-predicts-21-of-iclr-reviews-are-ai-generated)

### (d) Recommended positioning

19. **Submit to the main track.** Both the domain (sports multi-agent: findings 9) and the genre (representation analysis: findings 1–3) have recent main-track acceptances; the work is complete enough (diagnosis + mechanism + repair + external confirmation) that a workshop would undersell it.
20. **Target areas/keywords** (per ICLR CfP topic list): primary area *unsupervised/self-supervised representation learning*; secondary signals *causal reasoning*, *learning on graphs and other geometries & topologies*, *interpretability / science of deep learning*, *multi-agent / spatiotemporal modeling*. Suggested keyword set: "representation learning; self-supervised learning; mechanistic analysis; causal intervention; spectral geometry; multi-agent trajectory modeling; sports analytics; evaluation protocol". [ICLR 2026 Call for Papers](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers)
21. **Workshop fallback / parallel visibility**: ICBINB (if the diagnostic/negative-result component is emphasized), *Geometry-grounded Representation Learning and Generative Modeling* (ICLR 2026 workshop), *Unifying Concept Representation Learning* (ICLR 2026 workshop). [ICLR 2026 workshops](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/01/13/iclr2026-workshops)
