# Research: Methodological Novelty & Positioning — Support-Reallocation Probing + Geometry-Conditioned Response Prediction + Dual-Channel Repair (ICLR 2027)

## Summary

The closest prior art clusters around four lines: (1) split invariant/equivariant SSL representations (Xiao et al. 2021; EquiMod; Garrido et al.'s SIE), (2) learned partial equivariance (Romero & Lohit's Partial G-CNNs) and capsule routing (Lenssen et al.; Zhao et al.) — both direct ancestors of the proposed AMR, (3) equivariance-error / perturbation-benchmark diagnostics in point-cloud and molecular learning, and (4) controlled-per-factor sensitivity analysis (Morris elementary effects) and per-agent counterfactuals (COMA). No prior work was found that uses an **equal-energy displacement operator re-allocated across different node supports** as a controlled instrument to probe a multi-entity encoder — this appears to be a genuine methodological gap. The dual-channel context/intrinsic separation has strong precedents, so it must be framed as a *repair motivated by a new diagnostic*, not as novel architecture. A diagnosis + repair + independent-confirmation story is plausibly main-track-sufficient (precedent: RankMe-style diagnostic papers, SIE), **provided** the paper ships the demanded baseline/ablation battery; AMR is a strengthening, not a necessity, and carries real scope risk.

## Findings

### (a) Closest prior art and how this project differs

1. **Xiao et al., "What Should Not Be Contrastive in Contrastive Learning" (ICLR 2021)** — splits the representation into augmentation-invariant and augmentation-variant components, the direct conceptual ancestor of a dual-channel context/intrinsic split. *Difference*: they split over *image augmentations*; the project splits over *absolute deployment coordinates vs. translation-invariant configuration* of a physical multi-agent system, and arrives at the split via a measured intervention-response pathology rather than as an a-priori design. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05659; see also SIE/EquiMod cluster below.)

2. **Garrido, Najman & LeCun, "Self-Supervised Learning of Split Invariant Equivariant Representations" (SIE, ICML 2023)** — explicitly splits a joint-embedding representation into an invariant part and an equivariant part and benchmarks on 3DIEBench with controlled transformation probes. *Difference*: SIE decides *a priori* which factor is equivariant; the project *measures* which response modes are wrongly invariant/equivariant using support-reallocation interventions, and the 2:1 update allocation is tuned against the diagnosed failure rather than fixed by symmetry type. (https://openreview.net/pdf/4d239cf329589cb103624a418f3edc6aa2019656.pdf)

3. **Devillers & Lefort, "EquiMod" (ICLR 2023)** — a generic equivariance module that learns to *predict the displacement in embedding space caused by augmentations*. *Difference*: EquiMod predicts augmentation-induced displacement to shape training; the project predicts intervention-induced response from *normalized local geometry* (Jacobian, Gram, Laplacian spectral features) of a frozen encoder — geometry-conditioned response prediction rather than augmentation prediction. (https://openreview.net/forum?id=eDLwjKmtYFt)

4. **Romero & Lohit, "Learning Partial Equivariances From Data" (Partial G-CNNs, NeurIPS 2022)** — learns, per layer, *how much* equivariance each feature should have, interpolating between full equivariance and unrestricted maps; explicitly motivated by the fact that "relevant symmetries may differ for low and high-level features." This is the closest single paper to the proposed **AMR (Action-Mode Routing)**: routing modes to be invariant vs. equivariant ≈ learning per-mode equivariance levels. *Difference*: Partial G-CNNs learn the level from task loss on synthetic/CIFAR-style data; AMR would route based on an *empirically measured* response-to-intervention profile on physical multi-agent scenes. **The AMR section of the paper must cite and differentiate this or reviewers will.** (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/ec51d1fe4bbb754577da5e18eb54e6d1-Paper-Conference.pdf)

5. **Lenssen, Fey & Mutschewski, "Group Equivariant Capsule Networks" (NeurIPS 2018)** — a routing procedure that *guarantees* invariance/equivariance under group actions via manifold means and geodesic routing. *Difference*: hard-coded guaranteed symmetry vs. the project's data-driven diagnosis of which symmetries an already-trained unconstrained encoder actually violates, then a soft repair. (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/8100-group-equivariant-capsule-networks.pdf)

6. **Zhao et al., "Quaternion Equivariant Capsule Networks for 3D Point Clouds" (ECCV 2020)** — "the capsule layer enables us to disentangle geometry from pose," i.e., an explicit pose (≈ context channel) vs. configuration (≈ intrinsic channel) decomposition with equivariant routing on point sets. *Difference*: architectural disentanglement built in from scratch on rigid SO(3); the project retrofits the decomposition onto arbitrary sports-tracking encoders and validates it by controlled displacement interventions. This is the closest point-cloud analog of the dual-channel design. (https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/papers/123460001.pdf)

7. **Du et al., "Graph Neural Tangent Kernel" (NeurIPS 2019) / Jacot et al. (NeurIPS 2018)** — the Gram matrix of the encoder-output Jacobian w.r.t. inputs is precisely an *empirical (input-space) NTK*. Reviewers with theory background will immediately note this. *Difference*: NTK literature uses Gram(Jacobian w.r.t. *parameters*) to analyze training dynamics; the project uses Gram of ∂embedding/∂*positions* as a per-sample geometric feature to *predict response to redistributed interventions* — a different object and purpose, but the paper must state the relationship explicitly to avoid a "this is just the NTK" rejection. (http://papers.neurips.cc/paper/8809-graph-neural-tangent-kernel-fusing-graph-neural-networks-with-graph-kernels.pdf)

8. **Oono & Suzuki, "Graph Neural Networks Exponentially Lose Expressive Power" (ICLR 2020) and the over-smoothing / Dirichlet-energy literature (Cai & Wang; Nt & Maehara)** — Rayleigh quotients of node signals on the graph Laplacian are exactly Dirichlet energy, the standard over-smoothing diagnostic; GSP band-power features come from Shuman et al. (2013). *Difference*: that literature uses spectral features to characterize *representation collapse across depth*; the project uses them as *per-scene predictors of intervention sensitivity* (Spearman 0.6–0.85), a different claim — but the feature family is standard, so novelty must be placed on the predictive use and the equal-energy protocol, not the features themselves. (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/yang22n/yang22n.pdf for the spectral-effects line; https://web.media.mit.edu/~xdong/talk/ICASSP_GSPforML_Part1.pdf for GSP foundations.)

9. **Sports representation line: Stöckl et al., "Graph representations for the analysis of multi-agent spatiotemporal sports data" (Applied Intelligence 2022)** — explicitly studies permutation-invariant state representations of soccer scenes, the closest sports-side treatment of invariance in multi-entity encoders. *Difference*: they engineer invariance into the input representation; the project measures *which* invariances learned encoders fail to exhibit and predicts the failure from geometry. Complementary sports baselines: Play2Vec (Wang et al.), Disney's data-driven ghosting (Le et al. 2017), NFL Ghosts (arXiv:2406.17220) — all counterfactual *trajectory generation*, not perturbation-based representation probing. (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10489-022-03631-z; https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Data-Driven-Ghosting-using-Deep-Imitation-Learning-Paper1.pdf; https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17220v1)

10. **Novak et al., "Sensitivity and Generalization in Neural Networks: an Empirical Study" (NeurIPS 2018)** — precedent that Jacobian-norm sensitivity of a trained net is a meaningful, measurable quantity correlated with behavior. *Difference*: output-perturbation robustness vs. support-conditioned response prediction. Useful as a citation establishing Jacobian-based sensitivity as a legitimate diagnostic instrument. (https://openreview.net/forum?id=HJC2SzZCW)

### (b) Is "equal-energy perturbation redistributed over supports" prior art?

Searched sports analytics, MARL interpretability, adversarial graph attacks, point-cloud equivariance, and classical sensitivity analysis. **No exact match found.** The closest analogs, which the paper should cite as the lineage of the instrument:

- **Morris elementary-effects method (Morris 1991; GlobalSensitivity.jl docs)** — the canonical "apply the same-magnitude step Δ to one factor at a time and measure output change" design. The support-reallocation probe is a *spatial redistribution* generalization: the total displacement budget is held constant while the *support* (which subset of players/nodes carries it) varies. Framing it as "elementary effects with fixed total budget re-allocated across supports" gives reviewers a familiar anchor and makes the novelty claim precise and defensible. (https://docs.sciml.ai/GlobalSensitivity/stable/methods/morris)
- **Budgeted adversarial graph attacks (Zügner et al., Nettack, KDD 2018; Metattack)** — fixed perturbation budget allocated over nodes/edges to maximize damage. The probe is structurally identical in budget accounting but *non-adversarial*: same operator, different supports, measuring response rather than maximizing loss. Explicitly distinguishing "controlled instrument" from "attack" matters because adversarial robustness reviewers will otherwise misread the protocol. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07984)
- **COMA counterfactual baselines (Foerster et al., AAAI 2018)** — per-agent counterfactual marginalization (vary one agent, hold others fixed) is the MARL credit-assignment analog of per-support interventions. Same intervention-per-agent logic, different purpose (advantage estimation vs. representation diagnosis). (https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/shimon.whiteson/pubs/foersteraaai18.pdf)
- **Equivariance-error tests in point-cloud/molecular learning** (e.g., manual rotation/translation of a point cloud to measure descriptor response, as in Lin et al., PMLR 2023; EGNN/Vector-Neurons benchmarks) — apply a *known group element* to the *whole* input and measure embedding change. The support-reallocation probe differs by applying a *non-group, fixed-energy* displacement to *selected supports* — testing response structure, not group symmetry. (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v205/lin23a/lin23a.pdf)
- **Sports counterfactuals** (ghosting, pitch control, VAEP) generate plausible alternative trajectories for valuation; none fix perturbation energy and redistribute it across supports to probe an encoder.

**Verdict**: the equal-energy support-reallocation protocol appears novel as an evaluation instrument. The novelty claim should be scoped carefully: "controlled, budget-fixed, support-redistributed displacement interventions for probing multi-entity encoders" — with Morris/Nettack/COMA cited as conceptual ancestors so the claim is "novel combination," not "novel perturbation."

### (c) Baselines/ablations reviewers will demand

1. **SSL baselines on the same data**: SimSiam/BYOL/DINO(-v2) adapted to set/graph-structured sports scenes, plus at least one equivariant SSL (SIE or EquiMod on top of the same backbone). Reviewers will ask whether the diagnosed pathology is specific to the project's training recipe or generic to SSL of multi-entity scenes.
2. **Equivariant architecture baselines**: EGNN (Satorras et al.) and/or a permutation-equivariant set model (DeepSets/Set Transformer) trained on the same task — to show whether hard equivariance avoids the diagnosed response failure and what it costs on task metrics.
3. **Feature ablations for the predictor**: Jacobian-only vs. Gram-only vs. spectral-only vs. full; plus *simple baselines for the same prediction*: raw coordinate statistics, pairwise-distance histograms, attention-rollout scores, and a CKA-style representation-similarity predictor (CKA is the standard tool reviewers expect for comparing representation responses — Kornblith et al. 2019; note Davari et al. 2022 on CKA reliability caveats). (https://openreview.net/forum?id=8HRvyxc606)
4. **Intervention-protocol ablations**: effect of displacement magnitude (dose-response linearity check), support size scaling (1 → k nodes), random-support vs. structured-support controls, and a non-redistributed single-support control to prove the *reallocation* (not just perturbation) drives the signal.
5. **Statistical rigor**: per-match Spearman with match-level bootstrap CIs (n=8 external matches is thin — expect "small sample" pushback); report match-macro and pooled separately (already consistent with the project's PLAN); seed variance for all trained components (the 2:1 repair needs ≥3 seeds).
6. **Scale and generality**: at least one larger model (parameter/depth scaling) and ideally a second sport or dataset beyond soccer tracking; if compute-bound, a controlled capacity sweep on the frozen assets plus explicit limitation statement.
7. **Channel-separation ablations for the repair**: 1:0, 1:1, 2:1, 3:1 update allocations; remove-context-channel and remove-intrinsic-channel; verify translation-response near-zero by direct translation interventions (the project's own instrument should be applied to evaluate the repair — reviewers will insist the diagnostic closes the loop).

### (d) Is AMR (P4) necessary for a strong main-track paper?

**Honest assessment: not necessary, but it changes the paper's category.**

- The diagnosis + repair + independent-confirmation arc (P1–P3: instrument → geometry-conditioned prediction at Spearman 0.6–0.85 with prospective confirmation → task-semantic repair via dual-channel 2:1 → 8-match cross-provider confirmation) is a complete scientific story with three load-bearing contributions: a novel instrument (b), a predictive mechanism claim, and an actionable repair with external replication. Diagnostic/measurement papers of this shape are accepted at top venues: RankMe (Garrido et al., ICML 2023) is metric-only; SIE's benchmark half (3DIEBench) is a diagnostic contribution; "A New Perspective on the Effects of Spectrum in GNNs" (ICML 2022) is analysis-driven. (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/garrido23a.html)
- The main-track risk without AMR is the "so what" critique: reviewers may read the paper as an elaborate characterization of one family's encoders. The 2:1 repair partially answers this, but its demonstrated benefit must be on *task metrics and/or robustness*, not only on response-shaping (the project's own STATUS flags the causal switch as "RESPONSE_SHAPING_ONLY" — that gap is exactly where reviewer 2 will push). If the repair shows a measurable downstream gain under the frozen evaluation, the "diagnosis → validated repair" loop closes and AMR is dispensable.
- AMR adds a methods contribution and would strengthen novelty scores, but Partial G-CNNs (Romero & Lohit 2022) already exist, so AMR's marginal novelty is "routing *informed by the measured response profile*," not "learning invariance levels" per se. Shipping it half-validated (which the current gating suggests it would be) is riskier than omitting it: an under-powered P4 invites "the proposed method does not clearly improve over Partial G-CNNs / EquiMod" and can drag the whole paper down.
- Recommended framing: main-track paper = instrument + mechanism + repair + external confirmation, with AMR as a clearly-marked outlook; alternatively, AMR as a short "proof-of-concept" section only if it can be trained and gated to the same evidentiary standard as P3 within the submission timeline. Given the 8-match external confirmation is already the paper's strongest differentiator, protecting its statistical presentation matters more than adding P4.
