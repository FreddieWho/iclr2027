# Research: M7 — Why is the centroid (first moment) the easiest statistic to improve?

## Summary
Geometric and statistical theory jointly predicts the observed ordering (centroid −34% ≫ zone +0.0054, pair +0.0092): (i) mean/sum pooling is the native universal readout for permutation-invariant set functions, so a decoupled head directly optimizes the centroid's functional form; (ii) pairwise/zone statistics are second-/higher-order interactions requiring strictly richer pooling; (iii) the sample mean is statistically the cheapest moment to estimate. No theorem predicts the −34% magnitude — only the direction/ordering.

## Findings

**1. Centroid matches the architecture's native readout (DeepSets sum-decomposition).**
- **Claim:** Any permutation-invariant set function can be written as ρ(Σᵢ φ(xᵢ)) — i.e., a function of a pooled first moment in a learned embedding. A decoupled centroid head therefore optimizes exactly the object the backbone already computes, while pair/zone heads must extract information the pooling discards. **Sources:** [Deep Sets (NeurIPS 2017)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/f22e4747da1aa27e363d86d40ff442fe-Paper.pdf); universality-vs-latent-dimension refinement [JMLR 23 (2022)](http://jmlr.org/papers/volume23/21-0730/21-0730.pdf). **Support:** direct evidence (Theorem 2 of Zaheer et al.: sum-decomposition universality). **Confidence:** high for the representation claim; medium that it transfers to our backbone (researcher inference: applies if our encoder uses sum/mean pooling before the head).
- **Boundary:** Universality needs a wide-enough latent space. Wagstaff et al. prove latent dimension ≥ set size M is necessary and sufficient in the worst case ([PMLR v97](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/wagstaff19a.html)). With a fixed narrow bottleneck, universality fails first for complex functions — the simple linear mean survives longest. Do not claim "theory guarantees centroid learns best"; the guarantee is representability, not optimizability.

**2. Pairwise/zone statistics sit one rung higher in the interaction hierarchy (Janossy k-ary pooling).**
- **Claim:** Mean-pooling is 1-ary (first-order) pooling: it captures per-element terms but not element–element relations. Pairwise distances and zone occupancies are second-order/spatial statistics that need 2-ary (or spatially conditioned) pooling, at combinatorially higher cost. Under fixed head capacity and decoupled training, theory predicts smaller gains for pair/zone than for centroid. **Sources:** [Janossy pooling (Murphy et al. 2019)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17410v2) (survey account; original arXiv:1811.01900); limitations analysis [Wagstaff et al. 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/wagstaff19a/wagstaff19a.pdf). **Support:** interpretation (Janossy hierarchy explicitly ranks DeepSets as k=1; k=2 needed for pairwise interactions). **Confidence:** medium — the hierarchy is a theorem; that our zone/pair metrics map onto k=2 is researcher inference, though a defensible one.
- **Boundary:** Classical symmetric-function theory cuts both ways: every symmetric polynomial is expressible via power-sum moments (Newton's identities), so higher moments are reachable from the same scaffold *given enough capacity*. The prediction is only about difficulty/ordering at fixed capacity, and it reverses if the head is explicitly given pairwise inputs (e.g., edge features). Also note the caveat in finding 1: with latent dim ≫ set size, the gap should shrink.

**3. The mean is statistically the cheapest moment to estimate; covariance/pair structure costs more samples and stronger tails.**
- **Claim:** The empirical mean converges at O(1/√n) under only finite variance (CLT/Hoeffding; dimension-free mean-estimation bounds), while covariance/second-moment estimation needs fourth moments/bounded tails and sample size scaling with dimension or effective rank — so a finite training budget buys a larger error reduction for the centroid than for pair/zone statistics. **Sources:** [Estimating the moments of a random vector (Shawe-Taylor & Cristianini)](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/260372/1/EstimatingTheMomentsOfARandomVectorWithApplications.pdf) (dimension-free mean bounds); standard reference Vershynin, *High-Dimensional Probability* (covariance estimation, §4.6 — cited, not fetched). **Support:** direct evidence for the rate hierarchy; interpretation that it explains our gain ordering. **Confidence:** medium-high for the statistical hierarchy; medium for the causal link to our −34% vs +0.005/+0.009 split (consistent with, not proven by, theory).
- **Boundary:** This is about estimation error given n samples, not optimization dynamics. It does not predict a 34% figure, nor does it rule out large pair-gains with a better-suited head or more data. If pair/zone errors are dominated by model misspecification rather than sampling noise, extra data alone won't close the gap.

## Contradictions
None found on the core hierarchy (first moment ≤ second moment in difficulty) — representation theory, the Janossy hierarchy, and estimation theory all agree on direction. Partial tension: Newton's identities / universality theorems say higher moments are *representable* in the same scaffold, so a naïve reading ("mean pooling suffices for everything") contradicts the practical message of Wagstaff (capacity must grow with set size). Resolution: representability ≠ learnability at fixed capacity — state this boundary explicitly in the paper.
