# Research: Readout-head design beyond cosine for pair-similarity

## Summary
Keep cosine as the production readout. The best cost-adjusted upgrade for our setting (L2-normalized mode embeddings, triplet margin 0.2, InfoNCE τ=0.1) is (1) a learnable temperature + decoupled Circle-style margins on the same cosine scores, then (2) a diagonal/low-rank Mahalanobis (channel-gated cosine) if anisotropy is shown, and only (3) a hyperbolic pilot if hierarchy is demonstrated. Evidence is strongest for (1), moderate for (2), and negative-to-conditional for (3) at our scale.

## Findings

### 1. Rank #1 (do first): Learnable temperature + Circle-style asymmetric margins on cosine — highest gain/cost
**Claim:** Retaining `s=cos(u,v)` but replacing fixed `τ=0.1 / margin=0.2` with a CLIP-style learnable logit scale `s/τ` (clipped, e.g. `τ≥0.01`) plus Circle-loss self-paced weighting with separate within/between margins gives the largest reliable gain for ~10 lines of code and ~2 scalars.
**Sources:** [Circle Loss, CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Sun_Circle_Loss_A_Unified_Perspective_of_Pair_Similarity_Optimization_CVPR_2020_paper.html); [CLIP, ICML 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020); [SimCLR, ICML 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05709v1); [Understanding contrastive loss behaviour, CVPR 2021](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2021/wang2021cvpr-understanding/).
**Support:** direct evidence for components, interpretation for combination. Circle Loss unifies triplet and softmax-pair losses as `L=log[1+Σexp(γ α_n(s_n−Δ_n))·Σexp(−γ α_p(s_p−Δ_p))]` with self-paced weights `α` that up-weight hard/under-optimized pairs, and reports consistent gains over triplet/AM-Softmax/ArcFace-style baselines on re-ID and face benchmarks (researcher inference: the mechanism — letting each similarity optimize at its own pace with distinct `Δ_p,Δ_n` — directly addresses fixed-margin-0.2 rigidity). SimCLR ablations show ±2–5 pt swings from τ alone; Wang & Liu (CVPR 2021) prove/empirically show small τ sharpens uniformity but destroys tolerance to semantically close negatives, i.e. fixed τ=0.1 is likely too aggressive if modes share structure. CLIP makes `logit_scale=log(1/τ)` learnable (capped at 100) rather than fixed — direct precedent for learnable τ.
**Confidence:** high for "tune/learn τ + margins beats fixed 0.2/0.1"; medium for exact Circle hyperparameters transferring.
**Concrete recipe for OUR setting:** `logits = cos(u,v)/τ`, `τ=exp(−logit_scale)`, init `τ=0.1`, clip `τ∈[0.01,1.0]`; Circle head with `γ=64–128`, `m=0.25` (`Δ_p=1−m`, `Δ_n=m`) as starting grid; ablate `τ∈{0.05,0.07,0.1,0.2,learned}` × `m∈{0.2,0.25,0.35}`. Expected: +few % ranking accuracy / faster convergence at negligible compute. *Researcher inference.*

### 2. Rank #2 (conditional): Diagonal / low-rank Mahalanobis = channel-gated cosine — moderate gain, moderate risk
**Claim:** If embedding channels differ in relevance per mode-pair, a diagonal weight `s_W=Σ_k w_k û_k v̂_k` (or low-rank `L`, `s=(Lû)ᵀ(Lv̂)/…`) beats plain cosine; full `d×d` bilinear/Mahalanobis is overkill and prone to overfit at small sample sizes.
**Sources:** [ITML, ICML 2007](https://parajain.github.io/metric_learning_tutorial); [LMNN, JMLR 2009](https://jmlr.org/papers/v10/weinberger09a.html); [Cosine Similarity Metric Learning, ACCV 2010](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Cosine-Similarity-Metric-Learning-for-Face-Nguyen-Bai/472ba8dd4ec72b34e85e733bccebb115811fd726); [Large-scale similarity learning (joint Mahalanobis+bilinear), AAAI 2016](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/10459/10459-13-13987-1-2-20201228.pdf).
**Support:** interpretation. Classical result: Mahalanobis `d_M²=(x−y)ᵀM(x−y)`, `M=LᵀL`, equals Euclidean after learned linear map; CSML learns it explicitly for cosine/face verification. AAAI-2016 comparison finds Mahalanobis > pure bilinear and joint > single metric — but on linear features, not normalized deep mode embeddings (researcher inference: transfer is plausible, not proven). Diagonal `w≥0, Σw=d` has only `d` params, preserves cosine geometry, and is a strict generalization (reduces to cosine when `w=1`); full `M` adds `O(d²)` params plus PSD projection cost.
**Confidence:** medium for diagonal gating helping if anisotropy exists; low for full bilinear helping in OUR setting.
**Concrete recipe:** freeze encoders, train `w` (softmax-parameterized) on pair-ranking loss for 1–3 epochs with L1-toward-1 regularization; accept only if held-out pair-AUC > cosine by >2 SE. Full-rank only if `d<64` and pairs>10k. *Researcher inference.*

### 3. Rank #3 (pilot only): Hyperbolic readout — high cost, justified only with proven hierarchy
**Claim:** Poincaré/Lorentz distance `d_H` plus entailment cones help when similarity is genuinely tree-like (coarse→fine modes); otherwise it underperforms matched Euclidean/CLIP baselines and adds numerical/optimization overhead.
**Sources:** [Poincaré embeddings, NeurIPS 2017](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/7213-poincare-embeddings-for-learning-hierarchical-representations.pdf); [MERU, ICML 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/desai23a/desai23a.pdf); [Compositional entailment learning, ICLR 2025](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3i13Gev2hV).
**Support:** direct evidence (conditional). Nickel & Kiela show large gains over Euclidean *on WordNet-type hierarchies at low dim*; MERU matches/approaches CLIP on zero-shot retrieval while adding interpretable entailment structure — not a general retrieval win (researcher inference: hierarchy must be in the data, not the wish). Requires exp-maps, curvature `c` tuning, gradient clipping, and re-tuning of τ/margin because `d_H` scale differs from cosine.
**Confidence:** high that cost is real; low that it helps OUR task without a hierarchy test.
**Concrete recipe:** gate on a 1-day test: (a) tree-likeness check (δ-hyperbolicity / hierarchical clustering support); (b) if passed, Lorentz model with learnable curvature, `s=−d_H/τ`, entailment loss weight small. Otherwise defer. *Researcher inference.*

## Temperature / margin interaction (cross-cutting)
- Wang & Isola (ICML 2020) decompose InfoNCE asymptotically into alignment + uniformity on the hypersphere: τ controls the trade-off ([paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/wang20k.html)).
- Wang & Liu (CVPR 2021) extend to uniformity-vs-tolerance: lowering τ improves uniformity/separability but penalizes near-duplicate tolerance ([summary](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2021/wang2021cvpr-understanding/)). Practical read: τ=0.1 is on the sharp/low-tolerance end (SimCLR optimal ≈0.1–0.5 depending on batch/augmentation); if our modes contain near-positive pairs, learned or slightly larger τ + explicit margin is safer. Confidence: medium-high.
- Angular-margin losses (CosFace CVPR 2018; ArcFace CVPR 2019) show cosine margin `m≈0.25–0.5` with scale `s≈30–64` is the stable operating range for normalized features — consistent with starting Circle at `m=0.25, γ≈64`. Confidence: medium (face domain, interpreted transfer).

## Contradictions
- Hyperbolic papers report wins on hierarchical data while large-scale CLIP/MERU comparisons show parity, not dominance, on flat retrieval — resolved as conditional (hierarchy-dependent), not contradictory.
- SimCLR favors a single fixed τ after grid search; CLIP favors learnable τ — resolved as scale/batch dependence; learnable-with-clip covers both.
- None found on Circle-style weighting beating fixed triplet margin within face/re-ID suite.

## Missing evidence
- No direct ablation of τ∈{0.05…0.2} × margin 0.2 on OUR mode embeddings — needed to confirm Rank #1 sizing.
- No channel-anisotropy diagnostic (per-dim variance of `û⊙v̂` for positives vs negatives) — needed to trigger Rank #2.
- No tree-likeness score for our mode taxonomy — needed to trigger Rank #3.
- Exact Circle `γ/m` transfer to L2-normalized non-face embeddings is unverified.
