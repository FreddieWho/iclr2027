# Research: S3 — Title/Abstract Framing for Novelty Positioning

## Summary
Top ML mechanism papers lead with the mechanism as the subject ("X competes / emerges / localizes"), relegate the testbed to the subtitle or abstract sentence 1–2, and structure abstracts as gap → causal instrument → quantitative signature → generality claim. For our paper, framings that name **allocation-competition** and **support-reallocation probing** up front survive novelty/niche objections best; framings that lead with "soccer" read as applied. Three skeletons below (A: mechanism-causal, B: representation-structure, C: phenomenon-probe) each preempt a distinct reviewer kill-shot.

## Findings

**1. Claim:** Mechanism-first titles put a general noun (mechanism, competition, circuit, representation) before the colon; testbed goes after. Application-first titles ("Soccer tracking with…") get routed to niche/applied reviewers. **Sources:** [Emergent World Representations: Exploring a Sequence Model Trained on Othello](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13301) (ICLR 2023); [BERT Rediscovers the Classical NLP Pipeline](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1452/) (ACL 2019); [ICML Best Practices](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2022/BestPractices) (2022 — "title expresses the main contribution"). **Support:** direct evidence (title strings + best-practice wording). **Confidence:** high. *Inference: the Othello paper is our closest precedent — a game-board testbed accepted as general representation work because the title's subject is "world representations," not Othello.*

**2. Claim:** Surviving abstracts follow a 5-move structure: (i) broad problem, (ii) specific gap/limit of correlational evidence, (iii) our causal instrument, (iv) one quantified signature, (v) implication beyond testbed. Advice sources converge on this independently. **Sources:** [Highly Opinionated Advice on How to Write ML Papers](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/eJGptPbbFPZGLpjsp/highly-opinionated-advice-on-how-to-write-ml-papers) (situation → claims → payoff); [How to Write ML Papers](https://sebastianfarquhar.com/on-research/2024/11/04/how_to_write_ml_papers) (2024); [Writing More Successful ML Papers](https://medium.com/data-science/writing-more-successful-machine-learning-research-papers-39863ca9ea90). **Support:** interpretation (synthesis across advice, not a controlled study). **Confidence:** medium-high.

**3. Claim:** Probing-only papers draw the standard "correlation ≠ use" objection; papers that pair probe with intervention/edit (ROME-style) survive it. Our support-reallocation probe must be framed as an *intervention with dose-response*, not a decoder. **Sources:** [Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262) (NeurIPS 2022); [Amnesic Probing / probing critique](https://seantrott.substack.com/p/mechanistic-interpretability-for) (summary of Elazar et al. critique). **Support:** direct evidence for ROME structure; interpretation for the review dynamic. **Confidence:** medium.

### Three alternative framings (exact wording sketches)

**A — Causal-mechanism (recommended for ICLR).**
- *Title sketch:* "Allocation Competition in Permutation-Structured Representations: Causal Evidence from Support-Reallocation Probing"
- *Abstract skeleton:* "[1] Multi-entity models must allocate fixed representational support across interchangeable entities. [2] Whether errors reflect competition for that support — vs. noisy features — is unknown because probes are correlational. [3] We introduce support-reallocation probing: forcibly reassigning support from entity *i* to *j* and measuring the predicted, signed performance transfer. [4] In soccer tracking (22 symmetric agents, natural permutations), reallocation shifts error with dose-response [REPORT SLOPE/CIs], reversible under restoration, absent in permutation-ablated controls. [5] This identifies allocation-competition as a general failure mode of permutation-structured representations."
- *Preempts:* "just another probe" + "soccer is niche" (soccer appears once, as instantiation; generality is in sentence 1/5). *Closest example:* ROME, NeurIPS 2022 (locate-then-edit causal framing). **Evidence grade:** medium-high.

**B — Representation-structure (safest if reviewers doubt causality).**
- *Title sketch:* "Permutation-Structured Representations Share Support by Competition: Structure, Probe, and Transfer"
- *Abstract skeleton:* "[1] Representations of multi-entity scenes face a symmetry constraint: entities are interchangeable but support is finite. [2] We formalize this as allocation-competition with two falsifiable signatures (zero-sum transfer; symmetry-dependent magnitude). [3] A support-reallocation probe tests both without retraining. [4] Soccer tracking confirms both signatures across [N] matches/teams [NUMBERS]; signatures vanish when permutation structure is broken. [5] Formalism + probe transfer to any permutation-structured multi-entity domain."
- *Preempts:* "no formalism / unfalsifiable" + "single-domain" (falsifiable signatures + explicit transfer sentence). *Closest example:* Othello world-representations, ICLR 2023 (formal probes + game testbed → general claim). **Evidence grade:** medium.

**C — Phenomenon-first (highest upside, highest risk).**
- *Title sketch:* "When Entities Compete for Representation: Support Reallocation Reveals Allocation-Competition in the Wild"
- *Abstract skeleton:* "[1] Curious failure mode: degrading one tracked entity *improves* another. [2] Correlational analyses cannot say why. [3] We show this is allocation-competition, via support-reallocation interventions that move error predictably between entities. [4] Demonstration on professional soccer tracking ([N] sequences; transfer effect [X±CI]; reversal on restore). [5] Same probe applies wherever entities share a permutation-structured code."
- *Preempts:* "incremental / boring" (opens with surprise); *invites* "cute phenomenon, general?" — must close hard with sentence 5 + cross-team splits. *Closest example:* Induction-heads narrative (Anthropic 2022, non-archival) — phenomenon → mechanism storytelling. **Evidence grade:** low-medium (storytelling evidence is anecdotal, not controlled).

## Contradictions
- Advice splits on colon-titles: best-practice pages urge short memorable titles, while most-cited interpretability papers use long "Claim: Evidence from X" colon titles. Both succeed; no resolved rule.
- Phenomenon-first openings are praised in writing guides for hooking readers but flagged by reviewers as hype when the generality sentence is weak. Recorded, not resolved.

## Missing evidence
- No controlled data linking title pattern to accept/reject (would need OpenReview corpus study; not run here).
- Our dose-response numbers and control names are placeholders — abstract skeletons need the real slope, CIs, and permutation-ablation label before submission.
- Whether ICLR 2027 reviewers treat soccer as "natural multi-agent benchmark" (like NBA SportVU trajectories, e.g., Social-GAN, CVPR 2018) vs. "sports analytics niche" is unverified; framing B hedges this best.
