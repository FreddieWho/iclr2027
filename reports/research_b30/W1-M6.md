# M6: Cost of Single-Frame Restriction — What Would Temporal Coherence Add?

## Summary
Our encoder sees one frozen snapshot at 1 Hz with no temporal modeling, so any signal carried by velocity, acceleration, ordering, or multi-second coherence is structurally unavailable. The match-half probe going 0/8 against temporal-context-degraded input is consistent with (not proof of) this ceiling: when context is degraded, single frames cannot recover it. Literature across three families — sports trajectory models, slow-feature analysis, temporal-consistency SSL — shows temporal structure adds large, measurable gains precisely where single frames are ambiguous. Cheapest fix preserving our setting: a slow-feature/temporal-coherence auxiliary loss over adjacent 1 Hz frames.

## Findings

**1. Claim: Single frames are fundamentally ambiguous for motion-dependent categories; temporal models beat them by large margins in sports video.**
Trajectory-based sports representations exist because appearance alone cannot resolve formation shifts, runs, presses, or plays unfolding over seconds. TrajSV (Wang et al., arXiv 2025) builds clip (CRNet) + video (VRNet) representations from player/ball trajectories with a trajectory-enhanced Transformer and reports SOTA sports-video retrieval with ~70% improvement, 105.6% HR@1 gain over ResNet baselines on standard sets. **Sources:** [TrajSV arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11569), [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394525044_TrajSV_A_Trajectory-based_Model_for_Sports_Video_Representations_and_Applications). **Support:** direct evidence (reported benchmark deltas). **Confidence:** medium — deltas are retrieval/action-spotting, not our probe, and trajectory inputs assume tracking; magnitude direction is robust. *Researcher inference: our 1 Hz snapshots lose exactly the trajectory signal TrajSV exploits.*

**2. Claim: Sports trajectory transformers treat trajectories (not pixels) as first-class input; applicable pattern is masked trajectory modeling.**
Sports-Traj / UniTraj line frames arbitrary multi-agent trajectories as masked inputs adaptable to diverse sports scenarios (OpenReview). Related: pass-receiver prediction fusing video + player/ball trajectories via 3D-CNN+LSTM+Transformer (Honda et al., CVPRW 2022); hierarchical spatiotemporal transformers for tracking (GameSense). **Sources:** [Sports-Traj OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9aTZf71uiD), [Honda et al. CVPRW 2022 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/CVSports/papers/Honda_Pass_Receiver_Prediction_in_Soccer_Using_Video_and_Players_Trajectories_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf). **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** medium. *Inference: this is the high-cost, high-ceiling extension — needs tracked positions, not just frames.*

**3. Claim: Slow-feature analysis (SFA) formalizes the cheapest temporal supervision: nearby frames should be near in embedding space.**
Wiskott & Sejnowski (Neural Computation 2002) define SFA: extract slowly varying functions of quickly varying input under zero-mean/unit-variance/decorrelation constraints. Mobahi et al. (ICML 2009) extended temporal coherence to deep architectures. Jayaraman & Grauman (CVPR 2016, "Slow and steady") show first-order slowness is insufficient and add higher-order steadiness (predicting *how* features change), improving action/object learning from unlabeled video. **Sources:** [Jayaraman & Grauman CVPR 2016 PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~grauman/papers/slow-steady-cvpr2016.pdf), [arXiv 1506.04714](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.04714), [Wiskott lecture notes](https://www.ini.rub.de/PEOPLE/wiskott/Teaching/UnsupervisedLearning/StudentsWeb-skj8378df/SlowFeatureAnalysisApplications-L0-LectureNotes-StudentsWeb.pdf). **Support:** direct evidence for method; interpretation for transfer to our setting. **Confidence:** high (method), medium (expected gain size here).

**4. Claim: Temporal-consistency SSL (TCN, TCC) learns per-frame embeddings where time itself is supervision — directly relevant to 1 Hz frames.**
Time-Contrastive Networks pull together simultaneous multi-view frames and push apart temporal neighbors; Temporal Cycle-Consistency (Dwibedi et al., CVPR 2019) aligns videos via differentiable cycle-consistency, producing per-frame embeddings matchable by nearest neighbor, complementary to Shuffle-and-Learn/TCN (~495+ citations). Cycle-Contrast (NeurIPS 2020) combines TCC with instance discrimination for transferable video encoders. **Sources:** [TCC CVPR 2019 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Dwibedi_Temporal_Cycle-Consistency_Learning_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf), [arXiv 1904.07846](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07846). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high that the family works; medium on transfer to sparse 1 Hz sports frames (most results use dense video).

**5. Claim: Masked video modeling quantifies the temporal-modeling premium over single frames.**
VideoMAE (Tong et al., NeurIPS 2022 Spotlight) with tube masking + high mask ratio on vanilla ViT reaches 87.4% Kinetics-400, 75.4% SSv2, 91.3% UCF101 — SOTA without extra data at the time; parallel MAE-ST (Feichtenhofer et al., NeurIPS 2022) confirms masked spatiotemporal learning. SSv2 in particular is temporally sensitive (ordering matters), where single-frame models lag badly. **Sources:** [VideoMAE GitHub/NeurIPS 2022](https://github.com/mcg-nju/videomae), [NeurIPS 2022 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/416f9cb3276121c42eebb86352a4354a-Paper-Conference.pdf). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high for "temporal modeling adds large gains on motion-sensitive tasks"; medium on exact transfer (dense 16-frame clips vs our 1 Hz).

**6. Claim (internal context): the 0/8 match-half result is consistent with a single-frame ceiling but does not isolate cause.**
*Researcher inference, labelled as such:* 0/8 against temporal-degraded input is what a velocity-blind encoder would produce, but with n=8 it is also compatible with probe noise or sampling. Do not cite it externally as proof; use it as motivation for a controlled ablation (same backbone ± temporal head).

## Ranked temporal extensions for OUR setting (frozen 1 Hz, single-frame encoder)

**#1 (best cost/benefit): Slow-feature / temporal-coherence auxiliary loss — no input change.**
*Input change:* none; keep single-frame encoder, add loss pulling adjacent-frame (t, t+1s) embeddings together + pushing apart distant frames (SFA-style slowness or TCN n-pairs), optionally second-order steadiness (Jayaraman & Grauman, CVPR 2016).
*Expected effect:* small-to-moderate gain on temporally stable attributes (formation, phase), reduced jitter; teaches invariance to 1 Hz sampling noise. No new signals (no velocity readout).
*Key ref:* Jayaraman & Grauman, CVPR 2016; Wiskott & Sejnowski, Neural Comp. 2002.

**#2: Lightweight motion adapter — minimal input change (frame pairs/diffs).**
*Input change:* feed (frame_t, frame_t−1s) or frame-difference/optical-flow-lite into a small temporal adapter atop frozen single-frame features; or 3-frame tube embeddings à la VideoMAE with joint space-time attention only in top layers.
*Expected effect:* moderate gain; recovers direction/speed cues (press, counter, run) that #1 cannot; cost is one extra frame buffer + small head, backbone stays frozen.
*Key ref:* Tong et al., VideoMAE, NeurIPS 2022 (Spotlight); Feichtenhofer et al., MAE-ST, NeurIPS 2022.

**#3 (highest ceiling, highest cost): Trajectory-transformer head — new input (tracks).**
*Input change:* add player/ball tracks (even coarse detections at 1 Hz) as token sequences into a trajectory Transformer (CRNet-style clip encoder) fused with frame embeddings.
*Expected effect:* largest gain on play-level semantics (what TrajSV's ~70–105% retrieval deltas gesture at); cost is a tracker + new encoder + training data.
*Key ref:* Wang et al., TrajSV, arXiv:2508.11569 (2025); Fu et al., Sports-Traj/UniTraj, OpenReview; Honda et al., CVPRW 2022.

## Contradictions
None on core direction (all families favor temporal > single frame). Disputed: *how much* sparse 1 Hz gains transfer from dense-video results — VideoMAE/TCC use 8–16 dense frames; at 1 Hz, fast actions alias, so gains may concentrate on slow-phase structure. SFA-style slowness can also *harm* fast-event discrimination if over-weighted (steadiness term is the known mitigation).

## Missing evidence
No controlled same-backbone single-frame vs +temporal ablation in our 1 Hz sports setting (needed to convert 0/8 hint into a number). Exact venues/years for Sports-Traj (OpenReview preprint, year unverified) and TCN original (NIPS 2016 workshop/RSS 2017 versions) not re-verified; cite TCC/VideoMAE/Slow-Steady for decision-critical claims.
