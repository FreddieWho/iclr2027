# Research: S8 — Support Reallocation (eps=0.25) as Adversarial-Robustness Framing

## Summary
An equal-energy support reallocation with eps=0.25 is closest, in robustness language, to a **sparse/structured (L0 + budget-constrained) perturbation**, not a standard Lp attack; graph/structure-attack and sparse-certificate literatures are the relevant precedents. Validity-regime + repair results can be reframed as robustness contributions, but only the weakest framing (structured-corruption robustness) is safe without new analysis; any "certified" claim requires a formal threat model plus a certificate, which the current frozen-operator evidence does not provide.

## Findings

1. **Claim:** The mature precedent for "reallocation as attack" is graph/structure attacks with discrete budgets (edge/feature flips), where threat models are defined by perturbation count/radius, not Lp norm. Nettack introduced test-time evasion + poisoning on attributed graphs via structure+feature perturbations under a budget; later work formalized certifiable robustness to graph perturbations for PageRank-type models. **Sources:** [Zügner et al. (KDD 2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07984); [Bojchevski & Günnemann (NeurIPS 2019)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Certifiable-Robustness-to-Graph-Perturbations-Bojchevski-G%C3%BCnnemann/458757a205dc73e17683458d1c432e9bbff42e5c). **Support:** direct evidence (threat-model definitions and attack/certificate setup). **Confidence:** high.
   *Researcher inference:* eps=0.25 equal-energy reallocation maps naturally to this family: a support-L0 constraint plus an energy/mass-preservation constraint. State this mapping explicitly if reframing; do not call it L_inf/L2.

2. **Claim:** Certified vs empirical robustness are distinct bars. Empirical = performance under a fixed attack suite; certified = provable invariance within a radius via smoothing/ablation or deterministic certificates. Randomized smoothing gives L2 certificates (Cohen et al.); randomized **ablation** and sparsity-aware smoothing extend this to L0/sparse and discrete graph data; KDD'21 gives tight GNN structural-perturbation certificates via randomized smoothing. Separately, the evaluation literature warns that non-adaptive/fixed operators overstate robustness (obfuscated gradients; evaluation guidelines). **Sources:** [Cohen et al. (ICML 2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/cohen19c.html); [Levine & Feizi (AAAI 2020)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/5888/5744); [Bojchevski et al. (ICML 2020)](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/bojchevski20a/bojchevski20a.pdf); [Wang et al. (KDD 2021)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3447548.3467295); [Athalye et al. (ICML 2018)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/athalye18a.html); [Carlini et al. (arXiv 2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02918) [note: search conflated; Carlini evaluation paper is arXiv:1902.06705 — flagged below]. **Support:** direct evidence for certified/empirical split; interpretation for what eps=0.25 lacks. **Confidence:** high for split; medium for exact certificate transfer.
   *Researcher inference:* validity-regime = empirical-robustness curve at best; repair = a denoising/recovery defense. Neither is a certificate today.

3. **Claim:** Sparse-attack baselines (few-pixel/L0) show small-support changes can break models, so a "reallocation preserves behavior" result is non-trivial only against an adaptive adversary. **Sources:** One-pixel attack precedent [Su et al. (IEEE TEVC 2019)] — venue/year from general knowledge, not re-fetched; treat as medium confidence. **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** medium. ### Three reframing options (at most 3)

**Option A — Safest: structured-corruption / distribution-shift robustness (recommended).**
- *Exact claim:* "Under an equal-energy support reallocation of eps=0.25, [metric] stays within [tolerance] over the stated validity regime; a repair step recovers [metric] by [amount] outside it."
- *Extra analysis needed:* (i) write perturbation set formally (support move + energy preservation + eps definition); (ii) report degradation curve vs eps, not a single point; (iii) compare to at least one generic baseline (random reallocation / Gaussian noise matched on energy).
- *Reviewer pushback risk:* Low. Risk is only "incremental" if no adversary is claimed. Do not use "adversarial" or "certificate" here; say "structured perturbation."
- *Evidence grade:* Medium (analogy to corruption-robustness practice; no new theorem needed).

**Option B — Medium risk: empirical adversarial robustness to a sparse threat model.**
- *Exact claim:* "We evaluate a sparse equal-energy threat model (eps=0.25) and show [attack success rate / robust accuracy] = X with repair compression to Y."
- *Extra analysis needed:* (i) adaptive attack: optimize reallocation placement against the model (white-box or strong transfer), not only the frozen operator; (ii) follow evaluation guidelines — ablations, multiple restarts, gradient-free check so repair is not obfuscated gradients; (iii) clean-accuracy tradeoff. Cite Nettack threat-model style + Athalye/Carlini evaluation norms.
- *Reviewer pushback risk:* Medium-high if only the frozen non-adaptive operator is shown; security reviewers will call it "not worst-case." Mitigate by labeling frozen operator a *fixed attack* and reporting adaptive results separately.
- *Evidence grade:* Low-medium (requires new experiments; current evidence insufficient for a security claim).

**Option C — Highest risk / highest reward: certificate-style guarantee (only if willing to do theory).**
- *Exact claim (hedged):* "We characterize a certified-style radius: with [randomized ablation/smoothing over reallocations], the prediction is provably stable with probability ≥ 1−α for support moves within [radius]." Or weaker: "validity regime as an empirical stability certificate."
- *Extra analysis needed:* (i) pick one certificate machinery (randomized ablation for L0 per Levine & Feizi; sparsity-aware smoothing per Bojchevski et al.; or GNN structural certificate per Wang et al.); (ii) define null/perturbation distribution over support reallocations, compute certified accuracy vs radius; (iii) never claim "certified" from the validity curve alone.
- *Reviewer pushback risk:* High if "certified/verified/provable" appears without a theorem + certified-accuracy plot; ML-security reviewers treat this as a red flag. The word "reframe" alone will not survive.
- *Evidence grade:* Low (no certificate exists yet; substantial new work).
