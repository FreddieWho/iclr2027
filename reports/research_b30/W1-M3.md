# Research: M3 — When ranking metrics and response-prediction metrics dissociate

## Summary
Pair-ranking accuracy is ordinal and threshold-free; intervention-response rho (correlation of predicted vs actual response magnitudes) is cardinal and needs calibrated distances. The literature predicts exactly the observed dissociation: good ordering can coexist with bad magnitudes and vice versa. For triplet vs InfoNCE, the mechanism is geometric — margin losses stop at the margin (local order, clustered geometry) while contrastive uniformity spreads embeddings (graded distances, noisy local order).

## Findings
1. **Claim:** High AUC/pair-accuracy with poor calibration is a known, formal result — ranking metrics are invariant to any monotone transform of scores, while response prediction (rho, MSE/Brier) is not. A perfectly calibrated classifier has convex ROC, but high-AUC classifiers can be badly miscalibrated; isotonic regression on a held-out set fixes calibration without changing ranking. **Sources:** Hernández-Orallo et al., JMLR 2012 (unified view of metrics via threshold choice); Flach, AAAI 2019 (performance evaluation overview); calibration handbook chapter (Hernández-Orallo et al., 2010); neural-ranker scale-calibration work, Findings of ACL 2025. **Support:** direct evidence (handbook states "high AUC but probabilities might differ"; ACL paper jointly evaluates nDCG + MSE/ECE). **Confidence:** high. **Reading for us:** pair-acc ≈ AUC-family; response-rho ≈ calibration-sensitive. Dissociation is expected whenever embedding distances are monotonically right but cardinally wrong.
2. **Claim:** Triplet (margin) and InfoNCE (softmax-contrastive) produce systematically different geometries: triplet gives few strong updates on hard/mined cases, concentrating learning and preserving intra-class detail but collapsing to margin-satisfying clusters; InfoNCE applies frequent small updates favoring smooth, uniform hypersphere spread (alignment + uniformity). High uniformity can hurt fine-grained local order ("uniformity tolerance" to false negatives); low uniformity/dimensional collapse hurts graded magnitudes. **Sources:** Wang & Isola, ICML 2020 (alignment vs uniformity); variance/greediness comparison of 7 metric losses incl. Triplet vs InfoNCE, ICASSP 2026; Rusak et al., NeurIPS SSL workshop 2022 (content-suppresses-style dimensional collapse). **Support:** direct evidence for mechanism; interpretation that it maps onto our high-pair/low-rho vs low-pair/high-rho split is researcher inference. **Confidence:** medium-high. **Reading for us:** high-pair/low-rho ⇒ suspect collapsed/clustered distances (margin satisfied, magnitudes flattened); low-pair/high-rho ⇒ suspect over-spread/noisy local neighborhoods (graded trend kept, local flips).
3. **Claim:** Ordering disagreement between embedding-evaluation metrics across seeds/runs is empirically documented: Recall@1/NMI vs classification-accuracy rankings flip with seed and hyperparameter choices (Musgrave et al., ECCV 2020 "reality check"); linear-probe vs kNN vs fine-tune orderings of SSL methods disagree and are normalization-sensitive (Marks et al., IJCV 2025 / arXiv:2407.12210). Kendall-tau between metric orderings is the standard way to quantify this. **Sources:** Musgrave et al., ECCV 2020; Marks et al., IJCV 2025; Kornblith et al., ICML 2019 (representation-similarity methodology background). **Support:** direct evidence of metric-ordering instability; application to pair-acc vs response-rho Kendall-tau is inference. **Confidence:** medium. **Reading for us:** 3 dissociating cases across seeds/losses is within the documented noise-plus-geometry regime, not necessarily a new phenomenon — must test whether dissociation is systematic by loss or within-seed noise.

## Contradictions
None found on the core ranking-vs-calibration point. One tension: some sources frame InfoNCE uniformity as beneficial (cluster-preserving, COLT 2023/Parulekar et al.) while others frame excess uniformity as harmful (tolerance/collapse). Both can hold at different temperatures — researcher inference: temperature/batch-size regime decides which side we are on.

## Missing evidence
No paper found directly studying "pair-ranking accuracy vs intervention-response rho" as a named pair; the mapping to AUC-vs-calibration is an analogy (strong but not identity). Exact temperature/margin values in our artifacts were not re-examined here. No source-check tool was available; claims rest on fetched abstracts plus well-known results, not full-text verification.

## Next steps
Run the 3 frozen-artifact analyses below; if Analysis 1 shows isotonic recalibration closes the rho gap, report dissociation as calibration artifact, not representation failure.

### Concrete analyses on OUR frozen artifacts (max 3)
**A1. Ordering-vs-magnitude split (pair-acc vs response-rho + isotonic control).** Metric pair: pair-ranking accuracy vs Spearman rho of (embedding distance → intervention response). Compute per-seed/per-loss scatter + Kendall-tau between the two orderings. Then fit isotonic regression distance→response on train split, re-score rho on test. Meaning: gap that vanishes after isotonic fit = monotone order preserved, cardinal calibration lost (benign for ranking, fatal for response). Gap that persists = true ordering failure. Ref: Hernández-Orallo et al., JMLR 2012.
**A2. Geometry fingerprint of each dissociating case.** Metric pair: (intra/inter-class variance ratio, alignment, uniformity, effective rank of embeddings) vs dissociation direction. Meaning: high-pair/low-rho + low variance/low rank = margin-collapse (triplet satisfied margin, flattened magnitudes); low-pair/high-rho + high uniformity = over-spread (InfoNCE uniformity noise flips local pairs, keeps global trend). Ref: Wang & Isola, ICML 2020; ICASSP 2026 variance/greediness.
**A3. Systematic-vs-noise test.** Metric pair: Kendall-tau of (pair-acc ordering vs response-rho ordering) across seeds within each loss vs across losses. Meaning: dissociation stable by loss (all triplet one way, all InfoNCE other) = loss-geometry effect; scattered within loss = seed noise, cite Musgrave et al., ECCV 2020 and do not over-claim. Ref: Musgrave et al., ECCV 2020; Marks et al., IJCV 2025.
