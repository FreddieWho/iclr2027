# Research: Distilling a Neural Mode Embedding into Closed Form (A3)

## Summary
Our object is a 64-dim MLP output over spectral band features. The strongest precedent for distilling such a learned representation into equations is Cranmer et al.: impose inductive bias / bottleneck, fit the network, then run symbolic regression (PySR) on its internal activations — recovering exact force laws/Hamiltonians and one new cosmology formula. Concept-bottleneck models and Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KAN) offer two complementary routes: human-named concepts, or learnable univariate functions snapped to symbols. None guarantees lossless replacement at 64-dim; all succeed only after dimensionality reduction to a few functionally important directions.

## Findings

1. **Claim:** Symbolic regression on network internals (not raw data) recovers known physics and can beat the network's own error. **Sources:** Cranmer et al., NeurIPS 2020 ([paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/c9f2f917078bd2db12f23c3b413d9cba-Paper.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11287)). **Support:** direct evidence — GNN with symbolic-friendly bias fit N-body data, then Eureqa/PySR fit messages/edge functions; recovered force laws and Hamiltonians; cosmology case yielded a new analytic dark-matter formula. **Confidence:** high for method existence; medium for transfer to spectral features. *Inference: fitting SR to MLP outputs (denoised, dense sampling) is easier than fitting raw noisy labels — the key trick to reuse.*

2. **Claim:** PySR / SymbolicRegression.jl is the practical modern tool: evolutionary search + Pareto front (accuracy vs. complexity), recovers Kepler/Hubble-type laws with constants. **Sources:** Cranmer, arXiv 2023 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01582)). **Support:** interpretation of paper + benchmark claims. **Confidence:** high that tool exists and is maintained; medium on out-of-box recovery rates on correlated spectral inputs. *Inference: restrict operators (e.g. +,×,÷,exp,log,pow) or search explodes on 64 dims — must distill per-direction.*

3. **Claim:** Concept-bottleneck models (CBMs) distill decisions through human concepts with competitive accuracy and enable intervention. **Sources:** Koh et al., ICML 2020 ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/koh20a/koh20a.pdf)). **Support:** direct evidence — on bird identification and X-ray grading, CBMs matched standard end-to-end accuracy while predicting via interpretable concepts. **Confidence:** high for the accuracy-with-interpretability claim in those domains; medium for spectral-mode concepts (need to define them). Post-hoc variants (post-hoc CBM, label-free CBM, ICLR 2023) extend this to already-trained embeddings — relevant to us.

4. **Claim:** KANs replace MLP weights by learnable 1-D spline activations, then snap splines to symbols — a "closed-form by construction" path. **Sources:** Liu et al., arXiv 2024 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.19756v1); OpenReview). **Support:** interpretation — paper reports higher accuracy/interpretability than MLPs on small science tasks and symbolic recovery (e.g., knot invariants, physics laws). **Confidence:** low–medium: exciting but new, contested on scaling, sensitive to seed/version (v1 2404.19756 vs KAN 2.0 2408.10205). Treat as exploratory, not as established as (1)/(3).

5. **Claim:** Lossless replacements exist but are narrow: (a) Cranmer's symbolic formulas matched or beat the GNN on held-out cosmology error; (b) CBM matched black-box accuracy on two tasks; (c) KD+SR hybrids report SR matching NN with fewer parameters. **Sources:** (a) Cranmer NeurIPS 2020; (b) Koh ICML 2020; (c) Shmuel et al., Neural Computing & Applications 2026 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00521-026-12067-2)). **Support:** direct for (a)/(b) per abstracts; interpretation for (c) (only abstract verified). **Confidence:** medium. *Inference/researcher note: "without loss" always means on a fixed test distribution with a fidelity metric — never universal equivalence.*

## Three concrete distillation proposals for OUR setting

**P1 — Per-direction PySR on functionally-ranked embedding dims (recommended).**
- Target closed form: for top-k dims (k≈3–8): `z_j ≈ f_j(band powers, band ratios)` with operators {+,−,×,÷,sqrt,exp,log,pow}; e.g. rational functions of band energies plus log-ratio tilt terms.
- Method: (i) rank 64 dims by downstream importance (ablate or probe accuracy / PCA variance on mode task); (ii) sample dense (x, z=MLP(x)) pairs incl. augmented band vectors; (iii) run PySR per dim with complexity penalty, pick Pareto knee; (iv) plug formula back as frozen encoder, re-fit only final linear head.
- Success criterion: fidelity R²≥0.95 per kept dim on held-out inputs **and** downstream mode-task drop ≤1% (or ≤ noise floor) with formula swapped in; report full Pareto table, not one equation.
- Evidence grade: **B (moderate–strong)** — directly instantiates Cranmer NeurIPS 2020 + PySR 2023; risk is correlated band features inflating formula complexity.

**P2 — Concept-bottleneck surrogate with named spectral concepts.**
- Target closed form: `bands → concepts → linear mode map`, e.g. concepts = {centroid freq, bandwidth/Q, harmonicity, band-ratio contrasts, temporal stationarity}; final layer strictly linear `logit = W·c + b`.
- Method: compute concepts analytically from spectrum (no learning); train post-hoc linear map concepts→MLP embedding (or concept→label) by distillation; measure completeness/leakage; allow test-time intervention (correct a concept, watch prediction change per Koh et al.).
- Success criterion: concept→embedding R² or concept-only classifier within 1–2% of MLP; intervention monotonicity; each concept has a one-line formula disclosed in paper.
- Evidence grade: **B (moderate–strong)** — Koh ICML 2020 competitive-accuracy precedent; weaker if good concepts unknown — then P2 becomes concept discovery, not distillation.

**P3 — KAN / sparse-additive snap-to-symbol.**
- Target closed form: `z_j ≈ Σ_q g_q(Σ_p φ_{qp}(band_p))` with each φ, g univariate spline subsequently snapped to {x, x², exp, sigmoid, sin}.
- Method: train small KAN to mimic top embedding dims or the downstream logit; prune edges; auto-suggest symbolic snaps; refit constants; compare to MLP head.
- Success criterion: matched accuracy (±1%) with ≥50% edges snapped to named symbols and all surviving terms plotted; any unsnappable residual must be quantified, not hidden.
- Evidence grade: **C (exploratory)** — Liu et al. 2024 promising but immature/contested; use as complement to P1, not sole evidence.

## Contradictions
- KAN "more accurate and interpretable than MLPs" (Liu et al.) vs. community skepticism on scaling/robustness — unresolved; grade accordingly.
- SR+KD 2026 paper claims "more accurate SR without compromising" via distillation, but full text not verified here — do not cite as lossless proof.
- No contradiction found on Cranmer/CBM core claims within verified abstracts.

## Missing evidence
- Did not verify full-text details: Cranmer's dark-matter formula error bars; CBM exact accuracy deltas; KAN snap procedure reproducibility.
- Unknown for our data: intrinsic dimensionality of the 64-dim embedding; band-feature collinearity; whether downstream head is linear (much easier) or deep.
- No direct precedent found for 64-dim spectral MLP → single closed form with zero loss; reduction to k≪64 is assumed necessary but unproven here.
