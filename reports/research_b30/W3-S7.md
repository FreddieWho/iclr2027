# Research: S7 — Real-time application stories for soccer tracking representations

## Summary
Deployable soccer-tracking analytics today runs through analysts post-match, not live autonomous coaching: pitch-control and action-value models inform review and preparation, while surveys show coaches remain data-informed (not data-driven) and adoption is blocked by trust, staffing, and workflow fit. For an intervention-probe mechanism paper, the honest broader-impact claim is diagnostic infrastructure — making representations auditable — not real-time touchline decisions or proven performance gains.

## Findings

1. **Claim:** The credible deployed precedent is offline decision support (pitch control, action values), not live coaching. Spearman et al. built a physics-based pass-probability / pitch-control model from spatio-temporal tracking data to quantify off-ball scoring opportunities (MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference, 2017). Decroos et al. introduced SPADL + VAEP valuing every on-ball action by short-term scoring/conceding impact (ACM SIGKDD, 2019; now the open `socceraction` pipeline). Both are analyst tools for evaluation and recruitment, with no claim of real-time touchline use. **Sources:** [Beyond Expected Goals / Spearman et al. 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327139841_Beyond_Expected_Goals); [Actions Speak Louder than Goals / Decroos et al. KDD 2019](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3292500.3330758). **Support:** direct evidence (papers' stated scope). **Confidence:** high.

2. **Claim:** What coaches actually use is filtered, simple, and mediated by analysts; raw model outputs rarely reach the bench. A worldwide survey of 29 national federations + 32 pro clubs finds underemployment of analytics expertise, resourcing limits, and missing operational taxonomies blocking translation of raw data into coaching knowledge; authors urge a shift from "data-driven" to "data-informed" (Science and Medicine in Football / Taylor & Francis, 2024). A 2026 Frontiers synthesis names an "analytics–practice gap": performance analysis (observed/coded/communicated feedback) vs. sports analytics (models) fail at the handoff when outputs ignore coach workflow and trust. Practitioner surveys likewise rate the coach–analyst relationship as decisive, with analysts consulted but rarely determinative. **Sources:** [Data analytics in the football industry survey, 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24733938.2024.2341837); [Analytics–practice gap, Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1866960/full). **Support:** interpretation (survey + synthesis converge; exact effect sizes not re-verified). **Confidence:** medium-high.

3. **Claim:** Honest broader-impact language for a mechanism paper: claim auditability and misuse-aware limits, not wins or deployment. NeurIPS checklist guidance asks for limits, reproducibility, and societal-impact reflection; the large-scale audit of NeurIPS 2020 statements (Nanayakkara et al., arXiv 2021; Turing summary) found statements often vague, positive-skewed, and low-cost — the lesson is to state concrete non-claims. Rein & Memmert's agenda paper (SpringerPlus, 2016) likewise frames big-data tactics as future opportunity requiring a theoretical model of decision-making, not a solved application. FIFA's EPTS Quality Programme (FIFA, extended to performance testing 2019) is the relevant deployment reality check: even measurement itself needs certification. **Sources:** [NeurIPS Paper Checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist); [AI Ethics Statements analysis, arXiv 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01705); [Rein & Memmert 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40064-016-3108-2); [FIFA EPTS](https://inside.fifa.com/innovation/standards/epts). **Support:** direct evidence for checklist/EPTS existence; interpretation for "how to write it." **Confidence:** high for guidance, medium for framing advice (researcher inference — labelled).

### Three concrete application framings (what / for whom / deliberately NOT claimed)

**A. Post-match diagnostic lens for the analyst room.** What: intervention probes flag which tracked patterns (e.g., off-ball run timing, compactness cues) the representation actually uses, overlaid on video/timeline for the analyst to accept or reject. For whom: club/federation performance analysts preparing review. NOT claimed: no live touchline recommendation, no proof it changes results, no replacement for coach judgment. Evidence grade: Moderate (precedent: pitch-control/VAEP analyst use; gap: no probe-to-coach RCT). Hype risk: calling offline review "real-time coaching."

**B. Training-design and opposition-prep sandbox.** What: counterfactual probes ("what if the weak-side winger tucks in?") generate candidate session constraints or prep clips for staff discussion. For whom: coaching staff + analysts jointly. NOT claimed: not causal ("this will concede 0.x fewer goals"), not validated across teams/leagues, not a selection or substitution policy. Evidence grade: Low–Moderate (supported as direction by Rein & Memmert 2016 agenda; no deployment evidence for probe-based prescription). Hype risk: implying correlation-based probes license interventions on players.

**C. Open audit infrastructure for the research community.** What: probe suite + reporting template others can run on their tracking representations to check whether claimed tactical concepts are really encoded. For whom: sports-analytics researchers and tool builders. NOT claimed: not a product, not certified measurement (cf. FIFA EPTS), no player-level monitoring or surveillance function. Evidence grade: Moderate (strong methods precedent via socceraction/SPADL openness; impact is epistemic, not competitive). Hype risk: presenting a diagnostic as a deployed performance system.

## Contradictions
Vendor pages (e.g., KINEXON, Wisesport) market "real-time tactical insights," while peer-reviewed practitioner literature reports slow, mediated, post-hoc adoption with staffing/trust barriers. Record as marketing-vs-practice contradiction, not resolved. Frontiers 2026 vs. some club case studies (e.g., Chelsea academy GPS load-management) differ in emphasis (physical load is more operationalized than tactical models) — tactical-probe claims inherit the weaker side of this split.

## Missing evidence
No verified RCT or longitudinal study showing intervention-probe explanations improve coaching decisions or match outcomes; no latency/robustness benchmark for live tracking-to-probe inference under stadium conditions; no player/union perspective on continuous representation-based monitoring sourced in this pass.
