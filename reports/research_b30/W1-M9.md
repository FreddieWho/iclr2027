# Research brief M9: Popperian falsification design for geometry-predicts-response (ρ 0.5–0.85)

## Summary
A correlation of Spearman 0.5–0.85 between representational geometry and response is not yet a severe test: probes, RSA-style correlations, and analogy geometry all have a history of passing weak tests and failing controlled ones. The fix is pre-registered kill criteria plus adversarial controls (permutation/control tasks, confound-residualization, held-out-domain transfer). Full kill = fails in-distribution under controls; mere bound = survives in-distribution but shrinks or fails to transfer. We propose 3 kill-experiments below; our already-falsified μ-threshold auxiliary is the template: keep the core, drop the auxiliary.

## 1. Adversarial validation practices (what "severe" means here)
1. **Control tasks + selectivity (probing).** Hewitt & Liang show high probe accuracy proves little unless a control task (same probe, randomized labels/structure) fails: report selectivity = linguistic-task accuracy minus control accuracy; low selectivity = probe memorized, representation claim unsupported. **Direct evidence** (Hewitt & Liang, EMNLP 2019). **Inference:** our geometry→response map needs an identical-pipeline null (shuffled geometry, random embeddings of matched dim) and a pre-registered selectivity gap, not just raw ρ.
2. **Confound-controlled RSA.** Geometry–response correlations can be driven by regional-mean activity, sampling sparsity, or trivial covariates; Dujmović et al. demonstrate contradictory RSA inferences from such pitfalls, and eLife 2025 work shows when measured distances do/don't reflect true geometry. **Inference:** always report partial-ρ after regressing known trivial predictors + permutation nulls, not raw ρ alone.
3. **Pre-registered severe tests.** Mayo's severity: evidence = passing a test that would probably have caught the error if present; Nosek-style pre-registration makes the "would have caught it" checkable (metric, null, n, threshold fixed before seeing data). Recent philosophy literature debates whether pre-registration alone guarantees severity, but agrees it blocks post-hoc threshold-tuning of exactly the kind our μ-threshold episode illustrates. **Interpretation, confidence medium-high.**

## 2. Kill vs bound: outcome pattern table
- **KILLS the core claim** ("geometry predicts response"): fails even in-distribution when (a) true ρ falls inside the permutation/random-embedding null, or (b) partial-ρ after confound removal ≈ 0. No scope rescue — the mapping adds nothing.
- **BOUNDS (refines) the claim:** survives in-distribution controls with reduced effect (e.g., ρ 0.7→0.35) but fails on a new domain/modality, fails causal perturbation, or holds only for a sub-range. Correct response is a scope restriction ("holds for X, not Y"), exactly as happened to word2vec analogies and disentanglement (see §4).
- **Rule:** pre-register which experiment is a kill vs bound test before running; a transfer failure alone bounds universality but does not kill the in-distribution effect unless the claim was explicitly universal.

## 3. Three concrete kill-experiments for OUR claim
Pre-register all: Spearman ρ, two-sided permutation p, n, random seed, and the decision rule below in a timestamped plan. No re-tuning after seeing data.

**K1 — Control-geometry selectivity kill (KILL test).**
Setup: freeze the exact response-prediction pipeline; run (i) 1,000 geometry-label permutations and (ii) 200 random Gaussian embeddings matched in dimension to the true geometry, through the identical pipeline on the same splits. Key reference: Hewitt & Liang, EMNLP 2019 (control tasks/selectivity).
Pre-registered kill threshold: **KILL core claim if true ρ ≤ 95th percentile of the permutation null OR selectivity gap (true ρ − mean random-embedding ρ) < 0.20.** Surviving with gap ≥ 0.2 keeps the claim; report gap, not just p.
Evidence grade: strong method precedent, direct analogue. Confidence high.

**K2 — Held-out-domain transfer bound/kill (BOUND test, kill only if claim is universal).**
Setup: lock model/mapping on discovery data; evaluate once on a pre-named held-out domain (new cohort/modality/site — named before analysis, no model refit). Key reference: Locatello et al., ICML 2019 (unsupervised disentanglement fails without supervision/transfer; large-scale falsification study).
Pre-registered threshold: **BOUND universality to in-distribution if held-out median ρ < 0.30 or sign flips in >1/3 pre-defined strata; KILL only if the registered claim was explicitly "domain-general" and this plus K1 both fail.** Report per-stratum ρ, not pooled-only.
Evidence grade: strong precedent for representation claims collapsing on transfer. Confidence high.

**K3 — Confound-residualization kill (KILL the "geometry adds information" reading).**
Setup: regress response on known trivial predictors first (size/dose/batch/site or task-specified covariates — list fixed at pre-registration), then correlate residual response with geometry distance (partial Spearman); plus a matched linear-baseline comparison. Key references: Allen & Hospedales, ICML 2019 (analogies reduce to PMI/frequency geometry, not abstract relations); Dujmović et al., bioRxiv 2022 / RSA-pitfalls literature.
Pre-registered kill threshold: **KILL "geometry adds info" if partial-ρ < 0.20 or non-significant (permutation p ≥ 0.05) while the raw ρ stays 0.5–0.85 — verdict: epiphenomenon, raw correlation retained only as descriptive.** If partial-ρ stays ≥ 0.3 and significant, claim survives bounded (effect = partial, not raw).
Evidence grade: medium-strong; exact confound list is domain-specific so threshold is a convention. Confidence medium.

## 4. Famous falsified-then-refined representation claims
1. **word2vec linear analogies (king−man+woman≈queen).** Mikolov et al. 2013's striking regularity → Levy & Goldberg, NIPS 2014 (SGNS = factorizing PMI; much "semantics" is co-occurrence statistics) + subsequent analogy-method critiques (3CosAdd favors neighborhood/frequency) → Allen & Hospedales, ICML 2019 (parallelogram explained by PMI structure, conditional). Verdict: killed as "neural nets learn abstract relations," refined to "PMI geometry yields conditional linear regularities." **Interpretation; confidence high.**
2. **Unsupervised disentanglement.** Claim that VAEs discover disentangled factors unsupervised → Locatello et al., ICML 2019, large-scale study: unsupervised disentanglement impossible without inductive biases/supervision (seeds/hyperparams dominate metrics). Verdict: killed in pure form, refined to weakly-/semi-supervised disentanglement. **Direct evidence; confidence high.**
3. **BERT syntax probes.** Hewitt & Manning, NAACL 2019 structural probe finds syntax in BERT → Hewitt & Liang, EMNLP 2019 control-task critique (probes can memorize; demand selectivity) → refined practice: low-capacity probes + controls + limited syntax claim. Verdict: bounded, not killed. **Direct evidence; confidence high.**

## Contradictions
None on the core methods cited. Open debate: Synthese 2025 argues pre-registration does not by itself improve severity evaluation under Popper when deviations are allowed — so our kill criteria must also fix the analysis pipeline, not just the threshold.
