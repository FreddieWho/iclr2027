# Research: S2 — Figure/table design for maximum reviewer impact

## Summary
Reviewers are persuaded by regime maps over raw curves, compact falsification tables over prose, and mechanism panels that tie a performance effect to a measurable signature. For a paper whose core evidence is negative (15-run collapse, epsilon validity limits, mu-threshold null), the winning move is to reframe nulls as boundaries: one phase-diagram figure, one claim×test ledger table, and one head-to-head + spectra mechanism panel. Honest null reporting — full outcomes, CIs, prespecified thresholds — increases rather than decreases trust.

## Findings

1. **Claim:** Log–log scaling curves and phase/regime diagrams are the highest-persuasion plot types for "when does X hold" claims; they convert many runs into one boundary. **Sources:** [Kaplan et al., Scaling Laws (NeurIPS 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361); [Belkin et al., double descent (PNAS 2019)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1903070116); [Power et al., Grokking (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02177). **Support:** interpretation — each paper's most-cited figure is a single regime/transition plot, not a table of numbers. **Confidence:** high.
   Researcher inference: our epsilon-sweep + support-scaling assets map directly onto this pattern; a 2D validity map will read as a discovery, not a failure report.

2. **Claim:** Great mechanism figures use a 3-part left-to-right grammar: schematic of hypothesized mechanism → measurable signature → causal test (intervention/ablation). **Sources:** [Attention Is All You Need, Fig. 1 (NeurIPS 2017)](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/7181-attention-is-all-you-need.pdf); [Olah et al., Circuits / Zoom In (Distill 2020)](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/zoom-in/); [LabFig guide on venue style anchors (2026)](https://labfig.com/blog/ai-figures-for-machine-learning-papers). **Support:** direct evidence for Transformer schematic convention; interpretation for the grammar claim. **Confidence:** medium.
   Researcher inference: our singular-spectra panel should be the "signature" middle slot, flanked by a minimal method cartoon and the mu-threshold/head-to-head causal slot.

3. **Claim:** Compact "claim × test × prespecified threshold × outcome × verdict" tables persuade faster than prose ablations and blunt the "hidden null" suspicion. **Sources:** [NeurIPS 2026 Reviewer Guidelines (clarity, ablations)](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines); [ICLR 2026 Reviewer Guide](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuide). **Support:** interpretation — both venues instruct reviewers to weight clarity of ablations and evidence for claims. **Confidence:** medium.

4. **Claim:** Honest negative-result framing that works: report the full outcome distribution with uncertainty, state the prespecified bar, and name the boundary condition — never bury or average away the null. **Sources:** [Position: Embracing Negative Results in ML (ICML 2024)](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/35063); [How to present null/negative results (ConferenceLists guide)](https://www.conferencelists.com/how-to-report-null-or-negative-results-at-a-scientific-conference/); [Reflqt: Case for Negative Results (2026)](https://www.reflqt.com/blog/case-for-negative-results/). **Support:** direct evidence (position paper + guides converge on full reporting + prespecification). **Confidence:** high.

5. **Claim:** Data-ink discipline (no chartjunk, direct labels, uncertainty always shown, honest aspect/log scales) measurably aids comprehension and trust. **Sources:** [Tufte, Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983)](https://users.wpi.edu/~jdp/ma2611_18D/lecture_notes/graphics/Tufte/Tufte_on_graphics.pdf); [Chengshu Zhao, Good Figures for Papers (2025)](https://chengshuaizhao0.github.io/blog/2025/figures-for-papers/). **Support:** interpretation — principles literature, not RCTs on reviewers. **Confidence:** medium.

## Concrete specs for OUR paper (max 3)

**Spec A — Fig. 1/2: Validity-regime map (the paper's visual anchor).**
Content: x-axis = epsilon sweep (log scale), y-axis = support scale; each cell = one run outcome (pass/fail vs. prespecified bar); overlay the empirical boundary curve + shaded "holds / collapses" regions; annotate the 15-run collapse cluster as points inside the fail region. Layout: single panel, ~column-width, direct region labels ("valid", "collapse"), no legend hunt; caption states the boundary in one sentence. Why it persuades: turns 15 negatives + sweeps into one positive discovery (a law-like boundary), mimicking the Belkin/Kaplan/Grokking pattern reviewers already trust; kills "cherry-picked epsilon" objections at a glance.

**Spec B — Table 1: Falsification ledger (15-run collapse table, compressed).**
Content: rows = claims/tests (one row per falsification attempt, grouped: collapse series / mu-null / head-to-head), columns = N runs | prespecified bar | observed effect ± CI | verdict (✗/✓ icon + one number). Layout: full-width, grouped rules, bold only the verdict column; appendix holds the full 15-row expansion. Why it persuades: a reviewer verifies rigor in 30 seconds; prespecified bars + full reporting signal honesty per ICML-2024 negative-results norms; keeps the main text from drowning in null prose.

**Spec C — Fig. 3: Mechanism panel (head-to-head + singular spectra + mu-null inset).**
Content: left = support-scaling curves as small multiples with CI bands and a delta-vs-strongest-baseline forest strip; right = overlaid singular spectra (success vs. collapsed runs) showing the spectral signature; bottom-right inset = mu-threshold null (flat line at chance with CI, prespecified bar marked). Layout: two columns + inset, shared y-zero baseline, one accent color reserved for "our method," gray for baselines; schematic arrow from spectra to collapse. Why it persuades: executes the schematic→signature→causal-test grammar (cf. Circuits/Distill 2020); ties performance to mechanism so the null reads as explained, not merely observed.

## Contradictions
None found on core advice. Tension: some style guides urge minimizing negative-result space to "not lose the reader," while the ICML-2024 position line urges full reporting — resolved by Spec B's compress-in-main/expand-in-appendix split.

## Missing evidence
No RCT evidence that any specific plot type raises ICLR acceptance odds; persuasion claims rest on citation/adoption patterns and reviewer-guideline interpretation. Exact N/CI formatting must come from our own run logs, not yet inspected.
