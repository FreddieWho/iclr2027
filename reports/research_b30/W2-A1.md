# Research: Architecture transferability — Phase-GAT → EGNN / Set Transformer (A1)

## Summary
Literature predicts qualitative transfer of geometry-linked mechanisms from a small 2-layer GAT with mean pooling to EGNN and Set Transformer, but with shifted effect sizes: E(2)-equivariance should preserve or strengthen geometry–response links by removing rotation/translation nuisance variance, while Set Transformer's learned PMA pooling and removal of kNN edges should change margin/thinning magnitudes. No head-to-head study tests these exact mechanisms, so the Limitations sentence must state generality is untested and name EGNN/Set Transformer as the required controls.

## Findings

**1. Claim: Geometry-predicts-response SHOULD HOLD under both controls, and likely strengthens under EGNN.**
**Sources:** Satorras et al. (ICML 2021); Fuchs et al. (NeurIPS 2020); Liao & Smidt (ICLR 2023 Spotlight).
**Support:** direct evidence + researcher inference. Satorras et al. show EGNN (messages from relative squared distances, coordinate updates) cuts N-body MSE ~32% vs. second-best non-equivariant GNN and matches QM9 baselines — i.e., invariant-message passing preserves the information a GAT uses while improving geometric generalization. Fuchs et al.'s SE(3)-Transformer and Liao & Smidt's Equiformer show attention and equivariance compose (equivariant dot-product attention; Equiformer beats EGNN/SE(3)-Transformer on QM9/OC20/MD17), so an attention-driven geometric signal is not an artifact of non-equivariance. **Inference:** if the task has approximate rotation/translation symmetry, EGNN removes nuisance orientation variance, so embedding-geometry ↔ response correlation should persist or rise; if the task has no such symmetry, EGNN reduces to an invariant MPNN and the finding reduces to a same-class replication. **Confidence:** medium. **Caveat:** none of these benchmarks use 20-node kNN graphs with 2-layer ~140k-param encoders, so scale/depth transfer is inferred, not shown.

**2. Claim: Decoupled-training gains SHOULD HOLD qualitatively; margin thinning under auxiliaries MIGHT BREAK quantitatively (direction replicates, threshold/magnitude shifts), especially under Set Transformer.**
**Sources:** Lee et al. (ICML 2019); Zaheer et al. (NeurIPS 2017); Wagstaff et al. (JMLR 2022).
**Support:** direct evidence + researcher inference. Lee et al. show Set Transformer (stacked ISAB encoder + PMA pooling with trainable seed vectors) beats Deep Sets/mean-pooling baselines on counting, anomaly detection, and point-cloud tasks — the gain comes specifically from pairwise attention plus learned pooling, not local graph structure. Zaheer et al. prove sum/mean-decomposable universality, but Wagstaff et al. prove it requires latent dim scaling with set size — mean pooling (as in team-mean) is the bottleneck Set Transformer is designed to remove. **Inference:** a decoupled-training gain (optimization-level claim) does not depend on encoder symmetry and should survive; but any margin value or "thinning" threshold measured under mean pooling is pooling-dependent — PMA reweights members, changing class-margin geometry even when rank order is preserved. Dropping kNN edges (Set Transformer sees a set, not a graph) further removes the 2-hop receptive-field constraint, so local-geometry-dependent margins may not reproduce at the same numeric value. **Confidence:** medium for decoupled-gains transfer; low–medium for exact margin numbers transferring (predict shift, not sign flip).

**3. Claim: The Limitations sentence MUST say architecture generality is untested and name both controls, because GAT-vs-equivariant literature shows rank order is task/data-scale dependent and no study tests these mechanisms at this scale.**
**Sources:** Veličković et al. (ICLR 2018); Forestano et al. (Logistics/ML4Sci 2024, arXiv:2311.18672); Satorras et al. (ICML 2021).
**Support:** interpretation. Veličković et al. establish GAT as anisotropic (learned neighbor weights) vs. EGNN's distance-conditioned isotropic messages — attention distributions differ by construction, so identical margins are not predicted. Forestano et al.'s fair classical-GNN vs. EGNN comparison finds equivariant advantage is conditional on symmetry-aligned data, not universal. Combined with (1)–(2), the honest statement is: mechanisms are demonstrated only for one small non-equivariant attention encoder with mean pooling on 20-node kNN graphs. **Confidence:** high that this sentence is required; low that any numeric threshold generalizes. Suggested wording: *"All mechanism claims are established with a ~140k-parameter 2-layer Phase-GAT with team-mean pooling on 20-node kNN graphs; transfer to E(2)-equivariant (e.g., EGNN) and set-attention (e.g., Set Transformer with PMA) encoders is untested, and margin/threshold magnitudes should not be assumed to generalize."*

## Contradictions
None found on the core symmetry/expressivity facts. One tension recorded: equivariant literature (Satorras 2021; Equiformer 2023) reports equivariance *improves* sample efficiency, while pooling theory (Wagstaff 2022) implies small-latent mean-pooling models *underfit* set functions — these push in opposite directions on whether a 140k model understates or overstates effects. Resolution adopted: treat EGNN as variance-reducer (strengthens geometry signal) and PMA as capacity-adder (shifts margins); do not claim a uniform direction for all three mechanisms.

## Missing evidence
- No published head-to-head of GAT vs. EGNN vs. Set Transformer on the same 20-node kNN, 2-layer, ~140k-param budget measuring representation geometry, decoupled-training deltas, or auxiliary-induced margin change.
- No test of whether the kNN graph construction itself (vs. full-set attention) drives the geometry–response link.
- Freshness: key sources are 2017–2023; no 2024–2026 study found that overturns them, but a targeted 2025–2026 equivariant-transformer benchmark check would strengthen the brief.
