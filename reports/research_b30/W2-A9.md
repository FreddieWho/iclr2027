# Research: A9 — Capacity scaling laws for our mechanism (140–190k params, ~23k dev-only snapshots)

## Summary
At ~23k fixed snapshots, literature predicts width scaling gives small smooth gains then a flat plateau, not a breakthrough; depth scaling risks qualitative geometry damage (rank collapse). The right pilot is a cheap width-first sweep at fixed data/compute with geometry probes, not a score-only ladder. No training was run for this brief — design only.

## Findings

### 1. Small-GNN scaling: expect sub-linear gains then data-ceiling plateau
**Claim:** With data fixed at ~23k snapshots, test improvement vs params follows an approximate power law only while data is not binding, then flattens to an irreducible floor; Chinchilla logic says our regime (~23k samples vs 140–190k params) is already parameter-heavy, so 2–8× bigger encoders should add little. **Sources:** [Kaplan et al. (arXiv 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361); [Hoffmann et al. (NeurIPS 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556). **Support:** interpretation (both papers study Transformers/LLMs, not small GNNs; applying their data-bottleneck argument to our N/D ratio is researcher inference). **Confidence:** medium.
- Direct evidence: Kaplan fits L(N,D) with separate model-size and data-size power laws plus irreducible loss; Hoffmann shows many large models were data-starved and smaller models trained on more data win at fixed compute.

### 2. Geometry can change qualitatively even when scores barely move
**Claim:** Increasing depth/width is not geometry-neutral: theory and measurement show deep message-passing drives node embeddings toward a low-dimensional subspace (rank collapse / over-smoothing / over-correlation), so a bigger encoder can score ±flat while its representation (effective rank, pairwise distances, cluster structure) degrades. Width-first scaling is safer than depth-first. **Sources:** [Roth et al. (PMLR v231, 2024)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v231/roth24a/roth24a.pdf); [Liu et al. graph-scaling overview (PMLR v269, 2025)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v269/liu25c.html). **Support:** direct evidence for the rank-collapse mechanism (Roth proves it for standard aggregations); interpretation that it applies to our encoder (depends on our aggregation/norm, unverified). **Confidence:** medium.
- Researcher inference: monitor geometry directly (below); do not infer "same mechanism, bigger" from flat scores.

### 3. Graph scaling laws exist but use edges as data and saturate early
**Claim:** The graph-specific scaling study finds power-law-like improvement in both model size and data size (reformulating data size as edge count, extended to node classification/link prediction), intended to guide graph foundation models — but absolute gains are modest and domain-sensitive, so our 47-graph/single-lineage-style dev set should not be expected to yield LLM-like straight log-log lines over decades. **Source:** [Neural Scaling Laws on Graphs (arXiv 2024; PMLR v269, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02054v2). **Support:** interpretation (result summaries; full exponent tables not re-verified here). **Confidence:** low–medium.

## Pilot design (no training; ready to run)

**Principle:** vary capacity with data, optimizer, epochs, and splits fixed; each config × 3 seeds; all dev-only on the same ~23k snapshots.

**Configs (exact):** keep architecture family identical; scale by hidden width first, then one depth probe:
- W-sweep at fixed depth (= current depth): target params ≈ 35k (0.25×), 70k (0.5×), 140k (current low anchor), 190k (current high anchor), 380k (2×), 760k (4×), 1.5M (8×). Achieve by scaling hidden dim by ~√k (params ∝ d² per layer); e.g. if base d=128 → ~64, 90, 128, 150, 181, 256, 362. Round to multiples of 8/16.
- D-probe at fixed width (= current width): +2 and +4 message-passing layers with residual/norm unchanged, only at 140k-width and 380k-width (2×2 = 4 runs) to isolate depth-induced collapse.
- Controls: identical train/dev split, same epochs and batch size, same LR schedule (plus one LR-retune run at 760k only to test schedule-confound); log param count excluding embeddings per Kaplan convention.
- Total: 7 width × 3 seeds = 21 runs + 4 depth × 3 = 12 runs + 1 LR check × 3 = 3 runs → 36 runs max; minimal viable pilot = 5 widths (35k/140k/190k/380k/760k) × 3 = 15 runs.

**Measure (scores + geometry):** (a) dev loss/task score; (b) effective rank / singular spectrum of snapshot embeddings; (c) mean pairwise cosine + neighbor-overlap@k vs base encoder; (d) linear probe of known structure; (e) CKA between sizes; (f) train–dev gap (overfit detector).

**Curve shapes → meaning:**
- Log-log straight line on (a) = power-law scaling holds; extrapolate cautiously one doubling only.
- Plateau on (a) + flat (b–e) = data ceiling (Prediction 1 confirmed); stop scaling, buy data/diversity instead.
- Inverted-U on (a) (big models worse) + widening train–dev gap = overfitting/optimization penalty at fixed data; confirms small-data regime.
- Flat (a) + falling effective rank / collapsing pairwise distances / CKA break at some size = qualitative geometry change without score signal (Prediction 2 confirmed); reject that config even if score ties.
- Depth-probe degrades faster than width-matched control = depth-specific collapse, not parameter count per se.

**Cost estimate:** Current scale (~10⁵ params, 10⁴ snapshots) is tiny by scaling-law standards: each run is minutes to ~1 GPU-hour on one consumer GPU. Minimal pilot (15 runs) ≈ 8–15 GPU-hours; full 36-run design ≈ 20–40 GPU-hours, trivially parallelizable. Dominant cost is analysis (geometry probes), not training.

## Predictions (≤3, from literature)
1. **2–4× width at fixed 23k → ≤ small gain then flat** (Chinchilla data-bottleneck logic; grade: B/medium).
2. **Depth probe collapses rank before width probe does** (Roth rank-collapse theory; grade: B/medium, mechanism-direct).
3. **No emergent geometry jump in 35k→1.5M range; best case is smoother manifolds, not new structure** (graph-scaling + small-model priors; grade: C/low-medium).

## Contradictions
LLM scaling optimism ("bigger is predictably better") vs GNN-specific collapse literature ("deeper/wider can homogenize embeddings") — resolved by treating score curves and geometry curves as separate endpoints. Kaplan (grow model fast) vs Chinchilla (grow data with model) partially contradict on allocation; Chinchilla's data-side correction is the relevant one for our fixed-23k regime.

## Missing evidence
Exact exponents for small-GNN encoder-only scaling at 10⁴-sample scale (no directly matching study found); our aggregation/norm's rank-collapse susceptibility; whether our dev metric follows cross-entropy-like power laws at all; compute-optimal N/D ratio for our loss.
