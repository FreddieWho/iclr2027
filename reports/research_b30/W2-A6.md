# Research A6: Hierarchical / Multi-Scale Pooling Beyond Team-Mean

## Summary
Current pooling — mean over 10 outfield players per team (+ energy terms in v4+) — is a strong, permutation-invariant baseline for fixed-size sets, but it discards star-player dominance and sub-group structure (e.g., back-line vs attack). For n=10 fixed sets, evidence favors **cheap attention readouts and multi-layer/multi-stat readouts** over heavy DiffPool/top-k coarsening, which was designed for large graphs with community structure and adds cost and fragility at n=10.

## Ranked upgrades (gain vs cost)

### 1. Learnable attention readout replacing/augmenting team-mean (HIGHEST gain/cost)
**What:** `z_team = Σ_i α_i h_i`, α from a small gate (e.g., `softmax(MLP(h_i))` or single-query PMA block), kept permutation-invariant; concatenate with existing mean and energy terms: `[mean; att; max; energy]`. Per-team, ~1–2k params.
**Why first:** n=10 team outcome is typically dominated by 1–3 actors (ball carrier, presser, keeper-adjacent). Mean dilutes them; attention recovers them with negligible compute and no graph-coarsening hyperparameters.
**Evidence:**
- Set Transformer (Lee et al., ICML 2019) proves attention pooling (PMA) is a universal approximator for permutation-invariant functions and beats plain mean/sum on set tasks where element importance is heterogeneous (e.g., point-cloud and MIL benchmarks). **Grade: A (direct theory + benchmarks). Support: direct evidence. Confidence: high.**
- Deep Sets (Zaheer et al., NeurIPS 2017) establishes `ρ(Σ φ(x_i))` universality but notes sum/mean forces all interaction modeling into φ; attention pooling empirically dominates when interactions matter. **Grade: A. Confidence: high.**
- Sports-direct: GoalNet soccer GNN (arXiv 2025) uses global *mean* pooling over player nodes for xT-change prediction — works, but leaves star-player weighting on the table; football pass-receiver GNNs and NFL play GNNs that add attention over players report the receiver/carrier attention mass drives accuracy. Stropnik & Šubelj football-pass GNN ablation (NetSci 2025, StatsBomb open data) finds GNN edge/player structure beats feature-only baselines. **Grade: B (sports-adjacent, not same target). Confidence: medium.**
**Cost:** trivial (one MLP + softmax per team); no change to message passing; easy ablation (α-entropy + mean|att weight).
**Risk:** attention collapse to mean or to one player — regularize with entropy penalty and keep mean in concat.

### 2. Multi-scale / JK-style readout: pool each GNN layer, then fuse (SECOND)
**What:** read out after *each* message-passing layer (layer-0 = raw/embedded players, layer-1 = local units, layer-2 = team context) and fuse via concat + linear or LSTM-attention: `z = FUSE([pool(H⁰), pool(H¹), pool(H²)])`; pool can be mean or the attention head from #1.
**Why second:** gives hierarchy without coarsening: early layers preserve positions/roles, deep layers carry team context. One ablation tells you whether depth helps at all.
**Evidence:**
- JK-Net (Xu et al., ICML 2018) shows layer-wise "jump" aggregation with concat/max/LSTM-attention adapts per-node neighborhood range; JK-LSTM beats GraphSAGE/GAT substantially on PPI (+0.10 micro-F1 class of gain) and on Reddit/Citeseer/Cora. **Grade: A. Support: direct. Confidence: high** for mechanism, medium for transfer to n=10 sports sets.
- ASAP (Ranjan et al., AAAI 2020) and pooling surveys confirm hierarchical/global ablations: hierarchical variants beat flat global pooling on 5/5 graph-classification tasks when graphs have substructure. **Grade: B+. Confidence: medium.**
- Caveat (researcher inference): gains in citations come from variable-size graphs with 30–500 nodes; at n=10 with 1–2 GNN layers the scale separation is small, so expect modest (+0.5–2%) not JK-paper-scale gains. **Confidence: medium.**
**Cost:** low (K extra readouts + one fusion layer); no cluster-count tuning.

### 3. Explicit coarsening (DiffPool / SAGPool / Top-k) — NOT RECOMMENDED at n=10 (THIRD / do-last)
**What:** DiffPool learns soft cluster assignment S (nodes→K clusters) and coarsens; SAGPool/Top-k scores nodes and drops low-score nodes hierarchically.
**Evidence:**
- DiffPool (Ying et al., NeurIPS 2018): +5–6% mean accuracy over GraphSAGE global-mean baselines across ENZYMES/D&D/COLLAB/REDDIT/MUTAG (e.g., S2V 61.1→65.3 ENZYMES, 78.9→82.1 D&D with 2 DiffPool layers); SOTA on 4/5 sets. **Grade: A for large structured graphs. Confidence: high.**
- SAGPool (Lee et al., ICML 2019): beats DiffPool/TopK/SortPool/Set2Set on most graph-classification sets with fewer params (self-attention scoring, O(|E|)); Graph U-Nets Top-k (Gao & Ji, ICML 2019 / TPAMI 2022) similar. **Grade: A-minus. Confidence: high.**
- Negative transfer evidence: Quasi-CliquePool benchmark review (GMLR 2023) notes Top-k/SAGPool *fail on ENZYMES* (few examples → overfit/drop critical nodes); DiffPool needs pre-set max-clusters per layer and auxiliary link-prediction/entropy losses, sensitive to K. Dropping nodes is conceptually wrong for football: all 10 outfield players constrain space. **Grade: B. Confidence: medium-high.**
**Cost:** high (DiffPool adds O(n²) assignment + 2 losses + K tuning; SAGPool adds ratio/dropout tuning + information loss). Expected gain at n=10: ~0 to negative. Only revisit if teams are modeled as 22-node match graphs with clear sub-communities.

## When does finer pooling beat mean? (rule)
Finer pooling beats mean when (a) node importance is skewed (stars), (b) graphs have multi-scale community structure, (c) enough data to fit extra pooling params. (a) holds in football → do #1. (b) barely holds at n=10 → skip #3. (c) argues for #1/#2 (100s of params) over DiffPool (10⁴s).

## Contradictions
None on core claims; scope disagreement: DiffPool paper claims general pooling benefit, but follow-ups (ASAP, Quasi-CliquePool reviews) show top-k family overfits small datasets — consistent with our n=10 caution.

## Missing evidence
No published ablation of DiffPool/SAGPool specifically on 10-player football team-graphs vs mean; sports GNN papers (GoalNet, PassAI/Xenopoulos & Silva IEEE Access 2025) report GNN-vs-CNN gaps, not pooling-vs-pooling gaps.
