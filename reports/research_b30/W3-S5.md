# Research: S5 — Rebuttal Prehearsal: 10 Most Likely Reviewer Objections + Ready Answers

## Summary
The most likely objections are circularity, single-domain/n=8, and incrementality — all partially defused by frozen evidence: decoupled (held-out) training, a collapse/negative-result series, and external confirmation. The weakest defensive points are small effect sizes and seed variance, which must be conceded and reframed rather than over-claimed.

## Findings

**1. "This is circular — geometry predicts response because response defined the geometry." (likelihood #1)**
**Claim:** Pre-registered fear; our answer: training and evaluation are decoupled — geometric predictors are fixed on training split(s)/stages before any response labels in the test split are touched, so the prediction is genuinely out-of-sample. **Sources:** ICLR review taxonomy (most frequent: overlap/weak baselines/few datasets — arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15462); rebuttal best practice (zhiyichin.github.io/blog/posts/0-ml-rebuttal). **Support:** interpretation (standard taxonomy) + researcher inference (our decoupling design answers it). **Confidence:** high that objection comes; medium that our decoupling fully satisfies without seeing frozen split protocol. **Evidence grade: B+** — strong by design, needs exact split description + one-sentence rebuttal quote ready.

**2. "Single domain — does this generalize beyond your one setting?" (likelihood #2)**
**Claim:** Answer: external confirmation exists — the geometry→response relationship replicates directionally on held-out/external data, so the claim is not single-cohort overfit. Concede scope (one external setting, not a survey) and state the boundary explicitly. **Sources:** same taxonomy source above. **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** high objection recurs; medium on strength (depends on how external the external set is). **Evidence grade: B** — directional replication defuses "n=1 domain" but not "narrow generality"; do not oversell.

**3. "n=8 — the sample is too small for the claims." (likelihood #3)**
**Claim:** Answer: agree n is small at the unit that matters; report uncertainty honestly (wide intervals / descriptive framing), and lean on converging lines (geometry effect + decoupled gains + collapse series all point same way) rather than a single p-value. Offer exactly what n=8 can and cannot support. **Sources:** rebuttal checklist (github.com/mayanknagda/ml-rebuttal-checklist). **Support:** inference. **Confidence:** high. **Evidence grade: C+** — convergence mitigates but never cures n=8; framing must be modest.

**4. "Incremental — margin analysis / geometry is just another metric." (likelihood #4)**
**Claim:** Answer: the contribution is the *instrument + decision rule* (geometry predicts response; margin costs quantify the price of ignoring it), not a SOTA chase; decoupled-training gains and the collapse series (where removing the geometric component destroys the effect) show necessity, not decoration. **Sources:** ICLR novelty/contribution failure modes (arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15462). **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** medium-high. **Evidence grade: B** — strong if collapse ablation is clean; weak if framed as performance paper.

**5. "Effects are small / within noise." (likelihood #5)**
**Claim:** Answer: concede raw magnitudes are modest; rebut on *consistency and cost*: effects replicate across splits/external data, and margin-cost analysis shows small geometric shifts carry disproportionate response costs — the practical significance argument. Do not claim large effects. **Sources:** variance-in-benchmarks paper (openreview.net/pdf/c202b5fbccf26e908e76cc96f53580b799f88216.pdf). **Support:** inference. **Confidence:** medium. **Evidence grade: C+** — honest concession + cost framing is the only credible move.

**6. "Missing baseline — where is [obvious comparator]?" (likelihood #6)**
**Claim:** Answer: name the baselines actually run (frozen set), report the head-to-head deltas, and for any requested-but-unrun baseline explain why it is out of scope or offer it as rebuttal-period addition if cheap; never bluff an unrun number. **Sources:** taxonomy (insufficient/weak baselines = top complaint). **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** high that asked; low-medium on current coverage without frozen table. **Evidence grade: B− (pending)** — verify frozen baseline list before submission; this is the easiest reviewer kill if incomplete.

**7. "Seed/variance not reported — results could be luck." (likelihood #7)**
**Claim:** Answer: report seed-variance evidence as frozen (multi-seed spread / intervals), acknowledge remaining variance, and note the collapse series is robust across seeds (effect disappears on ablation regardless of seed). If only single-seed exists for some panel, label it and downgrade the claim. **Sources:** benchmark-variance paper above; rebuttal checklist. **Support:** interpretation + inference. **Confidence:** medium. **Evidence grade: C+ to B** — grade hinges on whether multi-seed table is actually frozen; check before promising.

**8. "Novelty of instrument — is the geometric measure itself new, and is it validated?" (likelihood #8)**
**Claim:** Answer: separate instrument validation (geometry is stable/measurable, decoupled from labels) from discovery claim (it predicts response); cite the decoupled-training and external-confirmation legs as instrument validation, and concede prior art on related geometric measures with a crisp differentiation sentence. **Sources:** ICLR novelty guidance (iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuide). **Support:** inference. **Confidence:** medium. **Evidence grade: B−** — needs one prior-art paragraph; do not claim instrument novelty if it is repurposed.

**9. "Negative results hidden — file-drawer / cherry-picked conditions." (likelihood #9)**
**Claim:** Answer: point to the collapse series as pre-committed negative evidence — conditions under which the effect vanishes are reported (not buried), which is precisely anti-cherry-picking; list them plainly in one table/paragraph. **Sources:** "Refutations and Critiques" position paper (arxiv.org/pdf/2506.19882); CACM rebuttal strategy (cacm.acm.org/opinion/rebuttal-how-to...). **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** medium. **Evidence grade: B+** — our strongest honesty signal; keep collapse conditions prominent, not appendix-buried.

**10. "Compute scale / reproducibility insufficient." (likelihood #10)**
**Claim:** Answer: state exact frozen compute (runs × seeds × splits), release code/configs/seeds, and argue scale is fit-for-claim (small-n study needs transparency, not FLOPs); invite reproduction on the external set. Least likely to be fatal but cheapest to fix — do it before submission. **Sources:** taxonomy (reproducibility issues); rebuttal checklist. **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** medium-low as a reject reason. **Evidence grade: B** — procedural, fully fixable.

## Contradictions
None found in the objection taxonomy itself; tension noted: conceding small-n/small-effects (honest, recommended by rebuttal guides) conflicts with selling a strong contribution — resolution is to sell the *instrument + decision rule + negative-result discipline* rather than magnitude. Sources agree baselines and multi-dataset evidence are the top reject drivers.
