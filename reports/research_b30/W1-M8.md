# Research: M8 — Scaling laws for interaction/coupling terms in multi-entity representations

## Summary
Our coupling signal (full/off-diagonal minus diag-only information, rising 0 → +0.20–0.27 over 4 support sizes × 60 snapshots) is best read as a **truncated many-body/interaction-order expansion in coalition size**: pairwise-plus-higher terms grow with support size but must saturate. Physics (cluster/ACE expansions) and ML (higher-order GNNs, Shapley interactions) both predict diminishing incremental gains per added entity and combinatorial cost blowup — so license only local/saturating extrapolation, validated by hold-one-support-out fits. Three concrete fits below implement this.

## Findings

**1. Claim: Many-body / cluster expansions predict convergent-but-slow interaction corrections; higher body orders give diminishing returns and need tighter precision.** **Sources:** [Drautz PRB 2019 (ACE)](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevB.99.014104); [cMBE arXiv 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10548); [ACE body-order convergence 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16125v1). **Support:** interpretation (analogy). **Confidence:** medium.
Explanation: MBE writes total energy as 1-body + 2-body + 3-body + … corrections; electron-correlation MBE converges faster from a good mean-field reference (cluster MBE), and linear ACE studies explicitly test truncation at body orders K=2,3,4 with convergence in effective interactions. *Researcher inference:* our "diag-only" ≈ 1-body/separable reference; "coupling gap" ≈ sum of ≥2-body corrections. Expectation transferred: gap > 0, monotone in coalition size, increments shrink unless strong collective mode appears. Caveat: physics convergence theorems assume locality/decay; our information metric has no such guarantee, so this is a modeling prior, not a theorem.

**2. Claim: In ML, explicit higher-order interactions buy expressivity at steep combinatorial cost, and empirical motif/HOGNN work shows higher-order counts grow but saturate in utility.** **Sources:** [MACE NeurIPS 2022](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=YPpSngE-ZU); [Subgraph/motif GNN counting 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15709v1); [Shapley Interaction Index approximation 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-026-07062-6). **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** medium.
Explanation: MACE builds higher-body-order equivariant messages (beyond pairwise) for atomistic systems; k-WL/subgraph-GNN hierarchy results show each expressivity rung costs combinatorics; SII literature defines interaction as discrete-derivative surplus of a coalition over its parts — exactly our "full minus diagonal" logic. *Researcher inference:* predict (a) coupling gap scales roughly with number of pairs/interacting subsets at small n, then bends sublinear as redundancy/oversmoothing-style saturation sets in; (b) report cost alongside fit (subsets ~ O(n^k)).

**3. Claim: For functional-form fitting with n=4 support sizes, only 2-parameter monotone-saturating or single-exponent forms are identifiable; extrapolation must be hold-out-validated (BNSL discipline).** **Sources:** [Kaplan et al. NeurIPS 2020 power-law scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361); [Hoffmann et al. 2022 Chinchilla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556); [Caballero et al. ICLR 2023 Broken Neural Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14891). **Support:** direct evidence for method, interpretation for our data. **Confidence:** high for method, medium for which form wins.
Explanation: Kaplan/Chinchilla establish power-law-plus-irreducible-loss forms; Caballero et al. show smoothly broken power laws extrapolate far better (∼0.86× RMS log-error of prior SOTA) and that naive power laws mis-extrapolate across breaks — directly relevant with only 4 x-points where a "break"/ceiling is plausible.

### Three concrete scaling analyses for OUR data (4 supports × 60 snapshots)

**A1 — Saturating vs. linear shootout (licensed: interpolation + one-step-out).** Fit per-snapshot coupling gap g(n): (i) linear g=a+bn; (ii) saturating g=L(1−e^(−n/τ)) or Michaelis–Menten g=An/(B+n) (2 params each). Compare by leave-one-support-out RMSE + AICc pooled over 60 snapshots (hierarchical: snapshot random effect). *Decision rule:* if saturating wins consistently, cap all claims at asymptote L with CI; linear extrapolation beyond max support is NOT licensed. *Key ref:* Caballero et al., ICLR 2023. *Cost:* minutes.

**A2 — Extensivity / per-interaction normalization (licensed: mechanistic claim about redundancy).** Test whether g(n) is extensive in pair count: plot g(n)/C(n,2) and g(n)/n vs n. Flat g/C(n,2) ⇒ pairwise-additive regime (each pair contributes ~const); declining ⇒ redundancy/saturation; rising ⇒ genuine higher-order synergy. Fit g(n)=a·n^b (single exponent): b≈2 supports pairwise-additive; b<1 supports saturating; b>2 flags higher-order emergence (requires snapshot-consistency check). *Key refs:* Drautz, PRB 2019 (body-order decomposition); Hoffmann et al., 2022 (power-law + constant form). *Licensed extrapolation:* none beyond range — this analysis diagnoses mechanism, not future values.

**A3 — BNSL-style hold-largest-out extrapolation audit (licensed: quantified near-extrapolation only).** Fit candidate forms on 3 smallest supports, predict the held-out largest; report log-RMSE and coverage. Only if saturating/BNSL form predicts held-out within CI across snapshots is +1-support extrapolation licensed *with interval*; far extrapolation (2×+ support) never licensed on n=4. *Key refs:* Caballero et al., ICLR 2023; Kaplan et al., NeurIPS 2020. *Evidence grade:* this audit itself is the grade — publish its error, not just the fit.

## Contradictions
- Power-law scaling literature (Kaplan vs. Chinchilla) disagrees on exponents/allocation under fixed compute; both agree on power-law + irreducible-loss shape but not coefficients — so importing any specific exponent to our setting is unjustified. Sources: Kaplan 2020 vs Hoffmann 2022.
- Physics MBE convergence (often good with good reference) vs. naive ML higher-order blowup (cost/combinatorics, oversmoothing): one suggests "add orders," other warns "regularize." Resolve by A2 (test additivity vs redundancy empirically).
- None found on BNSL outperforming simple power laws for extrapolation on diverse tasks (Caballero et al. report consistent win).

## Missing evidence
- We have not verified the 0 → 0.2–0.27 growth curve shape (linear vs. concave) or snapshot heterogeneity — A1–A3 require the raw 240-point table.
- No cost/complexity numbers for full vs. diag-only at each support size (needed to claim a scaling *tradeoff*).
- No null/permutation baseline: does diag-only already capture chance-level coupling? Unverified.
