# Research: Causal estimand of an intervention-response experiment (M2)

## Summary
Your current experiment estimates the **causal effect of an input on a fixed model output**, i.e. `E[Y | do(X=x')] − E[Y | do(X=x)]` where `Y = f(X)` is deterministic — this is "response shaping," and it is trivially identified because you control `X` fully. It becomes a **causal-representation claim** ("geometry predicts response" as a fact about the world, not about `f`) only under extra assumptions: (a) the pixel intervention maps onto an intervention on a latent causal factor (consistency/alignment), and (b) the embedding dimension stands in a modular/abstraction relation to that factor. Without those, geometry→response is a property of the encoder, not evidence of a causal mechanism.

## Findings

1. **Claim:** In Pearl's do-calculus, an input perturbation with full control is an atomic (or constrained-stochastic) intervention whose effect on a deterministic model output is identified without adjustment. **Sources:** Pearl (UCLA r402 do-calculus revisited); Correa & Bareinboim, "A Calculus for Stochastic Interventions" (AAAI). **Support:** direct evidence — `do(X=x)` severs incoming edges to `X` and induces `P(V|do(x))` via truncated factorization; stochastic/soft variants replace rather than fully fix the mechanism for `X`. **Confidence:** high.
   *Researcher inference:* because `Y=f(X)` with known `f` and experimenter-set `X`, there is no backdoor path `X←C→Y` inside the experiment; `P(Y|do(x')) = 1[Y=f(x')]` exactly. The only causal content is about `f`, not about football.

2. **Claim:** Schölkopf et al. (2021) argue observational data alone cannot identify causal variables/representations; what is identifiable depends on which interventions are observed, under principles of Independent Causal Mechanisms (ICM) and Sparse Mechanism Shift (SMS). **Sources:** Schölkopf et al., "Toward Causal Representation Learning," Proc. IEEE 109(5):612–634 (arXiv:2102.11107). **Support:** direct evidence (paper's stated theses; SMS hypothesis: small distribution changes from interventions affect only few mechanisms). **Confidence:** high.
   *Researcher inference:* your eps=0.25 equal-energy reallocations are "interventional data" in the CRL sense only if they sparsely target one latent factor (e.g. formation support geometry) while holding others (total energy, player count, background) fixed — which your equal-energy design attempts. But CRL still requires stating which latent factor was intervened on; pixel-space constraint ≠ latent-factor localization.

3. **Claim:** Geiger et al.'s interchange interventions test whether a high-level causal model is a faithful abstraction of a neural network: align high-level variable `V` to network states, swap in activations from a counterfactual input, and check output equivalence (interchange-intervention accuracy). **Sources:** Geiger et al., "Causal Abstractions of Neural Networks" (NeurIPS 2021); "Inducing Causal Structure for Interpretable Neural Networks / IIT" (ICML 2022, PMLR 162); Geiger et al., "Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation" (JMLR 26). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.
   *Researcher inference:* this is the missing bridge for your claim: "geometry predicts response" is an abstraction claim (`geometry variable → embedding axis`) and should be tested by interchange (swap the putative geometry component between two formations, hold rest fixed) rather than only by whole-input perturbation.

4. **Claim:** The assumption separating "response shaping" from "causal effect" is **consistency + modularity/alignment**: (i) the input manipulation corresponds to exactly one well-defined latent cause (no multiple versions of treatment), and (ii) intervening on it leaves other mechanisms invariant (SMS/modularity) with a stated alignment between pixel operation and latent factor. Eberhardt & Scheines' hard-vs-soft distinction is the direct precedent: soft interventions add an external influence without breaking incoming arrows. **Sources:** Eberhardt & Scheines, "Interventions and Causal Inference" (PSA 2006); Correa & Bareinboim (stochastic/soft calculus); Schölkopf et al. 2021 (ICM/SMS). **Support:** interpretation grounded in direct evidence. **Confidence:** medium-high.
   *Researcher inference:* your eps-reallocation is formally a **soft/stochastic intervention** (a constrained redistribution, not `do(support=S*)` atomically with all else severed). Treating it as an atomic `do` on "geometry" smuggles in the conclusion.

## Three formalization upgrades for OUR claim

**U1 — Restate the estimand as a constrained stochastic-contrast, not "the causal effect of geometry."**
- *Exact statement:* "Estimand τ = E[Δ(f(X),f(X')) | X'=g(X,ε), ‖X'‖₁=‖X‖₁, d(X,X')≤0.25], where g is the reallocation operator, Δ is embedding displacement; this is P(Y|σ(X)) under stochastic intervention σ, a property of fixed encoder f."
- *Assumptions:* (A1) full control of X (no unobserved confounding *within* experiment); (A2) g fully specified (reproducible); (A3) no claim about world mechanisms.
- *Falsifier:* a second implementation of g with same constraint producing systematically different τ distribution → operator underspecified; claim about f fails.
- *Evidence grade:* definitional (high). *Sources:* Correa & Bareinboim; Pearl r402.

**U2 — Add an explicit alignment + interchange test before any "geometry causes embedding shift" language.**
- *Exact statement:* "Let G(X)=support-geometry descriptor, Z=f(X) embedding axis. Claim: high-level model G→Z is a causal abstraction of f with interchange accuracy ≥1−δ under activation/pattern swaps."
- *Assumptions:* (B1) alignment map pixel-pattern↔G stated; (B2) counterfactual inputs realizing G=g₁ vs g₂ with matched energy exist in support; (B3) swap does not push f off-manifold (checked by reconstruction/validity metric).
- *Falsifier:* interchange accuracy ≈ chance, or matched-energy non-geometric controls (U3) produce equal Δ → G→Z abstraction rejected; downgrade to "f is sensitive to inputs," not "geometry drives Z."
- *Evidence grade:* medium (method established, your instantiation untested). *Sources:* Geiger et al. NeurIPS 2021; ICML 2022 (IIT); JMLR 2024 foundation.

**U3 — Require a same-energy non-geometric control to invoke SMS/modularity.**
- *Exact statement:* "Claim 'geometry predicts response' means: τ_geometry ≫ τ_control at fixed energy budget, i.e. SMS holds — only the geometry mechanism shifts Z."
- *Assumptions:* (C1) controls matched on energy, sparsity, and pixel-count; (C2) pre-registered τ metric and threshold; (C3) ICM: non-target mechanisms invariant under g.
- *Falsifier:* random same-energy reallocations produce same Δ distribution as geometric ones → response explained by energy/budget sensitivity, not geometry; SMS premise fails.
- *Evidence grade:* medium (design-level; evidence pending your run). *Sources:* Schölkopf et al. 2021 (ICM/SMS); Eberhardt & Scheines (soft-intervention confounding).
