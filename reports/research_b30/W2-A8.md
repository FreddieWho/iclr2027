# Research: Triplet vs InfoNCE margin-vs-uniformity (GOAL-R2 A8)

## Summary
GOAL-R2's 0.64 (triplet) vs 0.05 (InfoNCE) frozen margin gap is expected theory, not a bug: triplet hinge optimizes a single hard-negative gap while InfoNCE optimizes global alignment plus hypersphere uniformity with no hard margin. Temperature τ is the knob: small τ sharpens InfoNCE toward hard negatives (more triplet-like, less stable); large τ smooths toward calibrated spread. The fix is a margin-in-softmax hybrid; Circle loss is the most principled single-loss choice, margin-augmented SupCon is the simplest, and InfoNCE + small triplet auxiliary is the safest fallback.

## Findings

1. **Claim:** InfoNCE asymptotically decomposes into alignment (pull positives) + uniformity (spread on hypersphere); triplet enforces neither globally. **Sources:** [Wang & Isola, ICML 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/wang20k.html); [InfoNCE decomposition follow-up, PMLR 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/parulekar23a/parulekar23a.pdf). **Support:** direct evidence (theorem + loss split in Eq.3 of latter). **Confidence:** high.
   Explanation: triplet hinge gives nonzero gradient only on violating triplets, so it creates a local gap but no pressure for globally even spread — hence sharp margin plus collapse risk and mining dependence. InfoNCE gives dense softmax gradients over all negatives, hence calibrated spread but no enforced minimum gap. *Researcher inference:* this exactly predicts R2's sharp-vs-spread split.

2. **Claim:** Temperature controls a uniformity–tolerance dilemma: lowering τ emphasizes hardest negatives (better uniformity/separation, worse tolerance to semantically near positives, more instability); raising τ does the reverse. **Sources:** [Wang & Liu, arXiv 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09740); [review summary](https://deep-learning-study.tistory.com/753). **Support:** direct evidence (abstract + gradient analysis: τ scales hardness weighting). **Confidence:** high.
   Practical read: τ≈0.05–0.1 is sharp/discriminative but gradient variance explodes and false-negative penalty grows; τ≈0.2–0.5 is stable/calibrated but margins soften. SupCon authors report smaller τ helps to a point, then numerical instability dominates ([Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/d89a66c7c80a29b1bdbab0f2a1a94af8-Paper.pdf), §4). *Researcher inference:* an InfoNCE variant scored under a frozen triplet-margin metric will look near-zero (0.05) even when its ROC/AUC spread is better — metric mismatch, not representation failure.

3. **Claim:** Additive angular margin (ArcFace) shows how to inject a hard gap into a softmax without losing stability: penalize target logit by margin m in angular/cosine space with scale s. **Source:** [Deng et al., CVPR 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Deng_ArcFace_Additive_Angular_Margin_Loss_for_Deep_Face_Recognition_CVPR_2019_paper.html). **Support:** direct evidence (paper + ablations over m=0.4–0.55). **Confidence:** high for face verification; medium for transfer to our regime (class-center softmax, not pair-contrastive).
   Transfer recipe → Design 1 below.

4. **Claim:** Supervised contrastive (SupCon) generalizes InfoNCE to multiple positives per anchor with the same τ mechanics, and is the natural base for a margin hybrid. **Source:** [Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/d89a66c7c80a29b1bdbab0f2a1a94af8-Abstract.html). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

5. **Claim:** Circle loss unifies triplet-style (s_n − s_p) and softmax losses by self-paced weighting of each similarity by its distance from optimum, with separate margins for positives/negatives and a circular decision boundary; it converges to a more definite target than either parent. **Sources:** [Sun et al., CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Sun_Circle_Loss_A_Unified_Perspective_of_Pair_Similarity_Optimization_CVPR_2020_paper.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10857). **Support:** direct evidence (analytical unification + reweighting derivation). **Confidence:** high for pair/metric tasks; medium for our exact modality (evidence is face/person-ReID, not our data).

6. **Claim:** Alignment-adaptive temperature (MACL) is the principled fix for the dilemma when one global τ cannot serve both sharpness and calibration. **Source:** [Huang et al., PMLR v202, 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/huang23c/huang23c.pdf). **Support:** interpretation (method paper, three modalities). **Confidence:** medium.

### Three concrete loss designs (at most 3)

**D1 — Margin-Augmented SupCon (simplest; recommended first).** L = −mean over anchors/positives of log exp((s_ap − m)/τ) / [exp((s_ap − m)/τ) + Σ_n exp(s_an/τ)], cosine sims, τ=0.1 (sweep 0.07–0.2), m=0.1–0.3 cosine (≈0.2 start), rescale loss by τ per SupCon practice. Keeps InfoNCE dense gradients (stability/spread) + enforces a minimum positive–negative logit gap (sharpness). **Evidence grade: medium-high** — ArcFace margin-in-softmax (CVPR 2019) + SupCon base (NeurIPS 2020) are each strong; the combination is standard practice but the exact m×τ interaction in our regime is untested.

**D2 — Circle loss on pairs (most principled single loss).** Use cosine s_p, s_n with margins O_p≈1−m, O_n≈m (start m=0.25), scale γ=64–128 (start 80), per-pair weights α_p=[O_p−s_p]+, α_n=[s_n−O_n]+. Under-optimized pairs auto-upweight, so easy negatives stop dominating while hard positives still pull — directly fixes triplet's sparse-gradient and InfoNCE's no-margin problems. **Evidence grade: medium-high** — CVPR 2020 theory + ReID/face gains; needs γ/m re-tune for our embedding dim and batch composition.

**D3 — Decoupled InfoNCE + small triplet auxiliary (safest fallback).** L = L_InfoNCE(τ=0.15) + λ·L_triplet(cosine, m=0.2–0.3, semi-hard mining), λ=0.1–0.3 (start 0.2). Alternatively L_align + λ_u·L_uniform direct (Wang & Isola losses) + triplet. InfoNCE owns calibration/spread; hinge owns the frozen-margin metric. If training destabilizes, lower λ before touching τ. **Evidence grade: medium** — two-term hybrids (cf. ArcFace+Triplet ablations in CVPR 2019) routinely keep both metrics green, but λ tuning is ad hoc and hard mining reintroduces triplet variance.

## Contradictions
- Small τ: SupCon (NeurIPS 2020) and Wang & Liu (arXiv 2020) agree smaller τ helps discrimination but both warn of instability; no universal optimal τ — must be swept per batch size/negatives count.
- Circle loss claims superiority over joint triplet+softmax, while ArcFace paper's own tables show ArcFace+Triplet competitive on some verification splits — unresolved whether single unified loss or decoupled auxiliary is better; treat D2 vs D3 as empirical shootout.
- InfoNCE-as-MI-bound motivation (Oord et al., arXiv 2018) vs alignment/uniformity geometric view (Wang & Isola 2020): compatible framings, but MI-bound tightening does not monotonically predict downstream quality — do not use MI estimates as a model-selection metric.

## Missing evidence
- No direct benchmark of ArcFace/SupCon-margin/Circle in our exact task regime (our data, encoder, batch size, frozen-margin protocol) — all transfer claims are cross-domain.
- Optimal τ batch-size scaling for our negative count unmeasured; false-negative rate at small τ unknown.
- Whether frozen triplet margin 0.64 corresponds to better downstream decision cost vs InfoNCE's calibrated spread untested — need cost-aware threshold comparison, not margin alone.
