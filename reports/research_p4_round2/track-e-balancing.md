# Research: Multi-task balancing AFTER GradNorm + frozen-backbone routing (destructive-conflict regime)

## Summary
Your regime (auxiliary actively erases main-task features; route-x12 ate context; healthy warm-start still destroyed on entry) is **directional conflict**, not scale mismatch. Magnitude-only balancers (GradNorm, uncertainty weighting) are expected to fail here by construction; the evidence favors **directional surgery (CAGrad > PCGrad)** for joint training, and — more strongly for a destructive auxiliary — **a two-stage freeze-then-route protocol** (LP-FT / side-tuning logic) that never lets the router reshape the trunk until its head is near-optimal.

## Findings

1. **GradNorm (Chen et al., ICML 2018) and uncertainty weighting (Kendall et al., CVPR 2018) handle scale/rate, not direction — predict failure in your regime.** GradNorm equalizes per-task gradient norms times inverse training rates via an auxiliary L_grad loss ([paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/chen18a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02257)); it never projects or re-orients conflicting vectors. The CAGrad authors explicitly motivate their method from this limitation: prior heuristics "lack convergence guarantee and/or could converge to any Pareto-stationary point" when objectives are misaligned ([NeurIPS abstract](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/9d27fdf2477ffbff837d73ef7ae23db9-Abstract.html); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14048v2)). Your route-x12 experiment is the textbook demonstration: restoring norm parity just let the destructive direction dominate.

2. **CAGrad (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2021) has the strongest published evidence specifically under severe conflict.** It maximizes the worst-task local improvement inside a ball of radius c around the mean gradient, and provably converges to a minimum of the average loss (not an arbitrary Pareto point). Headline numbers: Meta-World MT10 0.83 vs PCGrad 0.72; MT50 0.52 vs 0.50 ([review of reported tables](https://liner.com/review/conflictaverse-gradient-descent-for-multitask-learning)); consistent wins on NYUv2/Cityscapes dense prediction, including combined with uncertainty weights ([tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14048v2)). An independent AAAI architecture paper's NYUv2 table is telling: GradNorm Δm −1.68, MGDA worse, PCGrad mildly negative/mixed, CAGrad-family and Nash-MTL positive ([AAAI PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/26312/26084)). **Rank for destructive regime: #1.**

3. **PCGrad (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020) is the direct "destructive interference" method but empirically second to CAGrad.** Its rule is exactly your diagnosis — if g_i·g_j<0, subtract the projection ([NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/3fe78a8acf5fda99de95303940a2420c-Paper.pdf)). It helps on toy-conflict, MT-RL, and some NYUv2 splits, but follow-ups show it can be over-aggressive (kills useful shared components when conflict is ubiquitous) and inconsistent on dense prediction, where CAGrad beats it. **Rank #2 as the cheap first surgery to try** (simple, no game solve), with CAGrad as the upgrade. MGDA/MGDA-UB (Desideri 2012; Sener & Koltun 2018) rank below both here: Pareto-stationarity guarantee sounds good but in practice it is conservative and can stall average-loss progress — visible in the negative Δ rows cited above.

4. **Nash-MTL (Navon et al., 2022) matches or tops CAGrad on quality but at high cost; Aligned-MTL (Senushkin et al., 2023) and FAMO (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023) fill cost/robustness niches.** Nash-MTL (bargaining solution) reports best mean-rank on NYUv2 and near-best on Cityscapes with balanced per-task gains ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01017v1); [code](https://github.com/AvivNavon/nash-mtl)). Cost: per-step per-task gradients + bargaining solve each iteration — painful beyond 2–3 tasks. FAMO instead equalizes *loss-decrease rates* with O(1) amortized updates using only scalar losses, matching SOTA at Adam cost ([NeurIPS paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/b2fe1ee8d936ac08dd26f2ff58986c8f-Paper-Conference.pdf); [code/readme](https://github.com/Cranial-XIX/FAMO)). But FAMO is still rate-balancing, not directional surgery — good scheduler/controller substrate, not a conflict cure. Aligned-MTL (gradient-alignment/stability) is newer with thinner independent replication. **Rank: #3 Nash-MTL (quality) or FAMO (budget); neither displaces CAGrad for erasure-type conflict.**

5. **Two-stage precedent strongly supports freeze-then-route when the second objective distorts good features.** Kumar et al., ICLR Oral 2022 ("Fine-Tuning can Distort Pretrained Features") show full fine-tuning with a random head destroys good pretrained features and underperforms linear probing OOD on 8/10 datasets; **LP-FT (linear probe, then fine-tune from the probed head) beats both** by shrinking the feature distortion needed ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=UYneFzXSJWh); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054); [summary](https://liner.com/review/finetuning-can-distort-pretrained-features-and-underperform-outofdistribution)). Side-tuning (Zhang et al., ECCV 2020) makes the same point architecturally: freeze the backbone, train a small side network, alpha-blend outputs — no catastrophic forgetting, best in low-data regimes ([repo](https://github.com/jozhang97/side-tuning)). MoE practice converges similarly: production teams **freeze converged routers** during fine-tuning and drop the load-balancing loss entirely ([Vector Institute survey](https://vectorinstitute.ai/mixture-of-experts-from-sparse-routing-to-multimodal-deployment)); "gate-only tuning" on frozen experts is an established cheap adaptation ([MoE survey](https://ai.plainenglish.io/mixture-of-experts-moe-models-in-ai-4bcbcdecccf8)). No paper does exactly "spectral routing on frozen context features beats joint," but the mechanism is identical: **routing heads trained on frozen features learn assignment over stable directions instead of dragging features toward router convenience.** That is precisely the failure you observed on warm-start entry.

6. **Scheduler evidence: per-step lightweight controllers on last-shared-layer grads + slow ramp after warm-start.** GradNorm practice: compute task grad norms w.r.t. the **last shared layer only**, renormalize weights to sum to #tasks each step, asymmetry α≈1.5 on NYUv2 ([notes](https://patrick-llgc.github.io/Learning-Deep-Learning/paper_notes/gradnorm.html)). FAMO updates its weight logits **every step** from loss-decay ratios with small lr β plus decay γ toward recency ([NeurIPS paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/b2fe1ee8d936ac08dd26f2ff58986c8f-Paper-Conference.pdf)). CAGrad's conflict radius c∈[0,1) (0 = mean gradient; papers peak ~0.2–0.4) is the ramp-adjacent knob. Combined with LP-FT logic, the supported recipe is: warm-start mains to healthy → freeze → probe routing head → unfreeze with **linear/cosine ramp of routing weight over ~10–20 epochs** and a per-step controller — never a step-function entry (your step entry destroyed context even from healthy).

## Ranked prescriptions for OUR setting (try in order)

**P1 — Freeze-then-route (do first; cheapest, directly anti-erasure).**
(a) Train context + triplet only (~20–40 of your 80 epochs) to healthy. (b) Freeze trunk; train only the routing head (linear probe) to convergence. (c) Optional: unfreeze trunk at 0.1× LR with P2 active, routing weight ramped 0→1 over 10–20 epochs. *Why:* removes the erasure channel by construction; LP-FT (ICLR 2022) + side-tuning evidence. *Controls:* probe must reach plateau before any unfreeze; abort unfreeze if context dev regresses >2 epochs.

**P2 — Joint rescue with CAGrad (if joint training is required).**
Per-step CAGrad on trunk grads (last shared layer), c=0.2 first (try 0.4 second), on top of warm-start + linear routing-weight ramp; keep Adam 1e-3 for params, renormalize task weights each step. *Why:* only method with prover convergence-to-mean-minimum plus best severe-conflict numbers (NeurIPS 2021). *Cheaper pre-test:* PCGrad projection (NeurIPS 2020) for one run to confirm directionality before paying CAGrad's per-task backward passes (3 tasks = affordable).

**P3 — FAMO controller as efficiency fallback.**
If CAGrad is too slow/unstable, use FAMO's O(1) logit controller (official defaults: model lr as now, weight-lr β and decay γ from [repo](https://github.com/Cranial-XIX/FAMO)) + same warm-start/ramp. *Why:* matches SOTA at Adam cost (NeurIPS 2023); but expect it to fix rate imbalance only — keep the freeze (P1) as the actual erasure guard. Do not retry GradNorm/uncertainty-weighting alone: no directional mechanism.
