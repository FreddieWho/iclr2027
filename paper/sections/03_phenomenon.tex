\section{Failure and Confidence Under Change}
\label{sec:phenomenon}

\paragraph{(i) Transition blindness.} Paths crossing a semantic boundary
often induce no model transition at all. Among single-turn paths
discovered by random edits, $108/128 = 84.4\%$ miss the turn ($107$ of the
$108$ show no transition at any scanned point; one turns with wrong
timing); on directly targeted fresh paths the miss rate is $28/64 =
43.8\%$ (95\% CI $[0.32,0.56]$), with flip coverage at $31.3\%$. The two
screens sample different path populations---the random screen over-samples
rare grazing single-turn crossings while the targeted sweep drives
decisively across the boundary---so the rates are reported side by side,
not averaged. The sampling explanation replaces an earlier crossing-angle
account, which a preregistered incidence analysis did not support
(audit history: Appendix~\ref{sec:audit}).

\paragraph{(ii) Compositional blindness (headline).} When \emph{both}
atomic edits of a scene are judged correctly, their joint compositional
change is still missed in $151/176 = 85.8\%$ of fresh-holdout cases
(discovery $62/66 = 93.9\%$, same direction). Matched single-edit controls
miss less ($61.0\%$ clean on fresh data, $n{=}251$), but the two rates use
different denominators ($176$ vs $251$) and different screens, so their
arithmetic difference ($24.8$pp fresh) is a descriptive contrast, not a
causal composition-specific effect. Path decomposition shows the miss
unfolding as missed updates: $111/111$ AB-miss quartets contain a
zero-transition path along $A \to AB$ or $B \to AB$, with no recrossing.
A model can know each part and still not track the change.

\paragraph{Confidence is reliable for current states, unreliable for true
changes.} Pre-change confidence predicts current-state error in the
classical direction (quartile error $0.27$--$0.51 \to 0.02$--$0.06$ over
unscreened starts, dev and confirmation banks showing the same shape) and
semantics-preserving error likewise. Conditioned on start correctness, the
same confidence ordering predicts update error in reverse: endpoint error
after a true semantic flip rises $0.36$--$0.61 \to 0.98$--$1.00$ across
confidence quartiles---six trained models (three seeds $\times$ clean and
flipmine arms) evaluated on two frozen banks: eleven of the twelve
model$\times$bank curves reach $1.00$ in the top quartile, one reaches
$0.983$. Grouped-by-parent cross-validation separates the two readings:
paired out-of-fold AUC differences (identical parent draws) favor the
confidence model by $+0.30$--$+0.48$ on dev and $+0.20$--$+0.37$ on
confirmation (all parent-cluster CIs exclude zero, conditioned on the CV
fit); pooled OOF AUC is $0.77$--$0.96$ versus $0.35$--$0.63$ difficulty-only
on dev and $0.77$--$0.95$ versus $0.52$--$0.63$ on confirmation.
On retained high-confidence misses, repair reduces endpoint error but
leaves a residual majority still wrong: with corrected frozen thresholds the
confirm residual falls from $1.00$ to $0.32$--$0.62$ (paired CIs exclude zero
in all three seeds; repair adds $26$/$26$/$52$ start errors, reported rather
than screened).
Risk-reweighted retention makes the trade-off explicit: keeping the
top-confidence quarter (a dev-set reference quantile; actual retained
fractions vary by pool and subpopulation) yields near-zero current and
preserve-update error on the retained population while concentrating
conditional update error ($100\%$ among retained flips; s11 clean,
$n{=}101/271$ retained). This is a reweighting of a selected population,
not an unselected deployment incidence. Natural football turns show no such
inversion after correction (floor effects); the claim is coordinate-only
(Fig.~\ref{fig:p1}).

\paragraph{(iii) Consequential blindness, stated as association.} The
misses co-occur with downstream failures, and we do not claim a causal
propagation chain. Fresh-holdout event detection is thin ($1/24$ clean
conditional misses) and reported without inflation; action selection under
a fixed action library is invalid in $75.0\%$ of hard fresh cases
($n{=}144$, $\lambda{=}2$); the single-flip decomposition (C1/C0) shows a
C1 miss of $52.4\%$ against a C0 false-alarm rate of $26.2\%$. Two
honesty constraints bound any stronger reading: across six training arms on
the dev pool, arm-level downstream invalidity is tracked better by static
error than by update metrics (Spearman $0.94$ vs $0.54$), and at scene
level the raw miss$\to$invalidity association is confounded (missed-update
scenes tend to be static-correct scenes; once static correctness is held
fixed, a missed transition still adds $+0.46$ log-odds of an invalid
action). We therefore keep all transition/consequence language descriptive
(discovery-grade, single-seed arms).
