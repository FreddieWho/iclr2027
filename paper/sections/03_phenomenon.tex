\section{Three Failure Modes, Plus Confidence}
\label{sec:phenomenon}

\paragraph{(i) Transition blindness.} Paths crossing a semantic boundary often
induce no model transition at all. Among single-turn paths discovered by
random edits, $108/128 = 84.4\%$ induce zero model transition; on directly
targeted fresh paths the miss rate is $28/64 = 43.8\%$ (95\% CI
$[0.32,0.56]$), with flipmine at $31.3\%$. Both numbers are true; their
denominators differ because the screens sample different path populations:
the random screen over-samples rare grazing single-turn crossings while the
targeted sweep drives decisively across the boundary. We report both, never
averaged, always with denominators. (An earlier draft attributed the gap to
crossing angle; a preregistered incidence analysis found no such law, so we
retract that interpretation and keep only the sampling description.)

\paragraph{(ii) Compositional blindness (headline).} When \emph{both} atomic
edits of a scene are judged correctly, their joint compositional change is
still missed in $151/176 = 85.8\%$ of fresh-holdout cases (discovery
$62/66 = 93.9\%$, same direction). Matched single-edit controls miss less
($61.0\%$ clean on fresh data), but the two rates use different denominators
($176$ vs $251$) and different screens, so their arithmetic difference
($24.8$pp fresh) is descriptive, not a causal composition-specific effect.
Path decomposition shows the miss unfolding as missed updates: $111/111$
AB-miss quartets contain a zero-transition path along $A \to AB$ or
$B \to AB$, with no recrossing. A model can know each part and still not
track the change.

\paragraph{Confidence is reliable for states, unreliable for true changes.}
Within currently correct coordinate states, pre-change confidence predicts
static error in the classical direction (quartile error $0.27$--$0.51 \to
$0.02$--$0.05$ on dev; $0.02$--$0.06$ pooled with confirmation)
and semantics-preserving error likewise, yet predicts update error in
reverse: endpoint error after a true semantic flip rises $0.36$--$0.61 \to
0.98$--$1.00$ (twelve model-seed combinations: eleven reach $1.00$ in the top
quartile, one reaches $0.983$; fresh parent-disjoint confirmation).
Grouped-by-parent 5-fold cross-validation, pooled out-of-fold: confidence-only
AUC $0.78$--$0.95$ (clean models $0.93$--$0.95) against difficulty-only
$0.42$--$0.61$; oracle difficulty and geometric covariates carry approximately
no held-out signal. On retained high-confidence misses, repair reduces endpoint
error $1.00 \to 0.53$--$0.84$ on confirmation (paired CIs exclude zero; dev
paired CIs touch zero in two of three seeds, s47 excludes), leaving a residual majority still wrong.
Stricter trust in high-confidence states lowers current error while
concentrating update failures (top-25\% retention, s11 clean, $n{=}101/271$ retained: $\sim$4\% static error and $0.00$ preserve-update error vs 100\% conditional update error). Natural football turns
show no such inversion after correction (floor effects); the claim is
coordinate-only (Fig.~\ref{fig:p1}).

\paragraph{(iii) Consequential blindness.} The misses propagate. Fresh-holdout
event detection is thin ($1/24$ clean conditional misses) and reported
without inflation; action selection under a fixed action library is invalid
in $75.0\%$ of hard fresh cases ($n{=}144$, $\lambda{=}2$); the single-flip
decomposition (C1/C0) shows a C1 miss of $52.4\%$ against a C0 false-alarm
rate of $26.2\%$. Decision-interface analysis (U02, $93$ oracle-feasible scenes of $171$) shows flipmine's gain is
in refusal reduction (refusal $0.43 \to 0.12$, $3.6\times$ fewer refusals;
non-refusal coverage $0.57 \to 0.88$) rather than non-refusal
precision ($0.23$ vs $0.22$ on the 93-scene hard subset), and that the gain survives across scoring rules
(see the evidence table, reports/final_closure/MASTER_CLAIM_LEDGER.md).
Two honesty constraints on this consequence module: across six training arms on
the dev pool, arm-level downstream invalidity is tracked better by static error
than by update metrics (Spearman $0.94$ vs $0.54$), and at scene level the raw
association is confounded (missed-update scenes tend to be static-correct
scenes; once static correctness is held fixed, a missed transition still adds
$+0.46$ log-odds of an invalid action). We therefore keep all
transition/consequence language descriptive, never causal (discovery-grade,
single-seed arms).
