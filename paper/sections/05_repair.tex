\section{Pixel-Observation Confirmation}
\label{sec:pixel}

The same quartet comparison rendered as pixels and
judged by a freshly trained CNN gives a compositional (emergent) error of
$0.537$ clean vs.\ $0.351$ pixflip ($-19$pp, static error unchanged at
$0.090$)---while the quartet-curriculum arm scores \emph{worse} than clean
($0.572$).

Across five unseen rendering styles (line width, color swap, bright
background, $0.9\times$ scale, same-style replication), pixflip has the lowest
emergent error in all five ($0.333/0.349/0.305/0.349/0.366$ vs.\ clean
$0.514/0.622/0.426/0.389/0.516$; gains $-18/-27/-12/-4/-15$pp). Absolute
levels are style-sensitive---under color swap all arms' atomic accuracy
collapses to $0.25$--$0.31$ (the CNN binds color to segment identity)---but
the relative gain is stable and same-signed $5/5$. After the nuisance-lock
fix (same fresh seed per quartet state; rerun \texttt{u03b\_fixed}), emergent
errors are numerically identical and atomic accuracies move by $\le 0.01$,
confirming the comparison was fair and the repair survives exact nuisance
locking. Two additional training seeds confirm the same pattern $5/5$ each:
s805 gains $-10/-10/-16/-8/-11$pp, s806 gains $-30/-30/-16/-21/-28$pp.
The cross-style repair now rests on three independent training instances,
not one. Pixel evidence supports compositional/update repair across visual
nuisance; it does not test the confidence-inversion or migration claims,
which rest on coordinate analyses.
