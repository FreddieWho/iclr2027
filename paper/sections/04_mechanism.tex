\section{Endpoint Repair, Migration, and Relational Input}
\label{sec:repair}

\paragraph{Component improvements.} The repair is diagnostic, not a
product: training on oracle-mined boundary-crossing variations (flipmine)
tests whether exposing the model to the missing update shape changes
behavior. On the fresh holdout: single-flip C1 miss $52.4\% \to 34.7\%$
(paired same-sample comparison; C0 false alarm $26.2\% \to 23.8\%$, within
$\pm 5$pp, so no alarm inflation); action invalidity at $\lambda{=}2$,
$75.0\% \to 62.5\%$; turn miss $43.8\% \to 31.3\%$; and static error
\emph{improves}, $18.0\% \to 10.7\%$. The decision-interface decomposition
(Sec.~\ref{sec:interfaces}) locates much of the action gain in coverage
rather than precision, and an affine rescaling of clean logits fitted on
dev cannot reproduce the repair (paired affine-vs-clean comparison
intervals touch or cross zero). The repair is therefore not a pure
operating-point shift, within the boundedness stated for that control
(Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}). The same coverage-not-precision pattern holds at
the decision-boundary level: on $1301$ oracle single-crossings (dev pool,
frozen checkpoints, matching radius $r{=}0.025$), clean models place a
decision root near only $9.6\%$ of oracle boundary points; flip coverage
raises this to $16.9\%$ ($+7.3$pp, parent-cluster CI $[0.020,0.128]$) with
offset and signed alignment unchanged---a boundary-coverage shift, not
boundary alignment (discovery-grade, no fresh confirmation).

\paragraph{Endpoint repair is not full repair.} Targeted flip coverage
improves several component metrics, but paired quartet analysis on the
baseline-110 subset $H$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:setting}) reveals that endpoint
repair substantially overstates recovery of full compositional consistency.
On the dev bank (parent-cluster CIs; Fig.~\ref{fig:migration}): full
repair $0.03$--$0.10$; error migration $0.36$--$0.40$; and since
$R_{\mathrm{endpoint}} = R_{\mathrm{full}} + M$, migration accounts for
$79$--$94\%$ of the observed endpoint repair across seeds. Joint
consistency $J = P(111 \mid E)$ moves only slightly ($0.05 \to 0.06$--$0.08$):
a small change, and not yet evidence of a large joint-consistency gain
from raw flip coverage alone. On the common-start-correct subset the
repair direction is inconsistent across seeds (including one reversal,
s23 $0.920 \to 0.947$). A preserve-balanced retraining pilot with matched
loss weights does not reduce migration; this bounds \emph{that fixed}
sampling/weighting scheme, not positive-example protection in general.
For reference, the flipmine arm's own-conditional emergent rate on the
fresh holdout is $109/141 = 77.3\%$ on a different denominator ($141$ vs
$176$, non-paired)---descriptive only, not a repair delta.

\paragraph{A bounded positive instance: relational input $\times$ flip
coverage.} Label-free relational input features change the picture where
flip coverage alone could not. Table~\ref{tab:fourarm} reports all four
arms---raw coordinates vs relational features, each with and without flip
coverage---on the same dev bank (paired quartets, $n{=}237$) with the
confirmation bank ($n{=}509$) alongside; the full $2{\times}2$ is evaluated
on both banks from archived outputs (confirmation: same-bank re-analysis,
no new sealed reads). On the dev bank, relational input
alone raises $J$ to $0.13$--$0.21$ with reduced static error
($0.15$--$0.17 \to 0.06$); combining relational input with flip coverage
reaches $J$ $0.38$--$0.47$ across banks (Fig.~\ref{fig:fourarm},
Table~\ref{tab:fourarm}), and on the confirmation bank full
repair $0.45$--$0.50$ exceeds migration $0.25$--$0.30$---the reverse of
the raw-flipmine pattern. The combination is super-additive on the joint
scale: $I=(J_{\mathrm{relflip}}-J_{\mathrm{relfeat}})-(J_{\mathrm{rawflip}}-J_{\mathrm{raw}})$
is $+17$--$+25$pp on dev (paired parent-cluster intervals exclude zero)
and $+11$--$+22$pp on confirmation (same-bank point estimates)---an
exploratory interaction with directional confirmation, not a universal
synergy law. Ordering analysis bounds the mechanism (Fig.~\ref{fig:fourarm}c):
the representation improves state separability $H$ ($0.54$--$0.57 \to
0.81$--$0.87$), while flip coverage on relational input mainly realizes
that separability at the fixed threshold rather than creating new
separability. This substantially improves joint consistency
and shifts repair toward full consistency; it does not solve the task
($J < 0.5$ leaves a majority of quartets inconsistent), and the features
are hand-designed and relation-aware by construction---an existence proof
for joint consistency under this input, not a general architecture
principle.
