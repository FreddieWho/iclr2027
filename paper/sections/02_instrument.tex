\section{Controlled Setting: Semantic Transitions}
\label{sec:setting}

We study scenes with oracle labels and edits with known effects. A base scene
$x$ has oracle label $y(x) \in \{0,1\}$ with margin $m(x)$. An atomic edit $e$
maps $x \mapsto x+e$ with known oracle outcome $(y(x+e), m(x+e))$; a composed
edit $e_a+e_b$ has a known \emph{joint} outcome that may differ from either
atomic outcome. The model's decision is judged twice: on the parts (each
atomic edit classified correctly?) and on the change (the composed or
boundary-crossing outcome classified correctly?). Static accuracy measures the
former; our object of study is the latter.

\paragraph{Coordinate scenes.} Synthetic two-segment scenes with a line-crossing
oracle. Development uses fixed pools (\texttt{train\_101}, \texttt{eval\_202});
all headline discovery numbers are re-tested on a freshly minted,
pre-registered holdout (\texttt{holdout\_909}), evaluated once in a single
uninterrupted run with fixed checkpoints and then archived as used
(one-shot discipline; Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}).

\paragraph{Real football.} IDSSE tracking data: turn (transition), event, and
action-outcome tasks evaluated per match, with frozen vs.\ covered model
variants across three training seeds (s11/s23/s47). There is no pristine
football holdout (the reserve field saw development use), so football evidence
is reported as three-seed same-direction confirmation with cross-field
evaluation, and its limits are stated plainly (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Pixel observations.} The same quartet geometry rendered as
$64\times64$ images and judged by a small CNN trained from scratch, clean vs.\
pixflip (flip-augmented) vs.\ pixquartet arms, then evaluated on five unseen
rendering styles. Within each evaluated quartet the three states share a fresh
same-seed RNG, so background nuisance is locked and only geometric state
varies (nuisance-lock rerun \texttt{u03b\_fixed}; Sec.~\ref{sec:pixel}).
