Static correctness does not guarantee reliable updating under change. In a
controlled setting with oracle labels and edits of known effect, we show
that when both atomic edits of a scene are judged correctly, their joint
compositional change is still missed in 151/176 (85.8\%) of fresh-holdout
cases. The same blind spot appears on single semantic-boundary crossings:
28/64 (43.8\%) miss on directly targeted fresh paths, versus 108/128
(84.4\%) of single-turn paths found by a random-edit screen---two true
rates from different denominators, never averaged. Within currently
correct states, pre-change confidence predicts static and
semantics-preserving correctness yet reverses under true semantic change:
endpoint error rises from 0.36--0.61 to 0.98--1.00 across confidence quartiles,
confirmed on fresh parent-disjoint data. Targeted flip coverage improves
static error (18.0\%~$\to$~10.7\%), single-flip C1 miss (52.4\%~$\to$~34.7\%),
and downstream action outcomes on the fresh holdout (paired same-sample
comparisons). Yet paired quartet analysis shows most AB
endpoint gains do not restore full atomic-plus-joint consistency: full
repair is 0.03--0.10 while error migration is 0.36--0.40 (dev bank,
baseline-110 quartets, parent-cluster CIs). Real football
tracking and pixel renderings support transition and repair behavior, but
not the confidence inversion. Correctness under change cannot be summarized
by static accuracy or endpoint repair alone.
