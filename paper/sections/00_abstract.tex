Static correctness does not guarantee reliable updating under change.
With oracle-known edits, jointly applying two individually judged-correct
edits still produces a missed compositional change in 151/176 (85.8\%)
of fresh-holdout cases. In coordinate scenes, pre-change confidence
predicts current-state correctness yet orders post-change error in the
opposite direction. Targeted flip coverage improves endpoint and downstream
metrics, but paired quartet analysis separates full repair (0.03--0.10)
from error migration (0.36--0.40). Task-adapted relational inputs with the
same coverage raise joint correctness to 0.38--0.42 on development and
0.44--0.47 on confirmation data, with full repair exceeding migration on
the latter. Two further geometric tasks support the input advantage:
distance-plus-flip reaches approximately 0.75 on point-in-triangle and
0.998 on segment--disk. Their original joint-correctness-scale interactions
have opposite signs, positive on the former and negative on the latter;
high combined performance does not imply universal super-additivity.
In a matched-budget rendered-image ResNet-18 experiment, static-to-flip
training increases joint correctness by 14.47 percentage points with
pretraining and 20.34 without. Matching auxiliary geometry targets to
visible information improves their learnability but does not consistently
add joint repair in the tested recipe.
Real football tracking supports bounded transition and repair observations,
but does not reproduce the coordinate confidence inversion. Static accuracy,
endpoint recovery, full compositional consistency, and downstream success
therefore require separate measurement.
