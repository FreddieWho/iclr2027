Models that judge static scenes correctly can still fail to update their
judgments when the scene changes. We document a systematic form of this
failure across three observation regimes---coordinate scenes, real football
tracking data, and pixel renderings. The headline result comes from a
pre-registered fresh holdout: when both atomic edits of a scene are judged
correctly, their joint compositional change is still missed in 151/176
(85.8\%) of cases. The same blind spot appears for single semantic-boundary
crossings (28/64~=~44\% miss on directly targeted fresh paths; 108/128~=~84\%
of single-turn paths discovered by random edits induce no model transition, a
rarer tangential subpopulation) and propagates into downstream consequences:
action selection is invalid in 75.0\% of hard cases and event detection shows
matched misses. A simple diagnostic flip augmentation---training on
boundary-crossing variations mined by the same oracle---reduces the fresh
compositional C1 miss from 52.4\% to 34.7\% at no static cost (static error
18.0\%~$\to$~10.7\%), with same-direction improvements on real football data
across three seeds (transitions, events, and action outcomes) and on unseen
pixel styles. Extensive failed alternatives (dense labels, slope supervision,
SDF readouts, distillation, relational architectures, quartet curricula, and a
rank-4 low-rank patch-map interchange that decodes but cannot install hybrid
truth into the frozen classifier at the tested layer) bound the claim: the
defect is in updating, not in seeing. Static accuracy hides failures to
update.
