\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Static correctness is not the same as reliable updating. Real systems must
maintain correct judgments as states change: a scene correctly classified
now must still be classified correctly after the world moves. This paper
studies correctness under controlled change, where scenes have oracle
labels, edits have known atomic effects, and composed edits have known
joint effects that may differ from either part.

Three failure modes recur. (i)~Transition blindness: paths crossing a
semantic boundary are missed---$28/64 = 43.8\%$ on directly targeted fresh
paths, $108/128 = 84.4\%$ among single-turn paths found by a random-edit
screen (different path populations; Sec.~\ref{sec:setting} states our
reporting convention once). (ii)~Compositional blindness: when both atomic
edits of a scene are judged correctly, their joint change is still missed
in $151/176 = 85.8\%$ of fresh-holdout cases (discovery $62/66 = 93.9\%$,
same direction); path decomposition shows compositional misses unfold as
missed semantic updates along $A \to AB$ and $B \to AB$ ($111/111$ AB-miss
quartets contain a zero-transition path). (iii)~Consequential blindness:
the misses co-occur with downstream failures---hard action selections are
invalid in $75.0\%$ of fresh cases ($n{=}144$, $\lambda{=}2$)---and we
keep this association descriptive rather than causal
(Sec.~\ref{sec:phenomenon}).

Our central descriptive finding concerns confidence. A score that is
reassuring for current-state correctness becomes misleading for true
semantic transitions: pre-change confidence predicts current-state error in
the classical direction, yet predicts update error in reverse---endpoint
error after a true semantic flip rises $0.36$--$0.61 \to 0.98$--$1.00$
across confidence quartiles (six trained models on dev and parent-disjoint
confirmation banks; coordinate scenes). The finding is domain-limited:
natural football turns show floor/null effects after correction, and the
pixel arm does not evaluate confidence.

Repair tells a subtler story. Targeted flip coverage improves several
component and downstream metrics on the fresh holdout, but paired quartet
analysis on frozen banks shows endpoint repair substantially overstates
recovery of full compositional consistency: full repair is $0.03$--$0.10$
while error migration---AB correct \emph{but} an atomic newly wrong---is
$0.36$--$0.40$. One intervention changes this picture within its stated
bounds: label-free relational input features combined with the same flip
coverage move joint consistency to $0.38$--$0.47$ across banks, with full
repair exceeding migration on the confirmation bank.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (C1)~A controlled-change evaluation separating
static correctness from update reliability: transition, compositional, and
consequential failure rates with oracle-known edits, re-tested on a
freshly minted pre-registered holdout. (C2)~A confidence--update
reliability separation in coordinate scenes, confirmed on parent-disjoint
data, with explicit domain limits. (C3)~A paired quartet analysis
distinguishing endpoint repair, full repair, and error migration, plus a
bounded positive instance---relational input $\times$ flip coverage---that
moves joint consistency toward full repair on both banks without claiming a
general architecture. Football and pixel observations provide
cross-observation behavior checks; the decision-interface analysis
supports, rather than anchors, the repair claims.
