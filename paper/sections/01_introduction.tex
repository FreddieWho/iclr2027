\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Static correctness is not the same as reliable updating. Real systems must
maintain correct judgments as states change: a scene correctly classified
now must still be classified correctly after the world moves. This paper
studies correctness under controlled change, where scenes have oracle
labels, edits have known atomic effects, and composed edits have known
joint effects that may differ from either part.

Three failure modes recur. (i)~Transition blindness: paths crossing a
semantic boundary induce no model transition---$28/64 = 43.8\%$ miss on
directly targeted fresh paths, $108/128 = 84.4\%$ among single-turn paths
found by a random-edit screen (different denominators, never averaged).
(ii)~Compositional blindness: when both atomic edits are judged correctly,
their joint change is still missed in $151/176 = 85.8\%$ of fresh-holdout
cases; continuous path decomposition shows compositional misses unfold as
missed semantic updates along $A \to AB$ and $B \to AB$ (111/111 AB-miss
quartets contain a zero-transition path). (iii)~Consequential blindness:
the misses propagate into events and action selection ($75.0\%$ invalid in
hard fresh cases at $\lambda{=}2$).

Our new insight concerns confidence. A confidence score that is reassuring
for current correctness becomes misleading for true semantic transitions:
within currently correct states, higher pre-change confidence predicts
\emph{lower} static error ($0.36 \to 0.02$) yet \emph{higher} update error
($0.53$--$0.68 \to 1.00$) across confidence quartiles, confirmed on fresh
parent-disjoint data in coordinate scenes.

Repair is not one-dimensional. Targeted flip coverage improves several
component and downstream metrics---static error $18.0\% \to 10.7\%$,
single-flip C1 miss $52.4\% \to 34.7\%$, action invalid $75.0\% \to 62.5\%$.
But paired quartet analysis shows endpoint improvement
($R_{\mathrm{endpoint}}$ $0.43$--$0.48$) is dominated by error migration
($0.36$--$0.40$): most AB endpoint gains do not restore the original atomic
competencies jointly with the composed outcome (full repair $0.03$--$0.10$).

\paragraph{Contributions.} (C1)~A controlled-change analysis of
static-correct update failures: transitions, atomic-conditioned
composition, and consequences. (C2)~Confidence--update reliability
inversion in coordinate controlled-change settings with fresh confirmation.
(C3)~Paired repair decomposition separating endpoint repair, full repair,
error migration, and joint consistency. (C4)~Cross-observation evidence
(football, pixels) and decision consequences, with stated boundaries.
