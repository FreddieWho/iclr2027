Static correctness is not the same as correct updating. A model can classify
every part of a scene correctly, respond strongly to change, and still fail to
update its decision when those parts jointly change what the scene means.
This paper documents that failure as a reproducible empirical fact, shows it
in three observation regimes, shows a diagnostic repair, and reports an
unusually wide set of failed alternatives that delimit what the defect is
(and is not).

We work in a controlled setting with semantic transitions: scenes have oracle
labels, edits have known atomic effects, and composed edits have known joint
effects. Three failure modes recur. (i)~Transition blindness: paths crossing a
semantic boundary induce no model transition---84\% among single-turn paths
discovered by random edits (a rare, mostly tangential subpopulation), 44\% on
directly targeted fresh paths. (ii)~Compositional blindness: when both atomic
edits are judged correctly, their joint change is still missed in 151/176
(85.8\%) of fresh-holdout cases. (iii)~Consequential blindness: the misses
propagate into events and action selection (75.0\% invalid in hard fresh
cases).

A diagnostic flip coverage---adding oracle-mined boundary-crossing variations
to training---significantly relieves all three: fresh C1 miss 52.4\%~$\to$~34.7\%,
action invalid 75.0\%~$\to$~62.5\%, static error improving 18.0\%~$\to$~10.7\%,
with same-direction turn, event, and action gains on real football tracking
data across three seeds, and repaired pixel-observation decisions on unseen
rendering styles. What failed matters as much: dense labels, slope
supervision, SDF readouts, distillation, relational architectures, quartet
curricula, and a rank-4 low-rank patch-map interchange (hybrid truth decodes
from concatenated hidden states yet cannot be installed into the frozen
classifier by the tested patch at that layer) all fail to remove the defect,
ruling out a family of ``the model just cannot see it'' explanations. The
defect is in updating, not in seeing.
