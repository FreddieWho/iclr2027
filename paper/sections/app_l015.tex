\section{Labeling symmetry as a training intervention}
\label{sec:l015}

The limitation in Sec.~\ref{sec:limits} is that published raw-coordinate rates move when the four endpoints are renamed, even though the oracle does not. This appendix asks whether that dependence can be trained out. It is a new matched-recipe comparison on the reused dev bank (237 quartets, 106 parents), not a reproduction of the four-arm table and not a fresh confirmation. Training uses the 512-scene source bank, 300 full-batch epochs, Adam at $10^{-2}$, clean BCE plus flip BCE, and four threads. Checkpoints are not selected on evaluation $J$. The eight relabelings are within-segment endpoint swaps and a swap of the two segments; the oracle label is unchanged on all 5688 evaluated states. The training script's own miner returned only two quartets; those values are discarded.

Permutation augmentation, which averages the same two loss terms over all eight relabelings, raises identity-labeling $J$ by $+0.144$ to $+0.270$ across ten seeds (mean $+0.195$, seed SD $0.048$). Every seed is positive and every parent-bootstrap interval excludes zero. Absolute $J$ swing does not fall ($0.063$--$0.156$ versus $0.046$--$0.089$ for the raw arm), and the fraction of quartets whose joint-correctness bit changes rises from about $0.15$ to about $0.29$. Augmentation is a gain, not invariance.

The handcrafted arm is an 8-d feature plus MLP, not a learned set network. Relative to sorted segment lengths and cross distances, it adds midpoint distance and an absolute direction cross (two sorted segment lengths, four sorted cross distances, midpoint distance, and the absolute direction cross; 6849 parameters, matching the raw MLP). Zero logit gap and zero $J$ swing are by construction, because the features are invariant under the group. They are not eight independent replications and not evidence that a learned set network acquired the symmetry. Identity-labeling $J$ is $0.540$--$0.595$. The paired gain over raw is $+0.435$ to $+0.532$ (mean $+0.472$, seed SD $0.033$); all ten parent-bootstrap intervals exclude zero, and the weakest lower bound is $+0.316$. Against indexed relational features the gain is same-signed in all ten seeds, but only seven intervals exclude zero, so it is not a settled improvement over that control. Invariance here is built into the features. The empirical content is that the constraint raises $J$ rather than buying invariance by collapsing the predictor.

A different arm, a learned segment-set with nonlinear pooling within each segment and then a sum over the two segments, does not learn the task under this recipe. This arm is not the 8-d feature plus MLP. Its segment-sum plus linear readout cannot represent a general crossing, so the negative does not exclude learnable relational ability. The witness is \texttt{artifacts/e832\_focus/structure/EXPRESSIVITY\_REPO\_AUDIT.json}. We do not claim that a repaired model succeeds on this bank. At 3297 parameters, $J$ is $0.004$--$0.021$ and training accuracy on three checked seeds is $0.71$--$0.73$. One wider setting with 7249 parameters, chosen only to match the raw MLP's parameter count, still has training accuracy $0.67$--$0.76$ and mean $J$ $0.019$. A four-point sum-pool, invariant under regroupings that change the label, is similarly near zero. Invariance alone is not the gain, and this recipe does not make the network discover an invariant feature that carries it. The near-zero $J$ is a restricted-class negative on a repeatedly analyzed historical bank, not a sealed confirmation and not a general exclusion.

A later comparison uses a newly generated parent bank and must not be subtracted from the 237-quartet numbers above. The denominator is 2188 official unordered $E$ pairs from 871 parents (\texttt{artifacts/e832\_focus/structure/bank\_quartets.npz}, sha256 \texttt{15f5bf18\ldots}). The same eight quantities, the same MLP, and the existing within-segment sort, under single-flip supervision, raise $J$ by $+0.061$ to $+0.092$ relative to the unsorted copy. All five parent intervals exclude zero. That is one specific invariant processing, not a proof that the two feature lists retain identical information, and not a learned set network. Replacing only the linear readout after the segment sum with a nonlinear readout does not recover the geometry gain: repaired $J$ stays about $0.017$--$0.046$, and the flip contrast against the additive control is at most about $+0.015$, with three of five intervals including zero. Six ordered distances reach about $0.40$--$0.42$ and do not solve the task. Of the 2188 pairs, 1745 have a composed margin below $0.02$. That slice was reported, not substituted as the denominator.

A second, independently generated source bank (1414 official E pairs from 389 parents) gives the same source-specific direction: rich-sorted exceeds rich-unsorted in all three seeds under both clean and single-flip supervision, with parent-bootstrap intervals excluding zero. The same comparison is uninformative on the expanded T1 bank (417 pairs/170 parents) and negative/unstable on T2 (912/361). Thus this is not cross-task confirmation. Moreover, a separate analytic segment-intersection parser solves the source E bank (230 pairs/65 parents), so the learned source comparison is a parser-bounded representation diagnostic rather than evidence that a complex relation model is required. The exact manifests and intervals are in \texttt{artifacts/e832\_focus/route1/round2\_analysis.json} and \texttt{route3/results.json}. No bank here is pooled with any other bank, and seeds are training replicates, not independent datasets.

A final contract-corrected visual screen uses a fresh 28-quartet/19-parent bank and the same ResNet18 input interface. The direct, additive, representation, and interaction arms obtain mean $J_3$ values .571, .417, .274, and .583 across three seeds. The interaction-minus-direct seed differences are $+.179$, $-.143$, and $.000$, so the interaction advantage is not stable. The historical low-exposure clean baseline has mean $J_3=.155$. A new three-seed baseline matching 323 training exposures per epoch and the singleton-dev selection set has mean $J_3=.202$ (128 unique clean images; the same 28-quartet bank). This is a bounded negative/structure result, not a visual interaction method claim; the corrected predictions and flow counts are in \texttt{route2/corrected\_results/}. The additive arm pools red/blue visible masked features through one shared linear head and sums them: an additive readout after a whole-image backbone, not object isolation; training and selection use singleton labels only. A follow-up run fixing only the mask-pooling contract (area instead of nearest, same bank/seeds) recovers the representation arm to mean $J_3=.619$ (additive $.464$, interaction $.476$, direct unchanged at $.571$); the interaction-minus-direct differences remain unstable ($-.250$, $-.036$, $.000$), so the bounded-negative verdict stands.

An implementation audit invalidated the frozen-versus-tuned comparison on the larger 692-quartet/351-parent visual bank. Both frontend branches disabled backbone gradients, while the nominally frozen branch updated BatchNorm buffers; model initialization also preceded seeding. Consequently, the historical frontend results cannot exclude capacity, prove the canonical interface unlearnable, or settle module transfer. The archived true-geometry frozen-head score ($J_3=.796$) remains a descriptive reference. A corrected rerun has been initiated, but its results await independent acceptance and are excluded from the scientific claims here. The implementation audit and deferred-run handoff are in the submission evidence directory.
