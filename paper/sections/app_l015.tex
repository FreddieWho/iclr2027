\section{Labeling symmetry as a training intervention}
\label{sec:l015}

The limitation in Sec.~\ref{sec:limits} is that published raw-coordinate rates move when the four endpoints are renamed, even though the oracle does not. This appendix asks whether that dependence can be trained out. It is a new matched-recipe comparison on the reused dev bank (237 quartets, 106 parents), not a reproduction of the four-arm table and not a fresh confirmation. Training uses the 512-scene source bank, 300 full-batch epochs, Adam at $10^{-2}$, clean BCE plus flip BCE, and four threads. Checkpoints are not selected on evaluation $J$. The eight relabelings are within-segment endpoint swaps and a swap of the two segments; the oracle label is unchanged on all 5688 evaluated states. The training script's own miner returned only two quartets; those values are discarded.

Permutation augmentation, which averages the same two loss terms over all eight relabelings, raises identity-labeling $J$ by $+0.144$ to $+0.270$ across ten seeds (mean $+0.195$, seed SD $0.048$). Every seed is positive and every parent-bootstrap interval excludes zero. Absolute $J$ swing does not fall ($0.063$--$0.156$ versus $0.046$--$0.089$ for the raw arm), and the fraction of quartets whose joint-correctness bit changes rises from about $0.15$ to about $0.29$. Augmentation is a gain, not invariance.

Features sorted to be invariant under the same group (two sorted segment lengths, four sorted cross distances, midpoint distance, and the absolute direction cross; 6849 parameters, matching the raw MLP) have zero logit gap under the group and zero $J$ swing. Identity-labeling $J$ is $0.540$--$0.595$. The paired gain over raw is $+0.435$ to $+0.532$ (mean $+0.472$, seed SD $0.033$); all ten parent-bootstrap intervals exclude zero, and the weakest lower bound is $+0.316$. Against indexed relational features the gain is same-signed in all ten seeds, but only seven intervals exclude zero, so it is not a settled improvement over that control. Invariance here is built into the features. The empirical content is that the constraint raises $J$ rather than buying invariance by collapsing the predictor.

A learned segment-set with the same invariance (nonlinear pooling within each segment, then a sum over the two segments) does not learn the task under this recipe. At 3297 parameters, $J$ is $0.004$--$0.021$ and training accuracy on three checked seeds is $0.71$--$0.73$. One wider setting with 7249 parameters, chosen only to match the raw MLP's parameter count, still has training accuracy $0.67$--$0.76$ and mean $J$ $0.019$. A four-point sum-pool, invariant under regroupings that change the label, is similarly near zero. Invariance alone is not the gain, and this recipe does not make the network discover an invariant feature that carries it.
