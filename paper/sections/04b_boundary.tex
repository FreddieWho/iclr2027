\section{External Observations and Decision Interfaces}
\label{sec:external}

This section reports behavior checks in other observation domains. Each
domain is labeled by how its changes arise: controlled edits constructed
on real coordinates (football E2), actual match events (football natural
turns), or controlled renders (pixel). None of these domains re-runs the
coordinate confidence analysis; their non-claims are stated separately.

\subsection{Real football tracking}
\label{sec:football}

The same failure-and-repair shapes recur on IDSSE real tracking data \citep{bassek2025idsse},
evaluated cross-field over three training seeds (s11/s23/s47). Two edit
types must be distinguished. \emph{Controlled crossings} (E2 protocol) are
single-turn zone crossings constructed on real coordinates with oracle
labels; \emph{natural turns} are actual match events.

\paragraph{Controlled crossings (E2).} Turn miss, frozen $\to$ covered:
s11 $0.277 \to 0.129$, s23 $0.383 \to 0.184$, s47 $0.336 \to 0.176$---
halved in all three seeds, with integral and accuracy metrics moving the
same way. Conditional event miss, frozen $\to$ covered: s11 $0.255 \to
0.092$, s23 $0.362 \to 0.180$, s47 $0.347 \to 0.168$. Hard action-invalid
rate ($\lambda{=}2$), frozen $\to$ covered: s11 $0.309 \to 0.102$, s23
$0.410 \to 0.184$, s47 $0.348 \to 0.141$; at $\lambda{=}0$ all three
models sit near zero.

\paragraph{Natural turns.} Natural-snapshot confidence analyses show floor
effects and, after correction, null inversion results. The confidence
finding of Sec.~\ref{sec:phenomenon} is therefore coordinate-only: it is
not reproduced on natural football turns, and no pixel-domain confidence
claim is made either way.

No pristine football holdout exists, so this is three-seed same-direction
confirmation, not single-read proof; the coordinate fresh holdout carries
the confirmatory weight and football carries the external-reality weight.
Football supports transition and repair behavior; it does not validate the
confidence inversion, the migration decomposition, or the relational-arm
claims.

\subsection{Pixel observations}
\label{sec:pixel}

The same quartet comparison rendered as $64\times64$ pixels---a CLEVR-style rendered diagnostic \citep{johnson2017clevr}---and judged by
a freshly trained CNN gives an emergent (joint-boundary) error of $0.537$
clean vs.\ $0.351$ pixflip ($-19$pp), with static error unchanged at
$0.090$ in both arms---while the quartet-curriculum arm scores
\emph{worse} than clean ($0.572$). Style transfer uses one same-style
replication control plus four altered styles (line width, color swap,
bright background, $0.9\times$ scale): pixflip has the lowest emergent
error on all five ($0.333/0.349/0.305/0.349/0.366$ vs.\ clean
$0.514/0.622/0.426/0.389/0.516$), and two additional training seeds
confirm the same-signed pattern ($5/5$ each; gains $-8$ to $-30$pp).
Absolute levels are style-sensitive---under color swap all arms' atomic
accuracy collapses ($0.25$--$0.31$; the CNN binds color to segment
identity)---but the relative gain is stable. This arm measures emergent
\emph{endpoint} improvement only: that small-CNN study did not measure full compositional consistency
($J$, full repair vs migration), and made no confidence claim. A later canonical-renderer ResNet-18 experiment measures joint repair on
159 quartets from 74 test parents. With equal total image exposure and
optimizer steps, static-to-flip training raises mean $J$ from $.539$ to
$.683$ with pretraining and $.465$ to $.669$ with random initialization;
all six seed-specific parent-bootstrap intervals for the changes are
positive. Full repair exceeds migration on every fixed baseline-110
subset, while some baseline-111 cases regress (App.~\ref{sec:f095}).
The training regimens differ in parent exposure distribution, so this is
not an isolated flip-content effect. Within identical auxiliary-arm image
exposure, observation-matched geometry targets improve dev geometry MSE
but do not consistently outperform ordered targets or BCE-only on $J$.
A color-segmentation/line-fitting analytic baseline reaches $J=114/159=.717$;
no neural superiority is claimed. Canonical rendering differs from the
historical D04 protocol, whose numbers remain separately reported.

\subsection{Decision interfaces}
\label{sec:interfaces}

How much of the repair benefit survives when the classifier feeds a
decision procedure rather than a naked threshold? On oracle-feasible
hard scenes ($93$ of $171$), the gain concentrates in refusal reduction
and coverage rather than precision:
refusal falls $0.43 \to 0.118$ ($3.6\times$ fewer refusals), non-refusal
coverage rises $0.57 \to 0.88$, with non-refusal precision $0.23$ versus
$0.22$ on the hard subset.
Two scale-related facts delimit what this analysis can exclude. First,
argmax-preserving temperature rescaling ($T>0$) changes no classification
decision; that action selections can shift substantially under such
rescaling shows the action rule is sensitive to the score scale, not that
the model perceives the input change. Second, dev-selected thresholds
coinciding with the lexicographic rule is an algebraic consequence of the
shared ranking at that operating point, not an empirical exclusion of all
thresholding schemes. Appendix~\ref{sec:failures} catalogs the failed
removal attempts---denser supervision, architecture and curriculum
variants, and an interchange patch---each reported with its falsification
bar and scope.
